## Brumadinho (Córrego do Feijão) — additions: criminal case status in 2026

- **Date:** collapse 25 Jan 2019; STJ ruling reported 7 Apr 2026; hearings from 2026 to 17 May 2027 (search summary).
- **Place:** federal criminal court (Justiça Federal, MG) and STJ; civil case in Munich.
- **Operator and ownership:** Vale (privatized 1997); TÜV SÜD (German certifier, private).
- **Deaths:** 270 (Mining Reporters); 272 (Agência Brasil). CUT cites 265 dead and 5 missing. Sources differ.
- **Affected:** about 1,400 victims from Brumadinho and Mário Campos are suing TÜV SÜD AG in Munich for about R$ 3.2 billion; hearings were set for 26-28 May 2026 (Agência Brasil, 26 Jan 2026). Outcome not found [UNVERIFIED].
- **Criminal case:** 16 defendants (former Vale staff and TÜV SÜD representatives), charged with qualified homicide and environmental crimes. In April 2026 the STJ Sixth Panel reversed a regional-court habeas corpus that had suspended the case against ex-CEO Fabio Schvartsman. A lower court had found in 2024 that evidence against him was insufficient. More than 160 witnesses are expected. No conviction yet, seven years after the collapse.
- **Further claim:** a search summary says the STJ rejected two more requests to stop the case on 16 June 2026, and that the case was split into three in 2023 (one for homicide, two for environmental crimes by Vale and TÜV SÜD). Not confirmed on an opened page [UNVERIFIED: search summary only]. It also says some reports count 15 individuals and others 17.
- **Counter-view:** TÜV SÜD denies liability and says a November 2018 official inspection found the structure sound (Agência Brasil).
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.miningreporters.com/noticia/news/2026/04/brazil-court-reinstates-charges-former-vale-ceo-brumadinho
  - https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/geral/noticia/2026-01/brumadinho-corte-alema-marca-audiencias-em-acao-contra-TUV-SUD-AG

## Mariana / Rio Doce (Fundão) — additions: 2026 conviction and health findings

- **Date:** collapse 5 Nov 2015; TRF-6 ruling 4 Sept 2026 (MPF page; one search summary said 3 Sept); ARSH health study sent to Renova 22 Mar 2019, reported 4 Nov 2019.
- **Place:** Mariana and Barra Longa, MG, then down the Rio Doce to Linhares, ES.
- **Operator and ownership:** Samarco, owned by Vale (privatized 1997) and BHP Billiton.
- **Deaths:** 19 (MPF).
- **Criminal outcome:** the 2nd Panel of TRF-6 unanimously overturned the first-instance acquittal in part (that acquittal was Nov 2024 in Ponte Nova, per search summary). Convicted of flooding with death and environmental crimes: Samarco and three former managers. Daviely Rodrigues Silva (geotechnics) 8 years 9 months, closed regime; Germano Silva Lopes 8 years 9 months, closed regime; Wagner Milagres Alves 7 years 3 months 14 days, semi-open. Samarco: criminal fine over R$ 6 million, R$ 1 million to the national environment fund, 10-year ban on public contracts. Acquittals kept for Vale, BHP Billiton Brasil, VOGBR and three individuals. MPF is assessing further appeals.
- **Health findings:** the ARSH study (Ambios Engenharia) found cadmium, nickel, zinc and copper in house dust in Mariana and Barra Longa above legal limits, and cadmium in soil and sediment up to 17 times the reference value. It rated areas "Perigo urgente para a Saúde Pública". Water and food showed no relevant metal levels. Renova had the study from March 2019 and the Minas Gerais health secretariat from 17 May 2019; residents were not told (Agência Pública). Separate ISS survey in Barra Longa (289 families, 576 people): 35% said health worsened, 40% reported respiratory problems, 15.8% skin problems (Greenpeace page; study date not given; self-reported).
- **Counter-view:** Renova said Nota Técnica 11/2017 barred publication without authority approval. Symptom surveys do not prove a metal link.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.mpf.mp.br/o-mpf/unidades/prr6/noticias/trf6-condena-samarco-e-tres-ex-gerentes-por-rompimento-de-barragem-em-mariana-mg
  - https://apublica.org/?p=60247
  - https://www.greenpeace.org/brasil/blog/da-lama-ao-po-o-impacto-da-tragedia-do-rio-doce-para-a-saude/

## Tapajós gold garimpo and mercury — additions: fines and legal action (2021-2025)

- **Date:** Greenpeace satellite data 2016-2021; desintrusion operation from 9 Nov 2024; MPF recommendation 16 Sept 2025.
- **Place:** Munduruku, Sai Cinza, Sawré Muybu and Sawré Bap'in Indigenous Lands, Tapajós, Pará.
- **Operator and ownership:** illegal garimpeiros, plus licensed garimpo permits (PLGs) held by cooperatives. No state or privatized company.
- **Deaths:** not quantified.
- **Affected:** Munduruku land has about 9,000 people in 61 villages (IHU). In Sawré Muybu, 49% of children and 57.9% of the general population had mercury above the WHO limit (MPF). A study of 109 Munduruku found 99% above the WHO blood-mercury threshold (Jennings study, cited by Greenpeace).
- **Environmental damage:** at least 632 km of rivers affected since 2016 (26.6 km up to 2016), compared by Greenpeace with 663 km hit by the Rio Doce disaster.
- **Fines and outcome:** the Nov 2024 operation in the Tapajós protected area next to Munduruku land brought ICMBio fines of R$ 20.27 million and a loss of R$ 6.39 million to illegal operations; seized 44 boats, 7 dredges and 209.1 g of mercury (IHU). MPF recommended annulment of 15 PLGs, 8 of them over 1,000 ha, and noted over 160 dredge alerts in June-Aug 2025. No evidence found that fines were collected [UNVERIFIED].
- **Counter-view:** none found from miners' cooperatives in the opened pages [UNVERIFIED].
- **Sources:**
  - https://ihu.unisinos.br/646517-operacao-de-desintrusao-impoe-prejuizo-milionario-a-garimpeiros-ilegais-na-terra-munduruku
  - https://www.mpf.mp.br/o-mpf/unidades/pr-pa/noticias/mpf-recomenda-anulacao-de-titulos-minerarios-no-pa-por-violacoes-socioambientais-e-a-decisoes-de-corte-internacional
  - https://www.greenpeace.org/brasil/blog/a-morte-dos-rios/

## Itabira, Sistema Pontal dams (Vale) and the Itabira complex (2020-2024)

- **Date:** removals debated 25 May 2021; MPMG case filed Apr 2022; 1st Civil Court of Itabira ruling reported 25 Sept 2024; separate court shutdown of the complex 6 June 2020.
- **Place:** Itabira, MG; neighbourhoods Bela Vista, Nova Vista, Jardim das Oliveiras, Praia.
- **Operator and ownership:** Vale (privatized 1997); Itabira is the original Vale mining site [UNVERIFIED: not stated in the opened pages].
- **Deaths:** none (no failure).
- **Affected:** at least four neighbourhoods below the structures (MPMG). Deputy Bernardo Mucida spoke of up to 300 families; Vale and the city gave no number (Money Times, 2021). A resident-committee claim of over 5,000 houses in high-risk areas appeared only in a search summary [UNVERIFIED]. I found no sourced figure on dust from the Cauê and Conceição mines; that part of the brief stays open [UNVERIFIED].
- **Environmental or social damage:** the decommissioning plan requires a large containment wall in an urban area; the ruling recognises damage from higher rupture risk and from the works.
- **Fines, outcome:** court ordered Vale to repair individual moral and property damage, collective damage and damage to the municipal health system, fund an independent technical body, and offer removed residents resettlement, a like property, or cash. No amounts given. June 2020: the labour court (TRT-MG) halted the Cauê, Conceição and Periquito mines after 188 covid-19 cases, with a R$ 500,000 daily fine for non-compliance; a later TRT item suggests the halt was suspended [UNVERIFIED].
- **Counter-view:** Vale says removals are considered for residents' safety and will be defined by studies with the least impact possible; Vale cited pellet-supply risk from the 2020 halt (Poder360).
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.mpmg.mp.br/portal/menu/comunicacao/noticias/em-acao-do-mpmg-justica-condena-a-vale-a-reparar-danos-morais-e-patrimoniais-causados-as-pessoas-atingidas-por-barragens-de-mineracao-do-sistema-pontal-em-itabira.shtml
  - https://www.moneytimes.com.br/vale-considera-remocao-de-familias-em-itabira-minas-gerais

## Córrego do Sítio II dam, Santa Bárbara, Minas Gerais (2021-2024)

- **Date:** leak May 2021; alert level 1 Oct 2022; cracks reported again May-June 2023; operations suspended 21 Aug 2023; false alarms continued to Oct 2024 (search summary).
- **Place:** Santa Bárbara, MG (city of about 31,000).
- **Operator and ownership:** AngloGold Ashanti, private foreign company.
- **Deaths:** none. No rupture occurred.
- **Affected:** about 650 workers dismissed on suspension (company cited negative results and rising costs, not the dam). Residents of the self-rescue zone, about 3,000 people [UNVERIFIED: search summary only]. Accidental siren soundings: five per MAM (Brasil de Fato Aug 2023), four in the June 2023 article.
- **Environmental damage:** a May 2021 leak reached Rio Conceição; AngloGold said it came from the plant, residents said from the dam (disputed; search summary only).
- **Fines and outcome:** none found. Dam rated level 1 after a crack 12 cm wide and 60 m long; Civil Defence reported a crack over 300 m long in 2023. ANM inspected on 23 and 25 May 2023. The dam is reported to be undergoing decharacterization.
- **Counter-view:** AngloGold calls the structure "segura e estável" and says cracks are monitored.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.brasildefato.com.br/2023/06/12/barragem-de-mineradora-com-trinca-de-300-metros-apavora-moradores-em-santa-barbara-mg
  - https://www.brasildefato.com.br/2023/08/28/anglogold-ashanti-demite-650-trabalhadores-ao-suspender-operacoes-em-mina-de-santa-barbara-mg/

## Caldas uranium (INB) — additions: tailings dam status 2023

- **Date:** report 18 Oct 2023.
- **Place:** Caldas, MG.
- **Operator and ownership:** INB, federal state company; extraction 1980-1995.
- **Deaths:** none.
- **Affected:** ANM estimates up to 500 people in a rupture.
- **Environmental damage:** about 2.5 million m3 of uranium tailings (0.70% grade). ANM rates rupture impact as "muito significativo agravado". Seepage seen on the downstream face in a 2023 ANM inspection.
- **Outcome:** dam at emergency level 1; independent auditors did not certify stability in early Oct 2023. MPF has followed it since 2019. It cannot be embargoed because the material has nowhere to go (CNEN). Responsibility moved from CNEN to ANM under Law 14.514 (Dec 2022). No decommissioning plan in the article.
- **Counter-view:** INB says level 1 means no imminent rupture risk.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.em.com.br/app/noticia/gerais/2023/10/18/interna_gerais,1578133/amp.html

## Pau Branco mine, Nova Lima: Dique Lisa overflow onto BR-040 (8 Jan 2022)

- **Date:** 8 Jan 2022; BR-040 reopened 10 Jan; fine notice 10-11 Jan; settlement signed 12 Dec 2022.
- **Place:** Nova Lima, MG, BR-040 at km 562.
- **Operator and ownership:** Vallourec Mineração, private (French group).
- **Deaths:** none reported. One person injured (O Liberal).
- **Affected:** BR-040 closed, 2 km traffic jams in each direction; firefighters said no evacuation was required (Poder360).
- **Environmental damage:** rain carried solid material from the Cachoeirinha pile into Dique Lisa, which overflowed with mud onto the highway. Volume not stated in the opened articles.
- **Fines and outcome:** Minas Gerais fined Vallourec R$ 288 million (maximum, doubled for recidivism after a 2020 fine). In Dec 2022 a settlement of R$ 73 million (R$ 48 million to environmental entities and projects, R$ 25 million to state agencies' projects) plus a state fine of R$ 80 million, totalling R$ 153 million. The R$ 288 million and R$ 80 million figures differ, and the opened pages do not say if the fine was cut or is a different instrument [UNVERIFIED]. Also required: monthly stability reports and an independent audit. A court suspended activity at the mine (search summary).
- **Counter-view:** Vallourec said the dike is a rainwater containment structure, not a tailings dam. Firefighters first reported a rupture, then retracted; advocacy group MAB still calls it a rupture.
- **Sources:**

  - https://www.suno.com.br/noticias/?p=220740

## Ouro Preto, Nova Lima and Barão de Cocais: Vale dam evacuations (2019-2021)

- **Date:** 20 Feb 2019 (Nova Lima and Ouro Preto); Nov 2020 to Apr 2021 (Barão de Cocais and Antônio Pereira).
- **Place:** Vargem Grande dam (Nova Lima); Forquilha I, II, III and Grupo (Ouro Preto); Doutor dam at Antônio Pereira (Ouro Preto); Norte/Laranjeiras (Barão de Cocais). Congonhas is not mentioned in the sources opened [UNVERIFIED].
- **Operator and ownership:** Vale (privatized 1997). The five Feb 2019 dams are upstream-raised and inactive (Vale).
- **Deaths:** none.
- **Affected:** Feb 2019: about 75 residents (Nova Lima 19 households, about 60 people; Ouro Preto 5 households, about 15). 2021: 30 houses in the expanded zone at Antônio Pereira; Norte/Laranjeiras and Doutor at level 2; Sul Superior at level 3. Over 400 people removed earlier for Sul Superior (Poder360).
- **Fines and outcome:** none found.
- **Counter-view:** Vale said geotechnical parameters had not changed and the 2019 removals were preventive to speed decommissioning.

## Gongo Soco pit slope, Barão de Cocais, Minas Gerais (2019-2020)

- **Date:** ANM closure 17 May 2019; predicted failure window 19-25 May 2019; blocks fell 31 May 2019 (search summary only); Vale statement 9 Mar 2020.
- **Place:** Gongo Soco mine, Barão de Cocais.
- **Operator and ownership:** Vale (privatized 1997).
- **Deaths:** none.
- **Affected:** 443 residents of the self-rescue zone had left home since 8 Feb 2019 (Defesa Civil MG, via Poder360).
- **Risk:** the north slope moved about 10 cm a year since 2012, then 5 cm a day from late April, 7 cm a day from 19 May. Sul Superior dam sits 1.5 km away; ANM feared vibration could affect it. The dam stood at level 3 (imminent) per Poder360 in 2021.
- **Fines and outcome:** none found. The failure did not occur on schedule; in March 2020 Vale said the slope was still sliding into the pit.
- **Counter-view:** Vale argued sliding would be inward, reducing impact on the dam.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.poder360.com.br/brasil/talude-de-mina-em-barao-dos-cocais-mg-pode-se-romper-ate-dia-25-diz-anm/
  - https://forbes.com.br/negocios/2020/03/vale-diz-que-talude-de-mina-gongo-soco-segue-deslizando/

## Minas-Rio ore pipeline spills, Anglo American (12 and 29 Mar 2018)

- **Date:** 12 and 29 Mar 2018; IBAMA fine 10 Apr 2018.
- **Place:** near Santo Antônio do Grama, MG; the line runs about 525 km from Conceição do Mato Dentro to São João da Barra, RJ.
- **Operator and ownership:** Anglo American, private foreign company. Project origin before Anglo [UNVERIFIED].
- **Deaths:** none.
- **Affected:** Santo Antônio do Grama, about 4,200 people, lost its water supply after the first spill; water trucks and an alternative source were arranged.
- **Environmental damage:** 474 t of ore pulp (first spill) and 647 t (second), per IHU. Another account gives 300 t and 174 t (search summary); sources differ.
- **Fines and outcome:** IBAMA R$ 72.6 million (R$ 40.1 million and R$ 32.5 million); Minas Gerais R$ 125.5 million for the first spill, second state fine pending then (IHU). Operations halted about 90 days. Final payment outcome not found [UNVERIFIED]. MPMG filed two civil suits (search summary).
- **Counter-view:** Anglo says the material is inert and non-hazardous under ABNT standards; estimated R$ 60 million for repair.
- **Sources:**
  - https://ihu.unisinos.br/577843-ibama-multa-a-mineradora-britania-anglo-american-em-r-72-6-milhoes-por-rompimentos-de-mineroduto

## Estrada de Ferro Carajás and the Carajás corridor (1985-2017)

- **Date:** railway since 1985; duplication for S11D (mine opened 2016); report 29 Nov 2017.
- **Place:** 972 km line, Pará and Maranhão, including Açailândia and Marabá.
- **Operator and ownership:** built under state-owned Vale; private after 1997.
- **Deaths:** 39 deaths from trains hitting people since 2010, record 7 in 2015, 2017 already equal (Agência Pública).
- **Affected:** the line crosses over 100 communities in 27 municipalities, including Indigenous lands and quilombola territories. 14 houses damaged in Auzilândia, 48 under evaluation; about 200 families in risk areas in Marabá (Civil Defence).
- **Legal:** Vale was behind more than 170 people being targeted in at least 57 civil and criminal actions from 2013 to Oct 2017.
- **Pollution:** FIDH says Vale sells ore to pig-iron smelters in Açailândia and ships the output, with over 20 years of pollution in Piquiá (search summary, not opened [UNVERIFIED]).
- **Counter-view:** Vale says it acts when its possession is threatened.
- **Sources:**
  - https://apublica.org/2017/11/processados-pela-vale/

## Lago Batata, Oriximiná, Pará: MRN bauxite tailings (1979-2024)

- **Date:** discharge from 1979; first dam 1989; restoration reported Dec 2024.
- **Place:** Lago Batata, Rio Trombetas, Oriximiná.
- **Operator and ownership:** Mineração Rio do Norte (MRN). 2023 statements name Rio Tinto, Vale and Norsk Hydro as shareholders (search summary [UNVERIFIED: not opened]). Ownership in 1979 [UNVERIFIED].
- **Deaths:** none recorded.
- **Affected:** residents report itching and allergies; Quilombo Boa Vista is about 500 m from the mine and 70% of residents rely on MRN jobs; over 90 families displaced from the reserved area (all Outras Palavras).
- **Environmental damage:** about 24 million t of solids on the lake bed within about 10 years (Outras Palavras); about 120 ha of igapó restored (Correio Braziliense, MRN-sourced).
- **Fines and outcome:** no legal outcome found in the opened pages. MRN admits a full return to the original state is impossible (Outras Palavras).
- **Counter-view:** MRN and UFRJ scientists call the restoration a model, with 99 publications; the Correio piece is company-sourced.

## Patterns

1. Vale (privatized 1997) is the operator or owner in most new cases (Itabira, Gongo Soco, the evacuations, Carajás) and in Mariana and Brumadinho through ownership.
2. Near-misses and fines outnumber convictions. One criminal conviction found (Mariana, 2026, nearly 11 years later); Brumadinho is still in hearings; the Herculano jury verdict was not found.
3. Fines are large on paper (R$ 288 million, R$ 125.5 million) but outcomes are lower or unknown (Pau Branco ended at R$ 153 million total; Minas-Rio final unknown).
4. Information was withheld: Renova and the state held the Mariana health study for months.
5. State-owned INB cases (Caetité, Caldas) show weak transparency, as private ones do.
6. Communities carry the cost of precaution: removals, false sirens, lost water supply.
7. Several figures conflict (Brumadinho and Herculano deaths, spill tonnes, Pau Branco fines) and both values are shown.

- Not found: Itabira dust from Cauê and Conceição; Caetité STF outcome; Herculano jury verdict; Munich TÜV SÜD hearing results; Minas-Rio final fine payment; Congonhas evacuations; current MRN ownership (verify).
