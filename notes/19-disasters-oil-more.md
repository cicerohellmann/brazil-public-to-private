## Mataripe refinery (ex-RLAM), hypochlorite gas leak, October 2023
- **Date:** 10 October 2023.
- **Place:** Refinaria de Mataripe, São Francisco do Conde, Bahia.
- **Operator and ownership:** Acelen (Mubadala fund, UAE). Former Petrobras refinery RLAM, **privatized December 2021** for US$ 1.65 billion; the oil-industry think tank Ineep says the asset was worth at least double (https://brasildefato.com.br/2023/10/18/refinaria-privatizada-na-bahia-tem-vazamento-de-gas-toxico-14-trabalhadores-vao-para-hospital/).
- **Deaths:** none reported.
- **Affected:** 14 workers hospitalized; 30 people inhaled the gas (same source).
- **Cause (disputed):** Sodium hypochlorite leaked while a truck was unloaded. The oil workers' federation FUP says cross-contamination with another chemical produced a highly toxic gas. Acelen says the situation was "rapidly contained", that all are well, and that it warned the union and CIPA at once; FUP says they were not told.
- **Environmental damage:** none documented.
- **Fines/cases:** none found [UNVERIFIED].
- **Counter-view:** Acelen's statement above. Other incidents (2025 unplanned stops, fish kill at São Paulo dam) are from one search summary only [UNVERIFIED].

## National blackout, 15 August 2023 (private wind/solar plants and a Chesf line)
- **Date:** 15 August 2023, supply interrupted at 08:31.
- **Place:** 25 states and the Federal District (grid split between North/Northeast and South/Southeast/Center-West).
- **Owner/operator:** The failing 500 kV line Quixadá–Fortaleza II (Ceará) belongs to Chesf, a subsidiary of Eletrobras. Eletrobras was **privatized in 2022** by a R$ 33.6 billion capitalization, the Union losing control but keeping a golden share (https://www.felsberg.com.br/eletrobras-fixa-valor-da-acao-em-r-42-e-divulga-detalhes-da-operacao/). The "root cause" per ONS was voltage-control equipment at **private** wind and solar plants. The system operator ONS is a private non-profit under ANEEL oversight.
- **Deaths:** none found [UNVERIFIED].
- **Affected:** 23,368 MW cut out of 67,507 MW (about 34.5% of load); about 30 million people. North/Northeast fully restored only at 14:49 (ONS draft report, https://eixos.com.br/falha-no-controle-de-tensao-de-usinas-eolicas-e-solares-causou-apagao-diz-ons/). Another outlet says about 16,000 MW (https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/nacional/do-raio-de-bauru-ao-caos-no-amapa-cinco-blecautes-graves-dos-ultimos-24-anos/). The sources disagree; ONS's own report is the more specific.
- **Environmental damage:** none.
- **Fines and cases:** On 11 December 2024 ANEEL fined ONS and 23 companies about R$ 132.4 million (headline of the same page says R$ 132.5 million) (https://canalsolar.com.br/aneel-aplica-multa-de-r-1325-mi-ao-ons-e-23-empresas-por-apagao/). By January 2025 there were 750 infraction notices to wind/solar agents and fines above R$ 150 million; in April 2025 ANEEL unanimously refused to suspend them (https://canalsolar.com.br/aneel-suspensao-multas-por-apagao-nacional/). In March 2026 ANEEL cut EDF's fine from about R$ 7.6 million to R$ 7.4 million and cancelled one plant's fine (https://tagdlaw.com.br/aneel-revisa-multa-aplicada-a-edf-por-apagao-de-2023/). A director asked for review of six appeals in August 2026 (https://cenarioenergia.com.br/2026/08/11/aneel-vista-willamy-frota-julgamento-multas-apagao-2023/ [snippet only, UNVERIFIED]). TCU opened its own inquiry (Acórdão 306/2024) [snippet only, UNVERIFIED].
- **Counter-view:** Abeeólica and Absolar say the ONS report has "technical gaps and generalizations" and ask for its review; they also say renewables face recurring ONS curtailment (losses above R$ 1.7 billion in 15 months) (canalsolar page above).

## Vale Fertilizantes, ammonium nitrate fire, Cubatão, January 2017
- **Date:** 5 January 2017 (fire out at 05:00 on 6 January).
- **Place:** Unit 2, Cubatão industrial complex (SP); Mantiqueira community on the border with Santos.
- **Operator:** Vale Fertilizantes, private company. [UNVERIFIED: how it inherited the plant from the former state-linked Ultrafértil/Fosfértil; not checked.]
- **Deaths:** none. One firefighter treated for intoxication; no employees hurt.
- **Affected:** 218 residents evacuated for about eight hours (https://www.abcdoabc.com.br/incendio-em-cubatao-chega-ao-fim-e-218-pessoas-voltam-para-casa/). A Cetesb release says more than 150 (https://semil.sp.gov.br/01/cetesb-multa-vale-fertilizantes-em-oito-milhoes-de-reais/); the later Agência Brasil figure is 218.
- **Cause:** Explosion on a conveyor belt feeding the ammonium nitrate warehouse, then a tank explosion. A union rep said a preliminary report showed abnormal belt heating; Vale said it had received no such alert. Cause still "under investigation" in the sources.
- **Environmental damage:** about 10,000 tonnes of product burned over more than five hours; Rio Mogi contaminated with ammonium-nitrate effluent (Semil page above). Cetesb's early analysis found changed ammoniacal nitrogen but no fish kill.
- **Fine:** Cetesb fined R$ 8 million and suspended the ammonium nitrate unit's licence until rebuilt and relicensed (Semil page). No criminal case found.

## Northern and Northeast blackout, 21 March 2018 (Belo Monte transmission)
- **Date:** 21 March 2018, 15:48.
- **Place:** North and Northeast most affected; 13 states lost power, only Acre, Roraima and Rondônia escaped among those regions (https://www.poder360.com.br/brasil/ons-falha-em-disjuntor-causou-apagao-no-norte-e-nordeste/).
- **Owner/operator:** Belo Monte Transmissora de Energia (BMTE), won in a 2014 auction: State Grid (China) 51%, Eletronorte 24.5%, Furnas 24.5% (https://socioambiental.org, Estadão report cited; BNDES also says State Grid 51%: https://www.bndes.gov.br/wps/portal/site/home/imprensa/noticias/conteudo/bndes-aprova-2-bi-para-belo-monte). One outlet gives State Grid 50% (https://www.nsenergybusiness.com/projects/belo-monte-rio-de-janeiro-uhvdc-transmission-project); the 51% figure has more sources. So: **private concession (2014), majority foreign state-owned capital, minority Eletrobras units.**
- **Deaths:** none found [UNVERIFIED].
- **Affected:** about 18,000 MW, 22.5% of national load (Poder360 above). About 70 million people (CNN Brasil, https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/nacional/do-raio-de-bauru-ao-caos-no-amapa-cinco-blecautes-graves-dos-ultimos-24-anos/); Poder360 gives no people figure.
- **Cause:** a bus-coupling circuit breaker failed at the Xingu substation. ONS's later report blamed human failure: BMTE had set a security limit on the breaker and did not tell ONS [search summary of ONS report: https://www.tribunapr.com.br/noticias/economia/apagao-de-marco-foi-causado-por-falha-humana-confirma-ons/amp/, UNVERIFIED because not opened].
- **Fines:** ANEEL fined BMTE (R$ 6.1 million upheld on appeal) and ONS (R$ 4.912 million cut to R$ 4.696 million); total about R$ 11 million [search summary of https://www.folhavitoria.com.br/economia/aneel-mantem-multa-da-transmissora-belo-monte-por-apagao-em-marco-de-2018/ — page returned 403, UNVERIFIED].
- **Counter-view:** ONS itself was fined, so the operator is not solely blamed. Energisa initially blamed "a technical problem" at the Belo Monte plant (https://www.moneytimes.com.br/ons-apagao-atingiu-225-do-sistema-interligado-nacional/).

## Itaipu-linked national blackout, 10 November 2009
- **Date:** night of 10 November 2009, 22:13, about three hours (https://enr.com/articles/3807-after-massive-nov-10-blackout-brazil-is-still-in-the-dark-over-its-true-cause).
- **Place:** 18 of 26 Brazilian states plus Brasília (ENR); Paraguay also lost about 90% of power (CNN Brasil above).
- **Owner/operator:** Itaipu Binacional (Brazil/Paraguay, public) and its transmission lines (Furnas, state-owned, Eletrobras). In 2009 this was **state-owned**; included for comparison with 2018 and 2023.
- **Deaths:** none found [UNVERIFIED].
- **Affected:** ENR: about 60 million. CNN Brasil: "almost 90 million" in Brazil and Paraguay. The sources disagree; probably different counting bases.
- **Cause (disputed):** ONS blamed bad weather (lightning and rain reducing insulators' effectiveness): three circuits of the 765 kV Itaberá–Ivaiporã line tripped, when the system tolerates two (search summary, https://www.senado.leg.br, UNVERIFIED). INPE said the lightning was not close or strong enough to damage the lines (ENR). Sabotage was ruled out by ONS (snippet only).
- **Fines/cases:** no fine found.

## Vicuña chemical tanker explosion, Paranaguá, November 2004
- **Date:** 15 November 2004.
- **Place:** Cattalini pier, flammable products terminal, Port of Paranaguá (PR).
- **Operator:** Chilean-flagged tanker Vicuña, owner Sociedad Naviera Ultragas (Chile); the terminal operator Cattalini was private. Ownership of the port itself not checked.
- **Deaths:** sources disagree: 4 dead (Gazeta do Povo, https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/vida-e-cidadania/a-maldicao-do-navio-vicuna-no-parana-ehrk7gmrxf5yc7l5drj8c0h8u); 2 dead and 2 missing (Cedre, https://cedre.fr/en/Resources/Spills/Spills/Vicuna).
- **Affected:** fishing in Paranaguá, Antonina and Guaraqueçaba fell about 60% (Gazeta do Povo). Cedre: 14,000 tonnes of methanol on board and about 400 tonnes of bunker oil spilled, the vessel broke in two. Gazeta says "millions of litres" of oil and methanol; the Cedre figure is the specific one.
- **Environmental damage:** mangroves and beaches oiled, fishing and aquaculture banned in the bay, dolphins, turtles and birds affected (Cedre).
- **Courts:** April 2005 Paranaguá courts awarded fishers R$ 13,000 and R$ 5,000 each; in late 2005 lawyers settled with the shipowner for under R$ 1,000 per fisher, often without telling them (Gazeta do Povo). The STJ (reported May 2009) held Cattalini liable for half of the material damages and R$ 5,000 moral damages to one fisherwoman; the shipowner had settled for R$ 713 (https://www.migalhas.com.br/quentes/84040/stj-pescadora-ganha-indenizacao-por-dano-resultante-de-explosao-de-navio-no-porto-de-paranagua).
- **Fines:** environmental fines by IAP and Ibama reported in search summaries; amounts not found [UNVERIFIED].

## Petrobras Tramandaí spill, Rio Grande do Sul, January 2000
- **Date:** 11 January 2000 (the court corrected the date from 11 March after Petrobras objected).
- **Place:** sea and beaches off Tramandaí (RS).
- **Operator:** Petrobras, state-controlled company [ownership structure in 2000 not checked in this file; see notes/04].
- **Deaths:** none.
- **Spill:** 18,000 litres of oil (https://www.migalhas.com.br/quentes/184265/petrobras-deve-pagar-mais-de-r-6-mi-por-vazamento-de-oleo-no-mar).
- **Environmental damage:** Ibama report: about ten metres of permanent preservation area polluted, health and quality of life harmed, beaches closed to public use.
- **Case:** federal prosecutors' civil action; Federal Environmental Court of Porto Alegre ordered more than R$ 6 million (R$ 3,045,229.64 for environmental damage plus the same for collective moral damages) and emergency plans within 90 days. Ruling reported 13 August 2013, 13 years later.
- **Note:** a separate 2012 monobuoy spill at Tramandaí exists (police inquiry); an infoescola table gives 1.2 million litres, but I could not confirm it (https://www.infoescola.com/ecologia/derramamentos-de-petroleo-no-brasil/) [UNVERIFIED].

## Osasco Plaza Shopping gas explosion, São Paulo state, June 1996
- **Date:** 11 June 1996.
- **Place:** food court, Osasco Plaza Shopping, Osasco (SP).
- **Operator:** private shopping company (B Sete Participações and Administradora do Osasco Plaza Shopping); gas supplied by Ultragaz (private).
- **Deaths and injured:** 42 dead, 472 injured (https://www.conjur.com.br/2006-out-23/tj-sp_nega_indenizacao_vitima_explosao_shopping//?print=1). Other reports give 300–372 injured (search summary, UNVERIFIED).
- **Cause:** gas leak in the buried distribution network, per the technical report cited in the Ultragaz case; shops reported a gas smell long before.
- **Courts:** June 1999 the TJ-SP ordered the owners and administrators to compensate victims (public civil action), upheld by the STJ. Criminal case: February 2005 the 6th Criminal Chamber of the TJ-SP acquitted the administrator, an engineer and three construction-firm engineers (some outlets say "convicted then acquitted"; the Conjur account is the one I opened). In 2006 the TJ-SP held that the shopping administrator, not Ultragaz, was responsible for inspection.
- **Counter-view:** the shopping says the fault was construction problems and gas-company inspection failures and sued Ultragaz; the court has sided with Ultragaz so far.

## REDUC LPG tank explosions, Duque de Caxias, March 1972
- **Date:** 30 March 1972, night (explosions around 00:50, 01:30 and 02:30).
- **Place:** Refinaria de Duque de Caxias (REDUC), Rio de Janeiro state.
- **Operator:** Petrobras, **state-owned**; under the military dictatorship (see notes/03). The page I read does not itself state ownership, it shows a Petrobras emblem [UNVERIFIED on that page].
- **Deaths:** 42 dead, 40 injured per military figures; residents and former workers say more than 70 (https://diariodorio.com/o-dia-em-que-a-reduc-explodiu/). Another source (search summary) gives 38 dead by 11 April 1972 [UNVERIFIED].
- **Affected:** the area was sealed by about 150 army soldiers and Duque de Caxias was declared a "National Security Area"; fragments thrown for kilometres, a highway closed.
- **Cause:** gas leak, three LPG tanks exploded. A worker climbing the sphere to open valves is credited with preventing a chain of explosions. The article links earlier accidents to rushed production and poor maintenance, but not directly to this one.
- **Environmental damage:** the article cites an NGO/Fiocruz study linking bladder cancer cases in Campos Elíseos to toxic smoke and contaminated soil and water (author's claim; not verified).
- **Fines/cases:** no investigation or prosecution found in the source.

## Patterns
- **Privatization changed who is named, not whether things fail.** Failures in Petrobras (1972, 1992, 2000, 2013) and in private firms (Vicuña 2004, Braskem 2011, Vale Fertilizantes 2017, Acelen 2023) look similar: poor maintenance, delayed or small penalties.
- **Grid incidents now hit private concessions and private generators** (BMTE 2018, wind/solar and Chesf-Eletrobras 2023), and the regulator fines the operator ONS as well. The firms contest the reports; the disputes outlive the outages (fines from a 2023 event still under appeal in 2026).
- **Fines are small next to the damage and slow.** Vale R$ 8 million; Anglo R$ 212 thousand; Tramandaí ruling 13 years after the spill; Osasco criminal case ended in acquittals 9 years later.
- **Victims often settle for little.** Vicuña fishers got under R$ 1,000 each in some deals.
- **Death counts are disputed and weakly recorded** (REDUC 1972: 42 vs 70+; Vicuña: 2 to 4), and many incidents have no root-cause finding in public sources.
