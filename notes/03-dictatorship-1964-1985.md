# Military dictatorship, 1964-1985: public power, land and assets to private interests

Research notes for the study. Governments: Castelo Branco (1964-67), Costa e Silva (1967-69), Médici (1969-74), Geisel (1974-79), Figueiredo (1979-85).

Confidence key: [A] read in the source itself; [B] taken from a search-result summary, not opened in full; [P] partisan or journalistic source, check before publishing.
Gaps and conflicts are stated inside each case. Nothing here is from memory.

## 1. Hanna / MBR iron ore and the 1967 Mining Code (Minas Gerais)

- **Years:** concessions cancelled 1962 (Goulart); MBR formed 1965; Mining Code 28 Feb 1967.
- **Government:** Goulart (cancellation), then Castelo Branco / Costa e Silva.
- **What was transferred:** iron-ore deposit rights in the Quadrilátero Ferrífero. In 1962 the Goulart government cancelled the registration of deposits held by Hanna's Novalimense and others (Águas Claras, Capão do Xavier, Pico do Itabirito and more) after a report that found them legally irregular. [A] https://periodicos.rc.biblioteca.unesp.br/index.php/estgeo/article/download/18706/13231/96226
- **Who got it:** Caemi (Augusto Antunes) started MBR in 1965 in partnership with M. A. Hanna Company. [A] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caemi . US Bureau of Mines (1968) lists Hanna at 34% of MBR's operating entity, with other holders. [B] https://search.library.wisc.edu/digital/AC7YH2GVPR5IDL8M/text/AFJS6L7ISYQNCM8F
- **Legal mechanism:** Decreto-Lei 227/1967 (Mining Code). The original text said prospecting authorisation and mining concession go "exclusively to Brazilians or companies" (text cut off in the excerpt, so the full rule is not confirmed). Foreign capital worked through Brazilian-registered firms such as MBR. [B] https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/declei/1960-1969/decreto-lei-227-28-fevereiro-1967-376017-publicacaooriginal-1-pe.html
- **Price / terms:** no price found for the 1960s rights. Later: Vale bought out Caemi in 2001-2006 (about US$3.2 bn total) and took 100% of MBR in 2007 (US$231 mn for 6.25% of EBM plus a 30-year lease of 13.75%: US$60.5 mn down plus US$48.1 mn a year). [A] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caemi
- **Who bore the cost:** the national mineral patrimony; the nationalisation attempt was reversed. Not quantified.
- **Links to the coup (contested):** the Unesp article argues US pressure over Minas iron was a factor in US backing for the 1964 coup, and lists Hanna-linked figures (Roberto Campos, Lucas Lopes, Bulhões Pedreira) in Goulart-era advisory bodies. This is an interpretive academic claim, not a court finding. [A] same Unesp link.
- **Gap:** I did not find a source stating exactly how or when the cancelled concessions were restored after 1964. Treat "restored by the dictatorship" as unconfirmed; the MBR/Hanna deal in 1965 is confirmed.
- **Sources:** links above.

## 2. Jari Project (Daniel K. Ludwig), Pará/Amapá

- **Years:** 1967 purchase; 1981-82 hand-over.
- **Government:** Castelo Branco to Figueiredo.
- **What was transferred:** an enormous private landholding in the Jari valley, plus state-backed finance, and in the end the debt.
- **Size (sources disagree):** "bought 1.6 million acres (6,475 km2) for $3 million" [A] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jari_project ; original holding about 9 million acres, about 4 million acres in 1982 [A] https://www.csmonitor.com/1982/0125/012532.html ; "16 mil km2" [A] https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projeto_Jari ; an opposition weekly says Ludwig sought legalisation of 2.4 million ha [P] (see Em Tempo below). Do not quote one number without noting the spread.
- **Who got it:** Ludwig bought in 1967 the firm Empresa de Comércio e Navegação Jari Ltda, which held the Amazon properties [B] https://jus.com.br/artigos/76098/o-maniqueismo-brasileiro . Brazilian law did not limit project area as long as Brazilians own it. [A] CSMonitor link.
- **Investment:** US$845 million by 1982, with "at least another $500 million" needed [A] CSMonitor; Portuguese Wikipedia: Ludwig declared US$863 mn, updated to US$1.15 bn in 1981 [A] pt.wikipedia Projeto Jari.
- **State role in the exit:** the government pushed businessmen to form the buyer consortium as a "patriotic duty" and began paying public services for Jari town. [A] CSMonitor. Per the opposition weekly Em Tempo (1982) [P, read from PDF text]: Ludwig refused in June 1981 a US$20 mn instalment on a US$200 mn foreign debt guaranteed by the BNDE; the government assumed US$180 mn "in exchange for shares without voting rights"; 23 Brazilian groups subscribed US$60 mn; Antunes bought Caulim exportadora for US$40 mn; BNDE lent Antunes US$35 mn to buy Ludwig's MBR and St John del Rey shares. https://siac.fpabramo.org.br/uploads/acervo/J_EM_TEMPO_1982_0143.pdf . English Wikipedia says Ludwig got no payment and the debt passed to the new owners. [A] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jari_project . These two accounts of who ended up with the debt differ; verify in Lúcio Flávio Pinto or BNDES records.
- **Who bore the cost:** treasury/BNDE guarantee (per Em Tempo), caboclo and extractivist communities (not sourced here).
- **After:** the group later went through further owners; the 2000 sale to Sergio Amoroso's group was reported at R$1 with US$415 mn of debt assumed [B] https://istoedinheiro.com.br/o-homem-de-jari (figure from search summary).
- **Gap:** no source yet for the exact BNDE exposure outcome; no price for the 1967 land beyond the US$3 mn Wikipedia figure.

## 3. SUDAM fiscal incentives (Operação Amazônia), 1966-1985

- **Years:** SUDAM created 1966 (Castelo Branco); Decreto-Lei 756/1969 on Amazon incentives (revoked 2001).
- **What was transferred:** the state waived part of income tax for firms investing in the Amazon, with up to 100% exemption for varying periods on income or import tax; firms could invest as shareholder in third-party projects (Art. 17) or via branches (Art. 18). [A] Rielo, ABPHE 2023, quoting Costa 2012: https://www.abphe.org.br/uploads/abphe2023/anais/joaoraiel.pdf
- **Size:** by end-1985 SUDAM had approved incentives equal to "US$ 3.928 milhões" for 959 firms, mostly agribusiness (figure as printed; Costa 2012 via Rielo) [A]. Conceição do Araguaia alone: 31 projects 1966-1988 covering 380,950 ha [A]. Large projects installed by the regime: 18 in the 1960s covering 238,030.6 ha and 10 in the 1970s covering 121,021.3 ha (Petit 2003 via Rielo) [A].
- **Who got it:** cattle and corporate ventures, many from the South and Southeast (VW, Ometto group, banks). Fraud: the paper quotes a study in which land book value equalled 92% of projects' own resources and many firms did not meet job targets. [A]
- **Legal mechanism:** tax-credit funds (the Rielo paper cites Arts. 17-18 of the incentive law); Decreto-Lei 756/1969. [B] https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/decreto-lei/Del0756.htm
- **Who bore the cost:** treasury (waived tax), forest (deforestation), peasants and posseiros (conversion of possession into private property in Conceição do Araguaia), workers (slave-like labour, see case 4).
- **After:** the paper reports incentive approvals fell by the 1990s and ended in fraud scandals (SUDAM closed 2001; not verified here).
- **Gap:** no consolidated hectares-total for the Amazon; US$ figure is as printed in the source and needs checking against Costa 2012.

## 4. Fazenda Vale do Rio Cristalino (Volkswagen), Santana do Araguaia, Pará

- **Years:** 1974-1986.
- **Government:** Médici-Geisel-Figueiredo.
- **What was transferred:** about 139,000 ha ranch run by VW's subsidiary Companhia Vale do Rio Cristalino; acquired 1974 "in a consortium with the military government", with SUDAM tax incentives and low-interest loans. [A] https://apublica.org/2025/01/volkswagen-manteve-por-12-anos-fazenda-com-trabalho-escravo-no-pa-financiada-pela-ditadura/ ; https://reporterbrasil.org.br/?p=98314
- **Size of public money:** prosecutors put public funds at about US$88.5 mn in today's values [A, Repórter Brasil]. No original SUDAM amount found.
- **Workers:** over 300 direct employees, up to 1,000 at once on clearing sites; the CPT estimates at least 1,000 victims. Labour-prosecutor case seeks R$165 mn. [A] Repórter Brasil. Sold in 1986 to the Matsubara group. [A] Pública.
- **Outcome:** news reports conflict on the sentence (R$165 mn collective damage vs four R$2 mn individual awards). Check the court ruling before use. [B] https://www.migalhas.com.br/quentes/450902/volkswagen-pagara-r-165-mi-por-escravidao-em-fazenda-durante-ditadura ; https://www.correiodamanha.com.br/colunistas/fernando-molica/2026/06/293366-trabalho-escravo-volks-condenada-por-fazenda-no-pa.html
- **Who bore the cost:** workers (forced labour allegations 1974-86), treasury, forest.

## 5. Fazenda Suiá-Missu and the Xavante of Marãiwatsédé, Mato Grosso

- **Years:** 1960s-1990s; Xavante removed 1966.
- **What was transferred:** Suiá-Missu estate (area given as about 800,000 ha; other sources 560,000, 695,000 or 1.7 million; sources differ) founded by Ariosto da Riva, sold in the 1970s to Italian group Liquifarm/Agip. [A] https://apublica.org/?p=25868 ; [B] varying figures via search.
- **Mechanism:** SUDAM incentives are cited by academic sources (e.g. "acquired through tax incentives backed by SUDAM") [B]; the Pública article itself does not mention SUDAM.
- **Cost:** about 300 Xavante flown out by FAB aircraft in 1966; a flu and measles epidemic then killed about 80 (about 30% of the group). [A] Pública.
- **After:** Agip announced donation of 165,000 ha at Eco-92; homologated 1998; Xavante effective possession only from 2015; about 80% of the territory later deforested. [A] Pública.
- **Gap:** CNV chapter not read directly (the official PDF failed on certificate). The CNV Vol. II, Texto 5, pp. 203-262 is the reference. [B] https://revistas.usp.br/rieb/en/article/view/201369

## 6. Federal-highway land strips (DL 1.164/1971) and Transamazônica colonization

- **Years:** DL 1.164 of 1 April 1971 (Médici); Transamazônica built 1970-74; colonization programs closed 1978; DL revoked 1987.
- **What was transferred:** the decree declared the public lands in a 100-km strip on each side of listed federal highways in the Amazon indispensable to security and development, i.e. federal property. Transamazônica (about 2,300 km, Estreito-Altamira-Itaituba-Humaitá) was listed. [B] https://planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/decreto-lei/Del1164.htm and https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/declei/1970-1979/decreto-lei-1164-1-abril-1971-375317-publicacaooriginal-1-pe.html
- **Colonization:** plan for about 100,000 families on 100-ha lots; Brasil de Fato reports no more than 8,000 families settled in the three Pará poles. [B] https://www.brasildefato.com.br/2017/11/14/transamazonica-45-anos-or-presenca-indigena-e-a-tabua-de-salvacao-da-floresta . Integrated Colonization Programs closed late 1978 after land-title conflicts. [B] same.
- **Link to private interests:** the federal land was then sold or titled to ranches and companies, see cases 3-5. This link is my inference from the sources; no single source here states the volume of strip land sold.
- **Indigenous cost:** a 2013 report says the road affected 29 Indigenous groups, 11 of them isolated. [B] https://www.ihu.unisinos.br/524054
- **After:** DL 2.375/1987 returned undisposed devolutas to the states. [B] Câmara link above.

## 7. Programa Grande Carajás: tax regime and pig-iron smelters

- **Years:** DL 1.813 (24 Nov 1980); DL 1.825 (22 Dec 1980); extension to 31 Dec 1990 by DL 2.152/1984.
- **Government:** Figueiredo.
- **What was transferred:** a special incentive regime for projects in the program area (about 900,000 km2, roughly a tenth of Brazil) [A] https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programa_Grande_Carajás . Ten-year income-tax exemption for firms setting up, expanding or modernising qualifying projects by 31 Dec 1985 [B] https://www.legisweb.com.br/legislacao/?id=63770 . Decreto 85.387/1980 created the interministerial council that approves projects; Decreto 86.417/1981 extended the Carajás legislation to the Albras-Alunorte area in Barcarena. [B] https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/508027/publicacao/15645057 ; https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/1980-1987/decreto-86417-1-outubro-1981-436169-publicacaooriginal-1-pe.html
- **Financing:** about US$600 mn from the European Community and an initial US$300 mn from the World Bank (per a 1986 CIMI document, as read by the search tool) [B] https://acervo.socioambiental.org/sites/default/files/documents/T2D00030.pdf . Not verified in full.
- **Who got it:** the incentives were aimed at firms processing the ore; dozens of pig-iron smelters later clustered along the Carajás railway (Açailândia, MA) using charcoal. [B] https://apublica.org/2012/11/ferro-gusa-valor-desagregado/ ; Fearnside, "The charcoal of Carajás" (1989) [B].
- **Cost:** concentration of income and large estates; mineral enclave for export [A] pt.wikipedia; forest loss to charcoal [B].
- **Context:** the ore itself was held by CVRD (state): US Steel left the AMZA joint venture in 1977 with a US$50 mn indemnity. [A] pt.wikipedia. So the state kept the ore but gave away the processing tax base.
- **Gap:** IPI/import-duty exemptions not confirmed; no total revenue-loss figure.

## 8. Tucuruí dam and subsidised power for Albras, Alunorte, Alumar

- **Years:** project 1974; Albras agreements 20 June 1978; works from 1976; plant online 1984; first 20-year contracts ended 2004.
- **Government:** Geisel-Figueiredo.
- **What was transferred:** subsidised electricity from a state dam to Japanese-owned (Albras/Alunorte with CVRD) and Alcoa/Billiton-owned (Alumar) aluminium smelters. [A] https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/3940/394034998010.pdf (Lúcio Flávio Pinto, Boletim Goeldi 2012; journalist-scholar [P-ish])
- **Cost sharing:** in Sept 1974 Albras was estimated at US$2.5 bn with about US$700 mn for the dam; by June 1975 Albras's share of the dam cost doubled to US$1.4 bn and the plant budget to US$3.5 bn; "the federal government decided to take over the whole cost of the plant". The state energy monopoly was bent to allow the Japanese up to a third of Eletronorte shares, but they never joined the dam works. [A] Pinto.
- **Tariff subsidy:** about US$2 bn over the first 20-year contract (to 2004), per Pinto, citing Eliezer Batista's remark on corruption [A, quoting an oral statement; treat as an estimate]. Albras's renewed contract: 20 years, US$3.4 bn, about US$11/MWh; the difference falls on non-favoured consumers, mainly households. [A] https://www.eumed.net/cursecon/ecolat/br/17/energia-electrica-para.html . A BNamericas item cites US$12/MWh (Albras) and US$22/MWh (Alumar) as subsidised (undated) [B] https://bnamericas.com/en/news/large-consumers-could-be-treated-as-distributors . Sources do not agree on exact tariffs; the original 1978 price was not found.
- **Share of power:** more than half of Tucuruí's energy goes to electro-intensive mineral industry; 64.3% of Pará's 2003 generation fed them. [A] eumed.
- **Who bore the cost:** treasury and Eletronorte, electricity consumers, riverside populations. Pinto: 1,100 islands formed, 6,500 people on them; the movement is still litigating compensation. [A]
- **Winners:** Albras/Alunorte (NAAC: 32 private firms 60% + Japanese government 40%; CVRD 51% in the original project) [A] https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albras ; contractor Camargo Corrêa (Pinto says Tucuruí "contributed decisively" to US$500 mn added to its owner's wealth; text partly cut) [A].
- **After:** Vale sold its Albras stake to Hydro (completed 2011). [A] pt.wikipedia.

## 9. Trombetas bauxite (Mineração Rio do Norte, MRN)

- **Years:** bauxite found 1967 (Alcan); MRN 1967/1979 (sources differ); Decreto 81.889 (1978).
- **What was transferred:** bauxite mining rights and a railway concession in Oriximiná/Porto Trombetas (Decreto 81.889/1978: right to build and operate the Trombetas railway). [A] https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineração_Rio_do_Norte
- **Who got it:** Vale and Alcan formed MRN; CBA 10% in 1973; Alcoa/Billiton entered later (1992 sale of Cruz Alta to MRN for US$17 mn). [A] same.
- **Subsidy:** none quantified. MRN's bauxite fed Alumar and the Barcarena smelters (case 8). [A] Pinto.
- **After:** present owners Glencore 45%, South32 33%, Rio Tinto 22%. [A] same.
- **Gap:** impacts on quilombola communities and tax terms were not found; flag for later research.

## 10. Pitinga tin mine (Paranapanema / Mineração Taboca) and the Waimiri-Atroari

- **Years:** BR-174 built 1972-79; Balbina works from 1979; Decree 85.898 (13 Apr 1981); Decree 86.630 (23 Nov 1981); mining from 1982.
- **What was transferred:** the 1981 decrees took 526,800 ha out of the Waimiri-Atroari reserve for tin mining and demoted it to a Temporary Restricted Area; Paranapanema (with Funai and DNPM) obtained this. Balbina flooded about 2,928.5 km2 of former Waimiri-Atroari land. [A] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waimiri_Atroari_Indigenous_Territory
- **Who got it:** Mineração Taboca (Paranapanema group), later sold to Minsur (Peru). [A] same.
- **Human cost (figures conflict):** population about 3,000 (1972, Funai), about 1,000 (1974), 374 (1988) [A same Wikipedia]; another source says about 332 in 1982-83 [B]. The CNV is reported to speak of 2,650 deaths, while a period source speaks of 1,300-1,500 deaths from the BR-174 [B] https://revistas.usp.br/rieb/en/article/view/201369 ; the MPF has accused the state of genocide. [B] https://justicadetransicao.mpf.mp.br/../../documentos-1/AIND_5_Waimiri.pdf . Verify in CNV Vol. II, Texto 5.
- **After:** reserve formally established 1989 at about 2.59 million ha; R$1.7 mn compensation for paving in 1996. [A] same.

## 11. Polonoroeste / BR-364 (Rondônia and Mato Grosso)

- **Years:** agreed 1980, started 1981, Bank suspended payments Feb 1985.
- **Government:** Figueiredo.
- **What:** US$1.6 bn program (budget figures vary: US$1.55 bn and US$1.1 bn also appear [B]) to pave about 1,500 km of BR-364 and resettle migrants, financed with World Bank. [A] https://www.culturalsurvival.org/node/4698
- **Cost:** in 1984 about 150,000 people migrated to Rondônia against INCRA lots for only 5,000 families; of 55 Indigenous areas only 4 were fully protected; of US$26 mn for the Indigenous component US$19 mn went unspent; Rondônia had the fastest deforestation in the Amazon. [A] same.
- **Link to private interests:** this is mostly a state project. The private-interest angle is land speculation and ranchers following the road (not documented in the sources I read). Include as context, weak fit.
- **After:** the Bank's first-ever halt over environment and Indigenous rights; the civilian government recognised two Nambiquara areas. [A] same.

## 12. Serra Pelada (Pará)

- **Years:** 1980-1992 (reopened by Collor 1991).
- **What:** gold deposit on land with a CVRD claim. Docegeo (CVRD) became the monopoly gold buyer in March 1980; Major Curió was the federal controller 1980-82; Docegeo left 1984, DNPM 1985. [A] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_Pelada
- **Volume (sources disagree):** official 44.5 t, black-market estimate 360 t (peak 1983, 17 t official). Other sources give 37-50 t or about 42-45 t. [A] Wikipedia; [B] search summary.
- **Fit:** weak. The state and CVRD managed an informal mass mine; private capital came later (Colossus Minerals, bankrupt 2013, investigated for embezzlement and gold smuggling). [A] Wikipedia. Included as a state-brokered extraction case where the miners' cooperative, not a firm, was the main private actor.
- **Cost:** about 80,000 people in the pit; 1987 massacre (9 confirmed dead, 79 missing). [A] same.

## 13. Aracruz Celulose and Tupinikim/Guarani land (Espírito Santo)

- **Years:** 1967-1983 land conflict; 1981 agreement.
- **What:** pulp company built its plantations on lands Tupinikim and Guarani Mbya say were theirs. [B] https://periodicos.ufes.br/agora/article/download/1891/1403/0
- **Terms:** in 1980 the Indigenous peoples self-demarcated and recovered 6,500 ha; in 1981 an accord between the military and the company cut this to 4,491 ha, in exchange for financial compensation. [B] https://www.wrm.org.uy/print/pdf/node/12778/pt (advocacy NGO; check).
- **State support:** WRM cites BNDES loans and federal incentives, no details. [B]
- **After:** 2005 retomada for 11,008 ha; TAC in 2006 transferring about 11,900 ha. [B] https://monitormercantil.com.br/?p=1501
- **Gap:** the reforestation tax-incentive law and amounts were not found.

## 14. Zona Franca de Manaus (DL 288/1967)

- **Years:** DL 288 of 28 Feb 1967 reformulated the 1957 free zone; Suframa created by Decreto 61.244/1967.
- **Government:** Castelo Branco.
- **What:** tax exemptions for industry in the Manaus Industrial Pole (IPI, import and other taxes). [B] https://www12.senado.leg.br/publicacoes/estudos-legislativos/tipos-de-estudos/textos-para-discussao/td-126-zona-franca-de-manaus-desafios-e-vulnerabilidades
- **Size (modern, not 1960s):** the Senate study estimates the 2011 total tax cost at R$22.4 bn (R$17.8 bn ZFM benefits plus R$1.4 bn and R$3.2 bn other parts). [A, from PDF text]
- **Who got it:** assembly plants of mostly foreign and national electronics/motorcycle brands (not detailed in my sources).
- **Dispute:** the Treasury says fiscal cost has been falling; defenders call it a constitutional alternative regime, not a waiver. [B] https://publicacoes.tesouro.gov.br/index.php/cadernos/article/download/149/163/663
- **Gap:** no figures from the 1967-85 period.

## 15. "Estatização" of external debt (1974-1985)

- **Years:** mid-1970s onward, accelerating after 1982.
- **What:** external debt concentrated in the hands of the public sector; part of the debt originally contracted by private firms was transferred to the Central Bank. [A] IPEA, Carlos Brandão Cavalcanti, "Transferência de recursos e substituição de dívida externa por dívida interna", Jan 1988: https://repositorio.ipea.gov.br/server/api/core/bitstreams/1faac9f7-872f-4cd8-842f-baae55950e70/content . Also [B] UFMG article describing "progressive concentration of the debt stock in the State's hands". https://revistas.face.ufmg.br/index.php/novaeconomia/article/download/2287/1232/7452
- **Mechanisms:** Circular 230/1974 and Resolução 432/1977 are Central Bank foreign-currency deposit rules, cited as instruments, but the sources do not say the 1983 measures were based on them. [B]
- **Link to cases:** Jari (case 2) and Tucuruí (case 8) are project-level instances of the same pattern.
- **Gap:** I did not extract the private-vs-public debt shares or a treasury cost. The IPEA text contains tables; one line in it says that substitution of external with internal debt explains at least 52.1% of something (context not read). Needs a proper read.

## 16. Cubatão industrial pole (São Paulo)

- **Years:** industrialisation from the 1950s; "Vale da Morte" label early 1980s; Vila Socó fire 24 Feb 1984.
- **What:** state-led industrial pole (Petrobras refinery 1955, Cosipa steel, petrochemical plants) where pollution costs fell on residents. [B] https://www.todamateria.com.br/desastres-ambientais-no-brasil/
- **Cost:** 37 stillbirths of 1,800 births, Oct 1981-Apr 1982, plus anencephaly reports (press, not an epidemiological study). Vila Socó: 93 dead is the most cited figure; one source says 11. [B] Sources conflict.
- **Fit:** weak for "public assets to private". It fits the pattern of private firms externalising environmental cost under a regime with no environmental law and no press freedom. Keep as a short note; not a transfer case.

## Patterns

1. **Public risk, private upside.** The state paid for infrastructure and tax breaks, firms took the profit: Tucuruí's cost taken over by the federal government so Albras/Alumar could get cheap power (case 8); SUDAM tax credits for ranches (3-5); Jari's debt taken on by the state when the project failed (2).
2. **Land was made private by legal fiat, then sold.** DL 1.164/1971 made roadside lands federal, SUDAM-backed estates followed, and possession turned into ownership (6, 3, 5). Indigenous peoples were removed or had reserves cut by decree to make room (5, 10, 13).
3. **Foreign capital, nationalist rhetoric.** Mining and bauxite opened to foreign or joint ventures (1, 8, 9) and Ludwig could hold millions of acres as long as the owner was Brazilian by the end (2). Japanese partners also took shares in a state-owned utility, bending the energy monopoly (8).
4. **Indigenous and rural peoples paid.** Waimiri-Atroari (10), Xavante (5), Tupinikim/Guarani (13), Nambiquara and Rondônia peoples (11), riverside Tocantins communities (8). Death and population numbers vary widely by source.
5. **Repeated tax-exemption model:** time-limited exemptions that were later extended (Carajás to 1990, ZFM to today).
6. **Losses socialised in the debt crisis** (15, 2).
