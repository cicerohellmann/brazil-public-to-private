# Colony and Empire, 1500-1889: public power, land and assets handed to private interests

Research notes. Every figure carries a source URL. "UNVERIFIED" = only seen in a search-engine summary or a low-authority page. Where sources disagree, both are given. Gaps are listed at the end. Most web pages were read through a summarizing fetch tool, so exact quotes should be re-checked before publication.

## 1. Brazilwood lease to Fernão de Loronha (1502 to c.1515)
- **Years / ruler:** 1502 onward; King Manuel I of Portugal. End date disputed: renewed 1505/1506 and again to 1512 per one account; Vogt (1967) argues possibly no renewal. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern%C3%A3o_de_Loronha ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/americas/article/fernao-de-loronha-and-the-rental-of-brazil-in-1502-a-new-chronology/BE81FFE8819E2B76FAD4E375DEC48DD0
- **Transferred:** exclusive commercial exploitation of the "Lands of Vera Cruz" (brazilwood trade) for three years, to a Lisbon merchant group. https://edisciplinas.usp.br/mod/resource/view.php?id=3192503
- **Who got it:** Loronha's merchant consortium (Lisbon, New Christian merchants per some accounts; not verified here).
- **Terms / price:** The charter is lost. Terms reconstructed from a letter of 3 Oct 1502 (Rondinelli): Crown share zero in year 1, one-sixth in year 2, one-quarter in year 3; he had to send six ships a year at his own cost, explore 300 leagues of new coast a year and build a fort. A different report (Ca' Masser, 1506/07) says a ten-year charter at 4,000 ducats a year; Vogt considers that letter wrong. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern%C3%A3o_de_Loronha . Reported 20,000 quintals harvested by 1506 and "400-500% profit": no citation in the source, UNVERIFIED.
- **Legal mechanism:** royal charter / lease (arrendamento) of a Crown monopoly.
- **Who bore the costs:** Indigenous peoples who cut and carried the wood by barter (not documented in the sources I fetched); the forest.
- **After:** On 14 Jan 1504 the king also granted Loronha the island of São João (Fernando de Noronha) as hereditary captaincy, making him the first donatary captain. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern%C3%A3o_de_Loronha
- **Sources:** URLs above; also https://periodicos.unifesp.br/index.php/alm/article/download/16546/13306/84698

## 2. Hereditary captaincies (capitanias hereditárias), 1534 to 1759/1770
- **Years / ruler:** created 1534-1536 under João III; remaining private captaincies absorbed by the Crown "between 1753 and 1770" (Wikipedia pt); Bahia taken back 1548-49. https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitanias_heredit%C3%A1rias
- **Transferred:** the coast of Brazil in strips, with jurisdiction. Sources disagree on counts: 14 captaincies (https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitanias_heredit%C3%A1rias), 15 shares to 12 beneficiaries (same page; also https://blogdoenem.com.br/?p=44536), 12 donatários (https://www.brasilparalelo.com.br/artigos/o-que-foram-as-capitanias-hereditarias). Size in leagues varies by grant (e.g. 60 leagues of coast from the São Francisco southward in one grant: https://periodicos.ufpe.br/revistas/revistaclio/article/download/24171/19613/48164 ; 80 leagues split between two captaincies for Pero Lopes de Sousa: https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/273/27353124008.pdf).
- **Who got it:** 12 donatários, mostly nobles and Crown servants (Duarte Coelho in Pernambuco per https://www.anpuh.org.br/uploads/anais-simposios/pdf/2019-01/1548772191_639ce402f46fba1f5160fa39ca38d48f.pdf).
- **Terms:** two documents, the Carta de Doação and the Foral (taxes, duties). Donatários got jurisdiction, rents and foros on mills and engenhos, part of the dízimos, power to appoint ouvidores, tabeliães, alcaides-mores, found vilas, and distribute sesmarias; the Crown kept brazilwood, dízimos and the quinto. https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitanias_heredit%C3%A1rias ; https://www.migalhas.com.br/depeso/419286/ano-de-1534-cartas-de-doacao-e-foral . Monopolies on mills, ovens and salt are mentioned for Madeira's captaincies, not confirmed for Brazil.
- **Legal mechanism:** royal letters of donation + foral; hereditary, indivisible.
- **Who bore the costs:** Indigenous peoples on the land (not documented in the pages fetched; needs a source), settlers under donatário jurisdiction.
- **After:** most failed; Crown bought or reincorporated the rest 1753-1770.
- **Sources:** as above; https://querobolsa.com.br/enem/historia-brasil/capitanias-hereditarias

## 3. Sesmarias (land grants), 1530s to 1822
- **Years / ruler:** first registered grant 10 Oct 1532 (Martim Afonso de Sousa to Pedro de Góis); donatários granted them from 1534; suspended by Resolution of 17 July 1822 (Prince Regent; credited to José Bonifácio). https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesmaria ; https://revistathemis.tjce.jus.br/THEMIS/article/download/917/77
- **Transferred:** Crown land, free, conditional on cultivation. Origin: Portuguese Lei das Sesmarias of 1375.
- **Size:** a royal letter of 9 Jan 1697 set 3 by 1 league per sesmaria, but in practice the Crown did not hold to it. Examples: 40 leagues in 1674 to men who fought Indigenous groups on the São Francisco; Casa da Torre requested grants of 40 and 60 leagues in Piauí. https://periodicos.ufam.edu.br/index.php/Canoa_do_Tempo/article/download/8002/6517 (via search summary; re-check).
- **Who got it:** settlers and elites; grants were also a reward for military service against Indigenous peoples (see above).
- **Terms:** cultivate within the stipulated time or lose it; dízimo to the Order of Christ. https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesmaria
- **Costs:** Indigenous peoples (conflicts and dispossession are referenced; direct expulsion figures not found); imprecise boundaries fed conflict with occupants.
- **After:** after 1822 occupation (posse) was the main way to get land until Lei 601 of 1850 (case 14). https://revistathemis.tjce.jus.br/THEMIS/article/download/917/77
- **Sources:** above.

## 4. Salt monopoly (estanco do sal), 1631 to 1801
- **Years / ruler:** 1631 to 1801 (alvará of 24 April 1801 ended it, together with the whale monopoly). https://revhistoria2.webhostusp.sti.usp.br/wp-content/uploads/revistas/004/A005N004.pdf (via search summary)
- **Transferred:** Crown monopoly on exporting salt from Portugal to Brazil, auctioned to a contratador.
- **Terms:** Azeredo Coutinho says the contractor paid the Fazenda Real 48 contos de réis a year (date of that figure not stated). Contractors needed a guarantor (fiador) in the 18th century. https://www2.historia.uff.br/tempo/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/artg8-2.pdf (search summary; verify)
- **Costs:** consumers; câmaras complained of contractors' neglect and excessive prices throughout the 17th century.
- **Reference work:** Myriam Ellis, O monopólio do sal no Estado do Brasil (1631-1801), 1955.
- **Sources:** as above.

## 5. Whaling monopoly (contrato das baleias), 1614 to 1801
- **Years / ruler:** first lease of whaling in the Bahia Recôncavo to Antônio da Costa in 1614 (citing Myriam Ellis); ended by the alvará of 24 April 1801. Another source dates monopolies 1747-1764 and 1765-1801 (different periodisation; unresolved). https://ojs.sites.ufsc.br/index.php/sceh/article/download/50/50 ; https://periodicos.ufpel.edu.br/index.php/CLIO/article/view/19128/13181
- **Transferred:** whales declared royal property (peixe-real); fishing rights leased by asiento/contract to contractors. https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/7010440.pdf
- **Costs:** enslaved labor: 525 enslaved people in the Santa Catarina armações in 1801, after the Crown had taken over management. https://ojs.sites.ufsc.br/index.php/sceh/article/download/50/50
- **Gap:** lease prices not found.

## 6. Gold in Minas Gerais: quinto, foundry houses, capitação, derrama (c.1700 to c.1790)
- **Nature:** here the Crown kept the royalty, but collection and the risk were pushed onto miners and local elites.
- **Terms:** quinto = 20% of gold; casas de fundição from 1719; capitação (a per-slave poll tax; 5$000 réis a year per enslaved miner is quoted) from 1 July 1735 to 1 Aug 1751; foundry houses returned 1751; derrama: if quinto fell short of 100 arrobas (about 1,500 kg) a year, the difference was charged to the population. https://periodicos.ufrn.br/mneme/article/download/16067/10845/51033 ; https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/3844/384446660010.pdf (via search summary; verify)
- **Scale:** peak output 18-20 tonnes a year c.1730-1755 (https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/2791/279149151002.pdf); 557 tonnes reached Lisbon 1720-1807, about 73% of estimated output 1721-1799 (same family of sources, working paper by Lima and Sousa: https://www.redalyc.org/journal/2791/279149151002/movil/). The gap is not explained in the sources.
- **Costs:** enslaved miners (taxed per head, and the labor itself); mining towns under derrama threat (the 1789 Inconfidência context).
- **Gap:** mining grants (datas minerais) and tax-farm contracts (contratos de entradas) not covered; need a source.

## 7. Diamond contracts and Royal Extraction, Distrito Diamantino (1740 to 1832)
- **Years / ruler:** contract system 1740-1771 (six contracts; some sources start 1734); João V and José I; Marquês de Pombal. Royal monopoly (Real Extração) by decree of 12 Aug 1771, until 1832 (Intendência abolished 1832; one source says the Real Extração ended in 1831). https://www.cartacapital.com.br/?p=78301 ; https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distrito_Diamantino ; https://periodicos.ufsc.br/index.php/mundosdotrabalho/article/download/95857/54764/374196
- **Transferred:** the right to mine and sell diamonds in a closed district, to contractors who paid the Crown.
- **Who got it:** the Fernandes de Oliveira and Caldeira Brant families stand out; João Fernandes de Oliveira held the sixth and last contract (1761/62-1771); Felisberto Caldeira Brant held the third (1749-1753) and ended in a diamond-theft prosecution. The Fernandes de Oliveira family took five of six contracts (one source). https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/chica-da-silva/diamond-contractors/A95742944C27FF40F481F1E02B370FCF ; https://www.anpuh.org.br/uploads/anais-simposios/pdf/2019-01/1547483138_1a45c86bae273fc7550dc2cde801515c.pdf
- **Terms:** sources disagree: payment proportional to sales revenue vs a yearly fifth. Exact annual sums not found. A Crown reserve of 241,405 carats valued over 2,200 contos is mentioned (Crown holding, not contractor output).
- **Mechanism:** contrato (auction/assent); Regimento Diamantino 1771 imposed licences for residence, trade and movement; unauthorised diamond possession was a grave crime (prison, confiscation, exile). https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distrito_Diamantino
- **Costs:** enslaved miners (legal mining only by slave gangs of the Intendência from 1772: https://www.cartacapital.com.br/?p=78301); residents under travel and trade bans; smuggling was the trigger for the 1771 change.
- **After:** the monopoly moved back to the Crown in 1771, i.e. the privatised contract was reversed by the state. Stands as a counter-example of renationalisation.

## 8. Companhia Geral do Grão-Pará e Maranhão, 1755 to 1778
- **Years / ruler:** founded 1755 (Pombal, José I); monopoly planned for 20 years; ended 1778 under Maria I (an Infopedia entry gives 1775; outlier). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%A3o_Par%C3%A1_and_Maranh%C3%A3o_Company ; https://www.infopedia.pt/artigos/$companhia-geral-do-grao-para-e-do-maranhao
- **Transferred:** monopoly on foreign trade of Grão-Pará and Maranhão and on the slave supply to those captaincies.
- **Who got it:** a joint-stock company of Portuguese merchants with Crown backing; capital about 1.2 million cruzados (about a third of its sister company's, per Wikipedia).
- **Costs:** 28,083 enslaved Africans transported 1755-1778 (Wikipedia; verify against Dias, USP Revista de História). Colonists complained of prices. Link to the end of Indigenous enslavement (1755 laws) is noted but not documented here.
- **After:** monopolies revoked and company shut by Maria I (viradeira); liquidation took decades. https://dl.uc.pt/bitstream/10316.2/46820/1/a_junta_liquidataria_dos_fundos.pdf

## 9. Companhia Geral de Pernambuco e Paraíba, 1759 to 1780
- **Years:** founded 1759 (13 Aug per Wikipedia, 30 July per another source); privilege suspended 1780; liquidation 1780-1813. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companhia_Geral_de_Pernambuco_e_Para%C3%ADba ; https://bdtd.ibict.br/vufind/Record/USP_a39bcf0ef82437d7c86fb3d9cfe8a670
- **Transferred:** trade monopoly in the northern captaincies; financed slave ransoms in Angola (Menz, Afro-Ásia). https://biblat.unam.mx/es/revista/afro-asia/articulo/os-negocios-globais-de-uma-companhia-colonial-a-companhia-geral-de-pernambuco-e-paraiba-e-os-negocios-da-china-1759-1783
- **Mechanism:** merchant company with shareholders and direct Crown financial and managerial involvement.
- **Gap:** capital and slave totals not obtained.

## 10. First Banco do Brasil, 1808 to 1829
- **Years / ruler:** charter 12 Oct 1808 (Prince Regent João); liquidation ordered by law of 23 Sept 1829. https://scielo.br/j/rbh/a/JJgTM6Q3Fkv7dGB8dtTr7Vj/?lang=en
- **Transferred:** privileges including handling Royal Treasury remittances and selling state-monopoly goods (diamonds, brazilwood, ivory, urzela) for a 2% commission on net proceeds (tobacco not confirmed). https://revista.enap.gov.br/index.php/RSP/article/download/7520/4637/23919 (search summary; verify)
- **Costs:** public finances: the bank was accused of worsening the country's financial position; no bank until 1838. 
- **Gap:** capital, shareholder list and Crown debt not obtained (see Cardoso 2010, Revista Brasileira de História).

## 11. Opening of the ports and the 1810 Treaty of Commerce with Britain
- **Years / ruler:** ports opened 1808; treaty signed Rio de Janeiro 19 Feb 1810; lasted to 1844 (original expiry 1825; renewed). Regent João. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangford_Treaty ; https://www.infopedia.pt/artigos/$tratado-de-comercio-luso-britanico-de-1810
- **Transferred:** a tariff privilege: British goods paid 15% on entry vs 25% for other nations (Wikipedia; secondary). Article 15 of the treaty set 15% for British goods; in practice monetary instability and valuation disputes often raised the real rate above 15% in the 1820s. https://revistas.usp.br/ee/en/article/view/172019
- **Legal privilege:** a "juiz conservador da Nação Inglesa" (decree of 4 May 1808, consolidated in the 1810 treaty) tried cases of British subjects; the office ended only with the 1827 treaty's expiry in 1844. https://revista.enap.gov.br/index.php/RSP/article/download/5650/3078/18364
- **Slave trade:** Portugal agreed to restrict and consider ending the trade (Wikipedia; see also https://www.bhsportugal.org/library/articles/the-treaties-of-1810-and-the-gradual-abolition-of-slave-traffic-in-brazil).
- **Costs:** local handicraft and the treasury (low tariff revenue); Wikipedia claims Brazilian tobacco and sugar were barred from British markets: check against a specialist source.
- **Gap:** treaty text for exact articles; tariff-revenue loss figures.

## 12. St John d'el Rey Mining Company (Morro Velho), 1830 to 1960/1985
- **Years / ruler:** company formed in London April 1830 (joint stock); bought Morro Velho in 1834; first dividends 1842; sold 1960 to the Moreira Sales group because of Brazilian limits on foreign control (Diário do Comércio); formally closed 1985 (Wikipedia). Pedro I and the Regency. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_John_d%27El_Rey_Mining_Company ; https://diariodocomercio.com.br/especial/dos-ingleses-ate-a-anglogold-ashanti/
- **Transferred:** an existing gold mine (about 50 years of earlier working), bought privately; price not given in the source I read.
- **Who got it:** British joint-stock investors and Cornish managers (about 350 Cornish miners in the 1860s).
- **Costs:** enslaved labor. 1845 agreement renting 385 enslaved people from the Cata Branca company for 14 years, with a promise of emancipation at the end; the clause was later forgotten (Wikipedia). 1879 report: "fully two hundred blacks in Slavery." Eakin calls it the largest industrial slave complex in Brazil (Diário do Comércio).
- **Source work:** Marshall Eakin, British Enterprise in Brazil (Duke UP, 1989). https://www.dukeupress.edu/a-british-enterprise-in-brazil
- **Note:** this is a private purchase, not a state grant; it matters as the template of foreign mining under the 1810-era legal climate.

## 13. Lei de Terras (Lei 601), 1850
- **Years / ruler:** 18 Sept 1850; Pedro II. Regulated by Decreto 1.318 of 30 Jan 1854. https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/leimp/1824-1899/lei-601-18-setembro-1850-559842-publicacaooriginal-82254-pl.html (not read directly; page blocked) ; https://planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l0601-1850.htm
- **Transferred:** the terras devolutas (public lands without valid title). After 17 July 1822 there was no legal grant, so occupation by posse was the only route for 28 years. Law 601 banned acquisition of public land other than by purchase (art. 1; exception: border zone of ten leagues), defined devolutas, validated consolidated posses and sesmarias with effective cultivation, and authorised sale at auction or otherwise (art. 14). https://www.revista.ueg.br/index.php/revistahistoria/article/download/13896/9822/53088 ; https://www.legjur.com/legislacao/art/lei_00006011850-14
- **Who got it:** existing landholders (squatters and sesmaria holders legitimised); buyers with capital.
- **Costs:** enslaved and later freed people, free poor, Indigenous peoples (access to land closed by purchase requirement). Some authors argue the law was meant to organise property, not widen access. https://revistas.usp.br/revhistoria/article/download/133157/129254/254809
- **Gap:** national land concentration figures; the price schedule; Indigenous reserve provision (art. 12) and immigrant-funding clauses were not read in the text.

## 14. Amazon steam navigation privilege to Mauá, 1852
- **Years / ruler:** Decreto 1.037 of 30 Aug 1852; Pedro II. Operations began 1853 (Companhia de Navegação e Comércio do Amazonas, 1852-1871). https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/1824-1899/decreto-1037-30-agosto-1852-559381-publicacaooriginal-81578-pe.html ; https://tedehomologacao.ufam.edu.br/items/1ca687d6-5cfc-4dff-9148-4ed303f563a0
- **Transferred (from the decree):** exclusive privilege for 30 years; subsidy of 160 contos de réis a year for the first 15 years on the Belém-Rio Negro line; a second line to Nauta (Peru) with a subsidy of at least 40 contos a year, guaranteed by the Empire and paid by Peru; free land for 60 colonies of foreigners or Indigenous people on the riverbanks, each sized for 3,000 inhabitants; free state land for a dock in Belém. Minimum capital 1,200 contos.
- **Who got it:** Irineu Evangelista de Sousa (Mauá).
- **Obligations:** fixed trip schedules, fines for missed trips, minimum speed of 8 miles an hour upriver, free mail and four state passengers per trip.
- **Costs:** treasury (subsidy); riverine and Indigenous lands (colony grants). Context: the state wanted to counter foreign pressure for free navigation of the Amazon (UFAM thesis above).
- **After:** the company lasted to 1871 (sources: UFAM thesis). Later fate (sale to a British company in 1872/1873) not verified here.

## 15. Railways: interest guarantee law of 1852 and the São Paulo Railway (1860)
- **Years / ruler:** Law 641 of 26 June 1852; later changes 1874, 1878, 1890; Pedro II. https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/decreto/historicos/dpl/dpl641-1852.htm (listed, text not read); change dates from https://hehe.org.br/index.php/rabphe/article/view/179/139
- **Mechanism:** the state guaranteed a minimum return (5% typical) on capital spent; four types of concession between 1852 and 1880 with different terms, guarantees and zone privileges (same HEHE source). A British engineer-promoter pushed the guarantee idea (source: Duncan/Cambridge chapter listed at https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.7312/dunc92472/html, not read in full).
- **São Paulo Railway (Santos-Jundiaí), Decreto 2.601 of 6 June 1860:** the Imperial Government guaranteed 5% a year, paid half-yearly, on capital spent on the first twenty leagues, with a ceiling of £2,000,000 (at 27 pence per milréis); the Province of São Paulo added 2%; the guarantee was later extended to the whole line and concession; privilege for 90 years; profits above 8% shared with the Government, dividend capped at 12%; duty-free import of materials; Government could redeem the privileges 30 years after full opening. https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/1824-1899/decreto-2601-6-junho-1860-556490-publicacaooriginal-76537-pe.html
- **Other lines:** 7% guarantee on capital spent for the Brazilian Imperial Central Bahia Railway (Decreto 6.637, 1877): https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/1824-1899/decreto-6637-31-julho-1877-549156-publicacaooriginal-64510-pe.html ; 5% on additional capital for the Pernambuco railway (Decreto 1767 of 9 July 1870): https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/1824-1899/decreto-1767-9-julho-1870-552491-publicacaooriginal-69797-pl.html
- **Recife and São Francisco Railway:** 1852-1901, opened 1858, bought by Great Western of Brazil in 1901. Guarantee terms not confirmed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrada_Ferro_Recife_ao_S%C3%A3o_Francisco
- **Who bore the costs:** treasury (contingent liability; total cost not found), freight payers. Scholarship: Summerhill, Order Against Progress (Stanford, 2003) and "Market intervention in a backward economy" (Economic History Review, 1998). https://muse.jhu.edu/article/174958
- **Gap:** total guarantee payments; foreign share of rail capital; Dom Pedro II railway terms.

## 18. Port of Santos concession, 1888 (late Empire)
- **Years / ruler:** Decreto 9.979 of 12 July 1888; Pedro II / Princess Regent. Some sources date the formal contract to 1890 and the Companhia Docas de Santos to 1890. https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/417971/publicacao/15778887 ; https://atom.cmu.unicamp.br/uploads/r/centro-de-memoria-unicamp/1/0/a/10ab05c2ff8ca094dc9281b0a5364c04f4c86a3594c1389cf0e310dcaca844cc/70469b1e-5263-4bc5-a95d-8bde3bd72130-CMP_2.1.8.172.pdf
- **Transferred:** build and operate the quay, with usufruct of expropriated land and of reclaimed fill; 39 years from the decree (clause II), later extended to 90 years (maximum under Law 1.746 of 1869, per https://mlawreview.emnuvens.com.br/mlaw/article/download/54/139).
- **Who got it:** José Pinto de Oliveira, C. Gaffrée, Eduardo P. Guinle and others (Gaffrée, Guinle & Cia., later Companhia Docas de Santos).
- **Terms:** fees fixed in the decree: 1 réis per kg handled, 3 réis per kg for bulky goods, 700 réis per day per metre of quay for steamers, 500 for sailing vessels, 2 réis per kg per month stored; construction materials exempt only where the tariff allowed; reversion to the State without compensation at term; no explicit exclusivity but preference for similar works; no interest guarantee in the text.
- **Mechanism:** concession under Law 1.746 of 1869 after public tender.
- **Costs:** port users (fees); coastal land.

## Patterns
1. **Monopoly or privilege leased for a fee, then renewed or defended by the holder.** Brazilwood, salt, whales, diamonds, trading companies: the Crown sells exclusivity for revenue and risk transfer. It ends when smuggling, price complaints or a change of ruler (Pombal's fall, 1771 diamonds, 1801 salt/whale) break it. Renationalisation happens (diamonds 1771, captaincies 1753-70, Banco do Brasil 1829), often because the holder failed or the Crown wanted the revenue back.
2. **Guaranteed returns shift risk to the public.** The 1852 railway law (5% in most cases, 7% in some) with provincial top-ups (+2% in São Paulo), duty-free materials, ninety-year terms, and profit caps/sharing. Amazon steam: a 30-year privilege with 160 contos a year. Investors are paid whether or not the line earns.
3. **The unfree pay the cost.** Mining (diamonds 1772-1832, Morro Velho), whaling armações, the Companhia's slave supply (28,083 people transported 1755-1778), and the capitação taxed per enslaved miner. Indigenous peoples and the free poor lose access to land through sesmarias, captaincies, colony grants on the Amazon and the 1850 purchase-only rule.
4. **Land follows the incumbent.** Grants were free and conditional (sesmarias), then possession was legalised after the fact (Lei 601), then buying became the only route; each step confirmed the large holders.
5. **Foreign capital arrives on legal advantage.** The 15% tariff and the juiz conservador (1808-1844) gave British merchants better terms than locals; later, London-incorporated companies took railways, gas, sewage, telegraph and a port, often after Brazilian founders (Mauá) ran out of capital.
6. **Terms are lost or fuzzy in the record.** For many cases (brazilwood charter, diamond contract prices) the original terms do not survive or sources conflict.
