## 1. Scale: the Livro Branco da Grilagem, INCRA 1999/2001 and the parliamentary inquiries

- Years: 1970 (Câmara CPI), 1999 (Livro Branco), 2001 (INCRA update; Câmara CPI on Amazon public land).
- Government/ruler: Médici-era Câmara CPI (1970); FHC government (1999-2001).
- What / how big: 1970 CPI: Pará had the most substantiated grilagem complaints (17 cases) and the largest area sold illegally, 8 million ha. Livro Branco (INCRA 1999): about 100 million ha nationwide under suspicion. INCRA 2001 update: 93 million ha nationwide, of which Amazonas 55 million ha and Pará 31 million ha. Source for all four: IMAZON-CART, https://imazon.t3.storage.dev/combate-a-grilagem-de-terras-em-cartorios-no-para-uma-decada-de-avancos-e-desafios-a28bb932.pdf (it cites Câmara dos Deputados 1970, INCRA 1999, INCRA 2001; I did not open those primary documents, so the figures are second-hand [UNVERIFIED at primary level]).
- Who got it (and financed): large claimants holding forged or inflated titles; INCRA's re-registration of holdings above 10,000 ha cancelled cadastros covering 20 million ha of irregular holdings (Barreto et al. 2008, cited in IMAZON-CART, same URL).
- Price/terms vs value: not applicable (appropriation, no price). The public estate was taken for free.
- Legal mechanism: forged titles, inflated areas, registry collusion (see case 2), later "regularization" of the occupations.
- Who bore the costs: the Union and the states (loss of the land), posseiros, Indigenous and traditional peoples (violence; IMAZON-CART notes the violence against posseiros and Indigenous peoples).
- After: INCRA recadastramento, Provimento 13/2006, CNJ 2009-2010 cancellations (case 2). The 2001 CPI report PDF is at https://apublica.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/33421741-relatorio-final-cpi-terras-amazonas-grilagem.pdf (found in search; not read in full here). A search summary says the CPI concentrated on property registries and cites "more than 50 million ha" improperly appropriated; I did not confirm that on the page [UNVERIFIED].
- Counter-view: "area under suspicion" is an estimate, not an adjudicated finding; the 100 million and 93 million figures are not additive with the Pará registry totals (different bases and years). Both numbers are shown as a range: 93-100 million ha (IMAZON-CART citing INCRA 2001 and 1999).
- Sources: https://imazon.t3.storage.dev/combate-a-grilagem-de-terras-em-cartorios-no-para-uma-decada-de-avancos-e-desafios-a28bb932.pdf ; https://apublica.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/33421741-relatorio-final-cpi-terras-amazonas-grilagem.pdf

## 2. Pará registry with more land than the state, and the CNJ cancellations

- Years: 2006 (Provimento 13/2006 blocks), 2009 (CNJ cancels the 410-million-ha matrícula), 2010 (administrative cancellation drive), reviewed in IMAZON-CART.
- Government/ruler: Lula government; Judiciary (CNJ, TJ-PA Corregedoria, with Iterpa and INCRA).
- What / how big: CNJ-driven cancellations hit at least 10,728 matrículas in 88 municípios covering 91.1 million ha (91,124,142 ha) in Pará, (a large share of a state of roughly 125 million ha [state area not sourced here]); São Félix do Xingu and Altamira together hold 45.6 million ha (50%) of the cancelled area; São Félix do Xingu alone: 3,167 properties in 32.8 million ha. Separately, one matrícula of 410 million ha in the Vitória do Xingu registry was cancelled in 2009, "3.9 times the size of Pará" (that single property is excluded from the 91.1 million total). Source: IMAZON-CART, https://imazon.t3.storage.dev/combate-a-grilagem-de-terras-em-cartorios-no-para-uma-decada-de-avancos-e-desafios-a28bb932.pdf
- Context numbers: the Amazonas Corregedoria cancelled registries totalling 48.4 million ha, validated by CNJ in 2007 (IMAZON-CART citing Lima 2002, same URL). The same study says Pará's private holdings are 10.5% of the state (12.8 million ha), by other data (same URL), which suggests the registries far exceeded the real private estate (the 410-million-ha matrícula alone was larger than the whole state).
- Who got it (and financed): claimants through "fantasma" owners and fake inventories; the people behind most chains are not identified in the opened sources.
- Price/terms vs value: no price; purchases were mostly registry entries. Where later sold, buyers were logging and cattle interests (see case 3).
- Legal mechanism: duplicated or inflated matrículas, forged adjudication letters, notaries' collusion; Lei 6.739/1979 allowed administrative cancellation of null registrations; Provimento 13/2006 blocked matrículas by size and date criteria; TJ-PA initially refused to cancel (it ordered only blocks), so the commission went to CNJ (IMAZON-CART, same URL).
- Who bore the costs: the state and the Union (land lost, years of litigation); innocent third-party buyers when matrículas were cancelled; judicial and registry staff time.
- After: Imazon found that some of the blocked or cancelled holdings re-entered INCRA's Sigef (1.5 million ha of areas of previous cancelled matrículas identified in Sigef; and a set of 332 imóveis of 2.5 million ha with 34% of the original forest, 811 thousand ha, already deforested) (IMAZON-CART, same URL). 89% of lawsuits started before the CNJ decision (IMAZON-CART, same URL).
- Counter-view: the cancellations were administrative and based on size/date proxies, so some legitimate titles were caught; critics argued this is a legal-certainty problem (jus.com.br article on administrative cancellation, https://jus.com.br/artigos/51653/o-cancelamento-administrativo-de-registros-e-matriculas-como-recurso-para-a-grilagem-de-terras-no-estado-do-para , listed in search, not read [UNVERIFIED as to content]).
- Sources: https://imazon.t3.storage.dev/combate-a-grilagem-de-terras-em-cartorios-no-para-uma-decada-de-avancos-e-desafios-a28bb932.pdf

## 3. Carlos Medeiros, the "phantom landowner" of Pará

- Years: papers dated to about 1800; first appears in an official document in 1975; 1981 inventory files vanish; 1984 copy reappears; 1993 inventory restored by a judge; 1996 Iterpa asks annulment; 2001 CPI calls it the largest grilagem case seen; 2005 press report; 2007 federal court suspends a plan on one farm (O Eco).
- Government/ruler: military regime (1975); Sarney through Itamar (1984-1993); Judiciary of Pará.
- What / how big: land registered in the name of a person who never appeared. Sources disagree on area: about 9 million ha in 32 municípios (8% of Pará, about 1% of Brazil), O Globo 20 Feb 2005 as reproduced by Arpensp https://arpensp.org.br/noticia/2015 ; about 12 million ha at one point, O Eco https://oeco.org.br/reportagens/2060-oeco_24013/ ; 1.8 million ha of his titles blocked, AMDA https://amda.org.br/opiniao/1807-mundo-fundiario-no-xingu-entre-o-caos-rios-rodovias-hidreletricas-mineracao-e-incertezas/ . So the range is 1.8 (blocked) to 9 to 12 million ha (claimed).
- Who got it (and financed): sold on to loggers and cattle ranchers; only one buyer is named in O Eco (Marinho Gomes de Figueiredo). The real backers were never identified; his lawyers (Viegas) were the only known figures (O Eco, same URL).
- Price/terms vs value: no price information in opened pages.
- Legal mechanism: an "adjudicação" letter built from inventories of two Portuguese men; 1993 restoration of "lost" inventory records granted without consulting Iterpa, the Union or the prosecutors (O Eco, same URL).
- Who bore the costs: Pará and the Union (public land); forest and communities on the land.
- After: MPF discovered the fraud after a logging plan was approved on Union land near Santarém and sought blocks in all registries; the Corregedoria chose provisional blocks instead of cancellation; Anoreg says the Judiciary has not cancelled all of Medeiros's registrations (search summary only) [UNVERIFIED].
- Link to Altamira / CR Almeida: the task text said Medeiros was a "fake heir" tied to Altamira and CR Almeida. The O Eco page I opened says explicitly that it does NOT mention C.R. Almeida or any Altamira registry, and does not say "heir". A search summary of Gazeta do Povo ties the Altamira registry and CR Almeida to a different fraud (case 4). Treat the Medeiros-CR Almeida link as NOT SUPPORTED.
- Counter-view: none found defending the titles; the debate is only about how many titles were cancelled.
- Sources: https://oeco.org.br/reportagens/2060-oeco_24013/ ; https://arpensp.org.br/noticia/2015 ; https://amda.org.br/opiniao/1807-mundo-fundiario-no-xingu-entre-o-caos-rios-rodovias-hidreletricas-mineracao-e-incertezas/

- What / how big: about 4.5 million ha (Belgium plus Netherlands in the DOL report), Gleba Curuá, Altamira, Incenxil (an C.R. Almeida group company), 9th Federal Court of Pará. ## 5. North Mato Grosso: private colonizers, Otsar/Cotriguaçu and the Sharp episode

- Years: 1970s-1980s (colonization), early 1980s (Figueiredo government: Otsar land passes to Cotriguaçu); IBGE: Cotriguaçu-Juruena project begins April 1984.
- Government/ruler: Médici-Geisel (incentives, SUDAM); Figueiredo.
- What / how big: INCRA authorised about 36 private colonization firms in northern Mato Grosso (Travessia 1989). Largest holdings: Rendanyl (later Otsar) 1,000,000 ha; Indeco (Ariosto da Riva) 400,000 ha; Colniza (Lunardelli) 400,000 ha; Juruena (João Carlos Meirelles) 200,000 ha. Only Indeco implemented its colonization on time; the others did little and the land did not return to the public estate. The Otsar land passed to Cotriguaçu "in a fraudulent operation" aimed at a later transfer to the multinational Sharp (the episode that brought down minister Amaury Stabile). The one million ha was then redistributed as 400,000 ha Cotriguaçu, 400,000 ha Juruena and 200,000 ha Indeco. Source: Travessia, Jan-Apr 1989 (opened PDF), https://travessia.emnuvens.com.br/travessia/article/download/37/33 . Corroboration of Cotriguaçu's 400,000 ha: IBGE profile https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtb/matogrosso/cotriguacu.pdf (search summary, [UNVERIFIED] in detail). A USP article citing Moreno (1999) says two million ha were transferred in 1973 to four colonization companies [UNVERIFIED], https://periodicos.usp.br/eav/article/download/132434/128568/253023 .
- Who got it (and financed): colonizers from Paraná/São Paulo capital and a Paraná cooperative (IBGE names the São Miguel do Iguaçu triticulture cooperative; an Assembly document names another cooperative, so the sources differ); fiscal incentives (SUDAM) per Travessia.
- Price/terms vs value: not stated.
- Legal mechanism: state land sales to colonizers, grilagem over Indigenous land, then resale or transfer between firms.
- Who bore the costs: Indigenous peoples (Travessia describes land taken from most of the state's peoples), posseiros, the public estate.
- After: colonizers sold lots to settlers from the South; the "Fazenda Cotriguaçu" as a named grilagem case was NOT found; only the colonizer is documented [UNVERIFIED as a grilagem case].
- Counter-view: colonizers describe themselves as "Bandeirantes of the 20th century", building towns on empty land (Travessia quotes their self-image).
- Sources: https://travessia.emnuvens.com.br/travessia/article/download/37/33 ; https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/dtb/matogrosso/cotriguacu.pdf

## 7. Undesignated public forests (UPF/FPND) and CAR self-declarations

- Years: CAR created 2012; data up to 2020 (IPAM, 2021).
- Government/ruler: Dilma (CAR created with Forest Code 2012); Temer/Bolsonaro (increase in registrations).
- What / how big: UPFs cover 57.5 million ha in the Amazon; by 2020 18.6 million ha of these were illegally registered in the CAR, 32% of the area. CAR inside UPFs rose 232% (period in the IPAM text). Source: IPAM-AOF7, https://ipam.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Amazon-on-Fire-7-en.pdf . Imazon: 86.1 million ha (17% of the Amazon) were still awaiting a destination (IMAZON-10, https://imazon.fly.storage.tigris.dev/uploads/2021/04/10FatosRegularizacaoFundiaria.pdf). The two figures use different definitions and dates (57.5 vs 86.1 million ha), so they are given as a range, not reconciled here.
- Who got it (and financed): claimants who file a CAR on public land; IPAM says 44% of 2020 CAR entries were large areas (figures per fiscal module class in the IPAM text).
- Price/terms vs value: CAR is free and self-declared; it is not a title but is used as proof of possession for later regularization (IPAM-AOF7).
- Legal mechanism: the CAR is self-declaratory and does not check ownership; overlaps with public forests are allowed to be registered, which feeds speculation and later regularization.
- Who bore the costs: the Union and the states; forest (deforestation and fire higher in CAR areas inside UPFs, IPAM-AOF7).
- After: ongoing; INCRA/SFB cross-checks, and in 2021 Imazon recommended blocking CAR on UPFs (not verified in the pages opened).
- Counter-view: defenders say CAR is only an environmental registry and does not confer ownership. IPAM's answer is that it works as a de facto land document.
- Sources: https://ipam.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Amazon-on-Fire-7-en.pdf ; https://imazon.fly.storage.tigris.dev/uploads/2021/04/10FatosRegularizacaoFundiaria.pdf

## 8. Terra Legal: prices charged vs market value, and the TCU audits

- Years: Lei 11.952/2009; TCU Acórdão 727/2020 (1 April 2020); Imazon price study 2021.
- Government/ruler: Lula (2009); Temer (2017 changes); Bolsonaro (MP 910/2019).
- What / how big: Lei 11.952 art. 11: free titling up to 1 fiscal module; art. 12 par. 1: for larger areas, 10% to 50% of the INCRA minimum land-value table (VTN); art. 17: payment over 20 years with up to 3 years of grace (statute text, gov.br/mda compiled PDF; the planalto.gov.br page was blocked). Imazon: if the federal government privatises the 19.6 million ha of undesignated public land at current legal values, society loses R$ 62 to 88 billion, about a quarter of Petrobras's 2019 market value; in Pará the gap between market value and state price reaches R$ 9 billion across 8,053 properties (IMAZON-10, same URL). Imazon also reports Amapá selling areas for as little as 4% of market value (IHU/Unisinos, https://ihu.unisinos.br/610016-grilagem-areas-publicas-da-amazonia-no-amapa-chegam-a-ser-vendidas-por-4-do-valor-de-mercado , search summary [UNVERIFIED]) and Roraima allowing sales with up to 85% discount for those who deforested (https://ihu.unisinos.br/611941-roraima-permite-venda-de-area-publica-com-ate-85-de-desconto-para-quem-desmatou , [UNVERIFIED]).
- TCU findings (via MPF-NT8, https://static.congressoemfoco.com.br/2020/04/nota-tecnica-8-2020-regularizacao-fundiaria.pdf): R$ 58.6 billion of land audited; 28,331 titles to 31/12/2017 (2,057,119.95 ha); at least R$ 1 billion loss from non-regularizable areas of at least 657.9 thousand ha occupied by private parties; 62 holders above 2,500 ha occupy 540 thousand ha (R$ 1.3 billion at VTN), with 385 thousand ha above the 2,500 ha limit (R$ 897 million), of which 193.5 thousand ha in Pará and 115 thousand ha in Rondônia; 887 thousand ha with refused applications still occupied (R$ 2.4 billion); at least 82 thousand ha deforested in a sample; 7,799.4296 ha titled to 94 holders who did not meet art. 5 of the law (loss above R$ 12 million). Production of titles fell to almost zero in the first half of 2019.
- Who got it (and financed): occupants of federal land in the Amazon, including holders above the legal limit.
- Price/terms vs value: legal price 10-50% of the INCRA VTN table, itself below market; Imazon compares with market averages per state (IMAZON-10 Table, same URL). The 2017 MPF recommendation (cnbsp.org.br page, 403 when I tried to open it) said new INCRA price values could fall far below market [UNVERIFIED].
- Legal mechanism: dispensation of bidding; discounts by size; instalments; self-declared occupation with remote checks.
- Who bore the costs: Treasury (forgone revenue), the forest, and legitimate small occupants who wait.
- After: TCU ordered the recovery of irregular areas; no complete recovery found [UNVERIFIED]. TCU 627/2015 price findings not confirmed [UNVERIFIED].
- Counter-view: the government argued that the subsidy helps poor occupants and that low land values reflect poor access and lack of infrastructure; Imazon replies that most area goes to large claimants and that the subsidy brings no socioenvironmental return (IMAZON-10, same URL).
- Sources: https://static.congressoemfoco.com.br/2020/04/nota-tecnica-8-2020-regularizacao-fundiaria.pdf ; https://imazon.fly.storage.tigris.dev/uploads/2021/04/10FatosRegularizacaoFundiaria.pdf

## 9. Amnesty laws and moving cutoff dates

- Years: 2009, 2017, 2019, 2018 (PL 8411/17 rejected).
- Government/ruler: Lula (Lei 11.952/2009); Temer (MP 759/2016, Lei 13.465/2017); Bolsonaro (MP 910/2019).
- What / how big: Lei 11.952 art. 5 IV and art. 38 sole paragraph: cutoff 22 July 2008; occupations after that date can be sold at the maximum VTN (statute text). Lei 13.465/2017 raised the regularizable limit from 1,500 ha to 2,500 ha (Câmara news, https://www.camara.leg.br/noticias/535768-projeto-que-reverte-regulamentacao-de-terras-da-amazonia-legal-e-rejeitado/ ); the stock of land in play was 19.6 million ha (MPF-NT8). MP 910/2019 would have allowed 55 to 65 million ha of undesignated Union land across Brazil to be regularised, including occupations with illegal deforestation after 22 July 2008 (MPF-NT8, same URL).
- Source conflict on the cutoff: Imazon/ERL 2019 says the 2017 law moved the cutoff to 2011 (cited from memory of the earlier read, 'ERL 2019', [UNVERIFIED]); the statute text opened shows 22 July 2008. Both recorded.
- Who got it (and financed): occupants with up to 2,500 ha and, under MP 910, larger claims; large claimants dominate the area (case 8).
- Price/terms vs value: see case 8.
- Legal mechanism: statutory amnesty by date; Câmara commission rejected PL 8411/17, which would have restored the 1,500 ha limit, on 11 April 2018. When MP 910 lapsed is NOT confirmed [UNVERIFIED]. Lei 14.757/2023 not researched.
- Who bore the costs: Treasury and forest; honest occupants who respected earlier cutoffs.
- After: ADIs against Lei 13.465 were filed (a former PGR, Raquel Dodge, argued unconstitutionality per an O Eco PDF found in search, not opened) [UNVERIFIED].
- Counter-view: proponents say regularization brings legal certainty and enables compliance with the Forest Code; MPF-NT8 argues it rewards past crimes.
- Sources: https://www.camara.leg.br/noticias/535768-projeto-que-reverte-regulamentacao-de-terras-da-amazonia-legal-e-rejeitado/ ; https://static.congressoemfoco.com.br/2020/04/nota-tecnica-8-2020-regularizacao-fundiaria.pdf

## Patterns

1. The same chain repeats from the 1970s to 2020s: occupy or forge, register, wait, then get an amnesty or discounted regularization.
2. Registries (cartórios) are the weak point: 91.1 million ha of Pará matrículas cancelled, plus a 410-million-ha single matrícula.
3. Estimates of "grilado" land (93-100 million ha) are suspicion figures, not court findings; adjudicated cancellations are smaller and slower.
4. Price is the hidden transfer: legal prices of 10-50% of an already-low INCRA table, and Imazon's R$ 62-88 billion forgone for 19.6 million ha.
5. Amnesty cutoffs move (2008, then area limits from 1,500 to 2,500 ha, then MP 910 proposals), which rewards earlier occupation and tells new grabbers to wait.
6. CAR turns a self-declaration into a de facto land claim: 18.6 million ha (32%) of undesignated forests were registered by 2020.
7. Costs fall on the Treasury, forests and Indigenous or traditional peoples; the people behind the "fantasma" owners are rarely named.
8. Several task-text claims did not hold up: the Medeiros-CR Almeida link, the Cuiabá registry case, a "Fazenda Cotriguaçu" grilagem case.
