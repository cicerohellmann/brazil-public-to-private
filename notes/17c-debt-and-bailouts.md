# 17c. Socialized losses and bank bailouts across eras

Scope: how private losses (external debt, failed banks, housing-fund gaps, pandemic credit) were moved onto the public balance sheet. Expands 03 #15 (external-debt estatização) and 04 #17-#20 (PROER, PROES, Banespa, other state banks); see those notes for the privatization side. ## 1. Estatização of the private external debt (1970s-1980s)

- **Years:** loans contracted mostly 1974-1980; state assumption via hedges and the 1982-83 renegotiation, running through the 1980s.
- **Government/ruler:** Geisel and Figueiredo (military regime); Delfim Netto at planning/finance.
- **What was transferred and how big:** private and state-firm foreign debts under Lei 4.131 and Resolução 63 loans were taken onto the public side. Mechanisms: Circular 230/1974 and Resolução 432/1977 (the BC guaranteed the exchange rate on foreign loans), DRME deposits, and "depósitos de projetos" so that private debtors paid cruzeiros to the BC while the BC owed the creditors dollars. Contemporary analysis of the public/private shift of the debt: IPEA 1988 https://repositorio.ipea.gov.br/server/api/core/bitstreams/1faac9f7-872f-4cd8-842f-baae55950e70/content ; Cruz 1995 https://revistas.face.ufmg.br/index.php/novaeconomia/article/download/2287/1232/7452 . Exact share of the debt that was private-then-public: [UNVERIFIED] for a single headline figure; the two papers give series, not one number I can quote safely.
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** private Brazilian borrowers and state firms were relieved of dollar risk; the foreign creditor-bank cartel was made whole through the 1982 rescheduling backed by the IMF. Brazilian taxpayers and monetary expansion financed it.
- **Price/terms vs value:** the BC sold exchange cover below the cost of the later devaluations; this is the central claim in Cruz 1995 (link above). Counter-view: the state took the debt to avoid a wave of private bankruptcies and a payments default during the 1982 crisis (argued in the IPEA text above).
- **Legal mechanism:** CMN/BC circulars and resolutions, not statutes passed by Congress.
- **Who bore the costs:** the Treasury and the BC (money printing, internal debt), so inflation and the lost decade fell on wage earners.
- **What happened after:** the debt was converted into the Brady bonds of 1994 [UNVERIFIED, not opened].
- **Sources:** the IPEA and UFMG links above; IMF crisis database for the later period https://www.imf.org/-/media/websites/imf/imported-full-text-pdf/external/pubs/ft/wp/2012/_wp12163.pdf .

## 2. PROER (1995-1997): the Nacional, Econômico and Bamerindus operations

- **Years:** MP 1.179 and 1.182 of Nov 1995, CMN Res. 2.208; operations 1995-97.
- **Government/ruler:** Fernando Henrique Cardoso; BC presidents Gustavo Loyola then Gustavo Franco's team (Franco as BC director) [UNVERIFIED who signed which operation].
- **What was transferred and how big:**
  - CPI PROER, final report: R$20.4bn lent; R$7.1bn net loss to the Union around 2002. https://www2.camara.leg.br/atividade-legislativa/comissoes/comissoes-temporarias/parlamentar-de-inquerito/51-legislatura/cpiproer/relatoriofinal/cap6consideracoesfinais.pdf
  - Estadão/Gazeta do Povo (2020): about R$30bn of Proer debt still owed by the banks involved. https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/economia/bancos-divida-proer/
  - Same article, quoting the Fruet estimate: R$71bn in corrected transfers.
  - IMF WP/12/163 (Laeven and Valencia): fiscal cost of the entire 1994-98 Brazilian banking crisis 13.2% of GDP, not Proer alone. https://www.imf.org/-/media/websites/imf/imported-full-text-pdf/external/pubs/ft/wp/2012/_wp12163.pdf
  - Range: R$7.1bn (CPI, net) to R$71bn (Fruet, corrected, gross transfers). Different scope and correction; do not average.
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** the BC lent to the buyer bank (Itaú-type buyers took Econômico's good assets, Unibanco took Nacional's, HSBC took Bamerindus in March 1997 [HSBC date UNVERIFIED, from search snippet only]). Healthy assets went to the buyer; bad assets and FCVS credits stayed in the liquidated estate. The Union financed it through BC credit and BC bond sterilisation.
- **Price/terms vs value:** the loans were at below-market terms and long maturities (CPI chapter VI above). Nacional: the hole was hidden by fictitious loans for years; Money Times https://www.moneytimes.com.br/a-historia-do-banco-que-estampou-o-bone-azul-de-ayrton-senna-e-acabou-no-centro-de-uma-das-maiores-fraudes-do-sistema-financeiro-isbl/ ; ConJur on 2013 arrests of former directors https://www.conjur.com.br/2013-set-03/policia-federal-prende-quatro-ex-dirigentes-banco-nacional//?print=1 .
- **Legal mechanism:** MPs 1.179/1.182 and CMN Res. 2.208. Lei 9.447/97 later made controllers jointly liable and allowed their assets to be frozen (CPI chapter VI above).
- **Who bore the costs:** the Treasury, through BC provisions that flow into the Treasury balance. Controllers largely kept personal wealth, which is why the 2020 debt article shows decades-long collection fights.
- **What happened after:** CPI "Proer" (Câmara) concluded intervention was necessary but criticised controls; the BC attorney-general (cited in the CPI chapter) argued that losses of R$40bn or R$69.5bn were avoided [exact attribution within the chapter: re-check before quoting]. FGC and the sale of the estates continued into the 2020s.
- **Counter-view:** a systemic run in 1995 would have cost more; supporters point to the avoided losses above.
- **Sources:** all links above; BCB book on state banks https://www.bcb.gov.br/htms/public/BancosEstaduais/livro_bancos_estaduais.pdf ; BCB saneamento paper https://www.bcb.gov.br/ftp/saneamento.pdf (opened; gives the R$3.0bn / R$3.1bn insured-deposit context for earlier cases only).

## 3. PROES and Banespa (1996-2000)

- **Years:** MP 1.514/1996; CMN Res. 2.365/1997; Lei 9.496/97 (state-debt refinancing). Banespa federalised Dec 1994 (RAET), sold to Santander in Nov 2000.
- **Government/ruler:** FHC.
- **What was transferred and how big:**
  - BCB book: R$61.4bn in federal securities issued to states under PROES. https://www.bcb.gov.br/htms/public/BancosEstaduais/livro_bancos_estaduais.pdf
  - ANPUH paper: "more than R$73bn". https://anpuh.org.br/uploads/anais-simposios/pdf/2019-01/1548772005_adf83124319e5fa5b6bfb10d220fef86.pdf
  - Sul21 piece: about R$500bn when state-debt assumption is included [UNVERIFIED, search snippet; scope is the whole state-debt refinancing, not PROES].
  - The R$74.58bn (R$12.18bn + R$62.4bn) figure sometimes quoted belongs to PROEF (federal banks), not PROES: Senate PLS 561/15 document https://legis.senado.leg.br/sdleg-getter/documento/download/df9a7785-ef5c-4545-aee0-076d3d3dfbcd
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** state governments (debts refinanced), state-bank creditors, and buyers (Itaú, Bradesco, Santander, ABN) who received cleaned banks. The Union financed with federal securities.
- **Price/terms vs value:** states federalised or privatised banks; the Union absorbed the bad assets. Price paid for Banespa: [UNVERIFIED, not opened].
- **Legal mechanism:** MP 1.514 and Lei 9.496/97; state-debt contracts with the Union indexed to IGP-DI plus fixed interest.
- **Who bore the costs:** the Union (and so taxpayers); states bore it through long debt service to the Treasury.
- **Counter-view:** the BCB book argues the cleanup cost belongs to past state-bank management (politically-driven lending), not to the program that closed them.
- **What happened after:** see 04 #18-#20 for the sales. Federal-bank follow-up (BB 1996-97 recapitalisation under MP 1.504, 1.553/1.555 with an R$8bn ceiling; PROEF 2001 and Emgea): amounts [UNVERIFIED], only search snippets seen.

## 4. Marka and FonteCindam rescue (Jan 1999, Cacciola)

- **Years:** 11-14 Jan 1999 events; civil judgment 2012; TRF-1 2017.
- **Government/ruler:** FHC; BC president Francisco Lopes (took over days before), Chico Lopes.
- **What was transferred and how big:** the BC sold dollar futures to Marka and FonteCindam below market after the 13 Jan 1999 devaluation.
  - 2012 civil sentence: R$1.05bn plus R$0.52bn to be repaid. https://www.jb.com.br/pais/noticias/2012/03/29/amp/ex-dirigentes-do-bc-e-banqueiros-sao-condenados-a-devolver-bilhoes-ao-erario.html
  - Annulment action: R$5.4bn.
  - ConJur on the annulled condemnation: "more than R$8bn". https://www.conjur.com.br/2017-jun-27/resgate-banco-marka-banco-central-foi-legal-decide-trf//?print=1
  - MPF claim: R$5.7bn plus R$3.1bn [UNVERIFIED, search snippet].
  - Counter-view: no loss at all (BC and TRF-1, same ConJur link).
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** Marka (Salvatore Cacciola) and FonteCindam; the Union financed through the BC.
- **Price/terms vs value:** futures sold at a rate below the market rate of the day (JB link above).
- **Legal mechanism:** BC discretion over exchange operations; civil improbity action.
- **Who bore the costs:** the BC/Treasury, per the 2012 sentence, until reversed.
- **What happened after:** the 2012 civil condemnation was annulled and TRF-1 held the rescue legal in 2017 (ConJur link). Cacciola criminal case (peculato, fled to Italy, extradited from Monaco 2008): [UNVERIFIED, not opened]. Regarding the criminal case, the figures above must not be mixed with the civil case.

## 5. FCVS (housing-fund salary-variation compensation)

- **Years:** fund created 1967; wide residual balances from the 1980s inflation; novação under Lei 10.150/2000; offset rider in MP 517/2011.
- **Government/ruler:** military regime (origin), then FHC/Lula/Dilma.
- **What was transferred and how big:** salary-indexed mortgage instalments versus inflation-indexed debt; residual balance assumed by the Union. Current total debt of the FCVS: [UNVERIFIED]. Gazeta do Povo (2011) on the rider letting failed banks cut their FCVS-related debts: https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/economia/lei-garante-reducao-de-divida-a-bancos-falidos-51z13fp30r12v5cig2ui1aez2/ . Diário do Comércio (2023) on AGU and FCVS: https://diariodocomercio.com.br/?p=339215 .
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** mortgage lenders (including banks later in Proer) and mutuários; FGTS and the Treasury financed it.
- **Price/terms vs value:** credits novated into securities at terms set by Lei 10.150; discounts in the 2011 rider (Gazeta link).
- **Legal mechanism:** Lei 10.150/2000 and an MP 517/2011 rider (a "jabuti").
- **Who bore the costs:** the Union and the FGTS.
- **Counter-view:** the FCVS was a promise made to mutuários by the state, so paying lenders was a contractual obligation rather than a gift [argument inferred, UNVERIFIED].

## Patterns

1. Mechanism over statute: BC circulars and CMN resolutions, MPs, and riders in unrelated bills moved most of the money, not laws debated by Congress.
2. Good bank/bad bank split: the buyer gets healthy assets and the liquidated estate or Treasury keeps the rest (PROER, PROES).
3. Cost figures are always a range because scope differs (net loss, gross transfer, corrected value, share of a whole crisis).
4. Collection is slow: controllers and debtors keep wealth for decades (Proer debts still owed in 2020, per Gazeta link above).
5. Courts later split on whether the bailout was lawful (Marka: condemned in 2012, legal in 2017).
6. State banks (BB, Caixa) are used as a bailout channel in crises (2009-10), blurring bailout and strategic purchase.
7. Guarantee funds replaced direct rescue in 2020, hiding the loss in fund equity.
