## 1. End of Petrobras's mandatory pre-salt operator role (Lei 13.365/2016)
- **Year / government:** 2016 (law of 29 Nov 2016), Temer. Bill PLS 131/2015 by Sen. José Serra.
- **What:** Revoked the rule in Lei 12.351/2010 that made Petrobras operator of all pre-salt blocks with at least 30% stake. Petrobras kept only a right of first refusal ("preferência"). First auctions under the new rules were set for 27 Oct 2017.
- **Buyer/beneficiary:** Foreign and private oil companies able to operate pre-salt blocks.
- **Price/terms vs value:** No single price. A dispute over the value of the reserves ceded; no estimate found in these sources **[CHECK]**.
- **Legal mechanism:** Ordinary law amending the pre-salt production-sharing regime.
- **Who bore costs:** Disputed. Critics (FUP, unions) say the Treasury and the country lose control of the strategic reserve. Supporters (Senate bill rationale) said Petrobras lacked capital to operate everything.
- **After:** Petrobras said it would use the preference "very selectively" (Pedro Parente, per Carta Capital). A union challenged the law in court (no outcome found).

## 3. BR Distribuidora privatization
- **Year / government:** 2019 (first sale, loss of control) and June 2021 (exit), Bolsonaro.
- **What:** 2019: ~350 million shares at R$ 24.50, about R$ 8.6 bn, taking Petrobras from control to 41.25% (37.5% if the supplementary lot was sold; up to R$ 9.6 bn). 2021: remaining 37.5% at R$ 26/share, R$ 11.36 bn. Together reported as about R$ 24.4 bn into the Treasury/Petrobras.
- **Buyer:** Dispersed market (public share offerings, no controller). The company later became Vibra Energia (not confirmed in the results; check).
- **Price vs value:** Market-priced offerings; no undervaluation estimate found.
- **Mechanism:** Follow-on share offerings registered with CVM, no control premium.
- **Costs/after:** Critics (Brasil de Fato, O Cafezinho, 2022) reported ex-BR stations selling the most expensive gasoline in several capitals. Seen as an advocacy claim, not a systematic study **[CHECK]**. Petrobras later discussed retaking part of the brand/stake (Money Times, Eixos).

## 5. RLAM refinery (Bahia) to Mubadala/Acelen
- **Year / government:** Concluded Nov 2021, Bolsonaro.
- **What:** Refinery Landulpho Alves (Mataripe), renamed Mataripe; operator Acelen (Mubadala Capital, Abu Dhabi).
- **Price vs value:** Price US$ 1.65 bn (some sources say US$ 1.8 bn **[CHECK]**). Petrobras's own base scenario was US$ 3.04 bn, so the price was ~45% below it (reported from the CGU audit of Jan 2024). INEEP (union-linked think tank) estimates US$ 3-4 bn, "half price". TCU authorized the sale in 2021 and rejected the challenges. CADE approved without restrictions.
- **Mechanism:** Competitive sale of an asset by a state company, with TCU and CADE review. The sale ended Petrobras's regional refining monopoly and created a private regional monopoly in Bahia.
- **Costs:** CADE opened an investigation into Acelen for alleged price discrimination against Bahia buyers of gasoline A and diesel S10. Acelen accuses Petrobras of the reverse. A current Bahia gasoline price series was **not found** **[GAP]**.
- **After:** Petrobras resumed talks to buy the refinery back (due diligence reported Jul 2024). Outcome not confirmed.

## 6. Eletrobras privatization (capitalization, 2021-22)
- **Year / government:** Law 14.182/2021 (Bolsonaro); offering June 2022; TCU approval 2022.
- **What:** Capital increase and share offering, diluting the Union. Offer priced at R$ 42/share, ~R$ 29.3 bn, up to R$ 33.7 bn with supplementary lots. Union's voting stake fell from ~72% to ~42-45% (bases vary: 45% per Bloomberg Línea, ~42% Felsberg, 40.29% voting / 37% total per O Antagonista). Voting power of any shareholder capped at 10%.
- **Price vs value:** TCU minister Vital do Rêgo (dissent) claimed undervaluation of R$ 46 bn, because the sale did not count the future commercialization of plant output, and called Itaipu's value "evidently undervalued". TCU technical staff projected a R$ 16.2 bn loss (a labor-union document is the source; treat with caution). The plenary approved 6-1 (stage one) and later 7-1 per press.
- **Jabutis:** The law forced contracting of 8 GW of gas thermal plants, even where there are no gas pipelines. Estimate: R$ 50 bn for the plants alone, excluding pipelines (PDE 2031 per one source).
- **Mechanism:** Law 14.182/2021 (from MP 1031) converting a state company into a corporation via dilution (no direct sale).
- **Costs:** Consumers via tariffs (thermal plants, CDE-related items); Treasury via lower value.
- **After:** Company renamed (Axia in some sources). Shares "still struggling" 3 years on (Eixos, 2025).

## 7. Eletrobras 10% voting cap and the Union's reply (ADI 7385)
- **Year / government:** Challenge filed 2023; agreement homologated by STF ~Dec 2025 **[CHECK exact date]**; Lula 3.
- **What:** The Union holds ~42% of common shares but votes only 10%. Settlement reached in the STF conciliation center: Union nominates 3 of 10 board members and 1 of 5 fiscal council members; rights drop if its stake falls below 20%, and the nominations fall to 2 below 30%. Eletronuclear guarantees were part of the deal. Rapporteur: Nunes Marques. PGR supported homologation.
- **Meaning:** The cap stays (10%); the Treasury gets governance seats, not proportional votes.

## 8. Sabesp privatization (São Paulo)
- **Year / government:** Offering closed 22 Jul 2024; State of SP (Tarcísio), federal law context from Lei 14.026/2020.
- **What:** State sold ~32% of shares (from 50.3% to ~18.3%) at R$ 67/share for ~R$ 14.7-14.8 bn. Equatorial Energia bought 15% (R$ 6.9 bn) and became the reference shareholder (no formal control). New single concession replaces 375 municipal contracts; investment commitment R$ 260 bn through 2060 (R$ 69 bn by 2029); universalization target moved from 2033 to 2029.
- **Price vs value:** Opponents say the price was low: shares hit R$ 85 days earlier, ~20% above the sale price. No independent valuation study found **[CHECK]**.

In 2026 critics (Haddad) cite Procon-SP complaints (8,875 so far in 2026, 3,829 for improper billing) and call it the "Enel of water". The governor claims tariffs are 15% below the no-privatization path. Both are political claims **[CHECK]**.

## 9. Marco Legal do Saneamento (Lei 14.026/2020) and CEDAE auctions
- **Year / government:** Law July 2020 (Bolsonaro); CEDAE auction 30 Apr 2021 (RJ state).
- **What:** The law requires bidding for sanitation services previously handed to state companies without competition. CEDAE auction: ~R$ 22.6-22.7 bn in grant payments for 3 of 4 blocks against a minimum total of R$ 10.6 bn. ## 14. Garimpo expansion, Yanomami crisis and PL 191/2020 (mining on Indigenous land)
- **Year / government:** 2018-2022 (Bolsonaro; PL 191 sent Feb 2020); emergency declared Jan 2023 (Lula 3).
- **What:** Illegal mining on Yanomami land: Hutukara estimated ~20,000 miners (ISA 2021: 21,000). Mining-related deforestation grew 309% between Oct 2018 and Dec 2022. Malaria cases rose from 9,908 (2018) to 21,883 (2021). Federal government declared a national public-health emergency.
- **PL 191/2020:** Would regulate mining, oil and gas and hydropower on Indigenous lands. Urgency approved by the Câmara on 9 Mar 2022; the mining industry association (Ibram) opposed. Current status unverified **[CHECK]**.
- **Beneficiaries:** Illegal gold miners and their financiers; no fiscal benefit to the State.
- **Costs:** Yanomami lives and health, rivers, forest. Bolsonaro's government at one point called the crisis exaggerated (DOL).
- **After:** Emergency response from 2023. Hutukara's report says the Uraricoera river holds 52% of the mining-degraded area.

## 16. Marco Temporal (Lei 14.701/2023 and STF ruling)
- **Year / government:** Law 14.701/2023 (Congress overriding Lula's vetoes); STF Tema 1.031 (Sep 2023); conciliation 2024-Jun 2025; STF merits Dec 2025.
- **What:** The law imposed the 5 Oct 1988 cutoff for Indigenous land claims and "renitente esbulho" requirements. STF (rapporteur Gilmar Mendes) declared the marco temporal and the renitente esbulho requirement unconstitutional in Dec 2025, with partial dissent from Fachin and Cármen Lúcia on other provisions. Vote tally differs by source (9-1 per Tribuna do Sertão; other outlets differ) **[CHECK]**. The conciliation commission drafted a bill (23 hearings, ended 23 Jun 2025); APIB left the table in Aug 2024, calling it forced conciliation.
- **Beneficiaries (had it stood):** Landholders, agribusiness, mining/energy interests on claimed land.
- **After:** Unresolved legislative risk; whether Congress responds (PEC) not confirmed here **[GAP]**.

## Patterns

1. **Price set by a narrow valuation method, then disputed.** Eletrobras (R$ 46 bn dissent), RLAM (45% below Petrobras's own base case; CGU), Vale rails (R$ 200 mn vs R$ 14 bn depending on method), Sabesp (R$ 67 vs R$ 85 days earlier). The regulator or the seller chooses the method, and the audit court mostly approves.
2. **Private monopoly or oligopoly replaces public one, with regulators arriving late.** RLAM in Bahia (CADE probe into price discrimination), Sabesp single concession, Liquigás (CADE conditions), Santos terminal concentration.
3. **Congress as the engine, and vetoes of the Executive are overridden.** Marco Temporal (2023), Licensing Law (Nov 2025). Also "jabutis" (gas thermal plants in Eletrobras law) attached to the main law. The same Congress can undo what the government or the court blocks.
4. **Land and environmental protection traded for infrastructure or mining.** Jamanxim cut (Ferrogrão), Renca, PL 191, Law 13.465, licensing law.
5. **The Executive swings back and forth by government.** Lula 3 contests some deals (Eletrobras ADI, buyback talks for RLAM, Braskem co-control) but keeps others (Sabesp model, Foz do Amazonas auctions, Redata waiver, licensing sanctioned with vetoes).
6. **Courts and the TCU are a brake only sometimes.** Renca stopped by court; Marco Temporal struck down by STF (Dec 2025); but TCU approved Eletrobras, RLAM and Vale rails, and the STF validated the Jamanxim cut in 2026.
7. **Costs fall on the Treasury and consumers (tariffs) or on those without voice (Indigenous peoples, environment).** Eletrobras thermal mandate; Sabesp tariff cuts paid from sale proceeds; Yanomami health crisis.
8. **Fiscal privileges grew while assets were sold.** Federal tax expenditure around 4.4-4.8% of GDP in 2025.
