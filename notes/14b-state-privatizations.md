# 14b. State-level privatizations, 1995-2026

Scope: what states sold, to whom, for how much, with what BNDES/federal money, and what happened to tariffs and service afterwards. Cases already under "## " headings in `04-privatization-1985-2002.md` are not repeated. Coelba, CPFL and Banespa appear only as "additions" (cases 10 and 18 there). Banestado, BEG and BEA are touched only inside the state-banks entries.

Conventions: every number has its source URL beside it. `[UNVERIFIED]` means the figure comes from a search snippet or a page that could not be opened, so do not quote it without checking. Where sources disagree, both figures are shown. "Counter-view" is the other side's argument. Union and CUT/Sindiagua pieces are advocacy sources and are labelled so.

## CEEE (RS), 1997 split of distribution

- **What:** Under Governor Britto, CEEE's distribution area was cut in two and sold as AES Sul and RGE. CEEE kept generation, transmission and the rest of distribution (later CEEE-D). Source: https://www.brasildefato.com.br/2019/09/18/privatizacoes-conta-da-venda-da-ceee-vai-cair-no-colo-dos-gauchos
- **Price / buyer:** total of R$ 3.14 bi for the two concessions [UNVERIFIED, snippet only]. Buyers were AES (AES Sul) and the RGE consortium. Per-lot prices not confirmed.
- **BNDES / public money:** not established for these two lots.
- **After:** a former Fundacao CEEE manager says CEEE "lost 50% of revenue and inherited 88% of expense" (claim by an interested party, same Brasil de Fato article). CEEE debt was estimated at more than R$ 4 bi and about 4,000 direct jobs were at risk when the remainder was put up for sale (same article).
- **Counter-view:** the Leite government argued the remnant could not be saved as a state company (see next entry).

## CEEE-D (RS), 2021 sale

- **What:** the rump distributor, sold at an auction in 2021. Contract signed 8 Jul 2021. Source: https://ihu.unisinos.br/638169-qual-e-o-balanco-da-privatizacao-da-energia-no-rs
- **Price / buyer:** **R$ 100,000** (symbolic). Equatorial was the only bidder. Source: https://braziljournal.com/a-equatorial-levou-a-ceee-d-um-bom-negocio-para-os-dois-lados
- **Liabilities:** analysts assumed about R$ 3.5 bi of debt, and the ICMS debt alone exceeded R$ 3.3 bi (same Brazil Journal piece). Equatorial must invest R$ 1.6 bi. The 2015 renewal to 2045 depended on quality indicators that CEEE-D missed two years running. Analysts put value at 1.5x-2x RAB. CPFL did not bid, citing risk and return.
- **BNDES money:** none found.
- **After:** Equatorial serves about 1.7 million consumers in 72 municipalities. After the 16 Jan 2024 storm, Governor Leite threatened the concession. Critics cite layoffs and outsourcing, and the Porto Alegre council opened a CPI (https://ihu.unisinos.br/638169-qual-e-o-balanco-da-privatizacao-da-energia-no-rs).
- **Counter-view:** Leite says private firms should deliver the service and that the state could not fund the needed investment (same IHU piece). The sale was needed to stop a caducidade risk (Brazil Journal link above).
- **Open:** a May 2021 court suspension of the share sale (Sul21 snippet) and its outcome are [UNVERIFIED].

## CEEE-T (RS), 2021 sale of transmission

- **What:** 66.08% of CEEE-T auctioned on B3 on 16 Jul 2021. Over 6,000 km of lines and 69 substations. Source: https://www.infomoney.com.br/?p=1667233
- **Price / buyer:** CPFL won with **R$ 2.67 bi**, a 57.13% agio over the R$ 1.7 bi minimum. Runner-up Energisa R$ 2.66 bi. Others: MEZ R$ 2.035 bi, Cymi R$ 1.958 bi, Transmissao Paulista R$ 1.925 bi, CL RJ 004 R$ 1.765 bi (same InfoMoney page).
- **BNDES:** none found.
- **After:** CPFL annual report snippet says transfer closed in October 2021 [UNVERIFIED]. CEEE-G (generation) was not sold as of 2021. The fate of CEEE-GT is unconfirmed.
- **Pattern note:** the same state got R$ 2.67 bi for the profitable wires and R$ 100,000 for the money-losing distributor.

## CRT (Companhia Riograndense de Telecomunicacoes, RS)

- **What:** state telecom sold in two steps in the 1996-98 telecom wave. Source for all prices: https://www.mobiletime.com.br/museu-movel/15/12/2023/operadoras-do-passado-crt/
- **Price / buyer:** Dec 1996, 35% to Tele Brasil Sul (Telefonica, RBS, Portugal Telecom, Iberdrola, BBV) for **R$ 681 mi**. Jun 1998, full privatization for **R$ 1.17 bi** (note: the R$ 1.17 bi is the later, full sale, not an alternative price for the same stake). Jul 2000, Telefonica sold CRT to Brasil Telecom for US$ 800 mi, and the two merged in Dec 2000.
- **BNDES:** not found for this sale.
- **After:** the buyer re-sold within about two years, which shows how much of the early value sat in the licence.
- **Counter-view / unverified:** union and party-site allegations about Opportunity involvement and irregularities are advocacy and snippet-only [UNVERIFIED]. A R$ 472 mi agio in a Sedep snippet belongs to TBH's 1997 incorporation, not to the auction premium.

## Cemig (MG), 1997 stake sale and the shareholders' agreement fight

- **What:** auction on 28 May 1997 on the Rio stock exchange. Southern Electric Brasil (Southern Co., AES, Opportunity) was the only bidder of five pre-qualified, for about 33% of ordinary shares. Source: https://southerncompany.mediaroom.com/index.php?item=1355
- **Price:** US$ 1.056 bi for 33% of voting shares, a 14.4% economic interest (Southern release above). Local figure R$ 1.1 bi paid in installments over ten years, financed by BNDES (https://istoedinheiro.com.br/o-dono-da-energia, which wrongly dates the sale to 1995, a source error). Minimum R$ 1.13 bi with half financed by BNDES at 3.35% a year [UNVERIFIED, snippet].
- **BNDES money:** SEB's BNDES debt was R$ 2.115 bi, contracted in 1997 (https://exame.com/negocios/andrade-gutierrez-faz-acerto-ter-33-cemig-528026/). It stood at R$ 3.19 bi at end of 2008 (same).
- **The fight:** the deal gave the buyer a veto over investments above US$ 1 mi, and a state inquiry in Sep 1999 judged it "unsound" (https://www.nsenergybusiness.com/?p=95990). In Jan 1999 Governor Itamar Franco, whose state held 51% of ordinary shares, ordered a review of the agreement (https://teletime.com.br/14/01/1999/itamar-quer-reaver-os-33-das-acoes-da-energetica/). AES then defaulted on BNDES (Dinheiro link above).
- **Outcome:** in 2007 SEB offered US$ 1.2 bi cash, which BNDES would accept. The Ministerio Publico argued this meant a loss and asked a court to veto it. Andrade Gutierrez assumed the debt, with only about R$ 300 mi of discount in court costs (Exame link). Closing year is probably 2009 but not stated.
- **Court detail [UNVERIFIED]:** STJ Revista snippet says the state sued to annul the agreement and a TJMG rapporteur suspended it (agravo 164.822-9). An ALMG snippet says the MP's civil action was dismissed in Sep 1998 and minority holders got 4 of 11 board seats.
- **Counter-view:** the buyers' side was that they paid for control rights and the state later changed the rules.

## Celg (GO), 2016

- **What:** distribution arm (Celg-D) auctioned 30 Nov 2016 after a failed attempt in Aug 2016 (no buyers). Source: https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/economia/noticia/2016-11/privatizacao-da-celg-distribuicao-obtem-agio-de-2803
- **Price / buyer:** Enel won with **R$ 2.187 bi**, a 28.03% agio. Minimum was R$ 1.791 bi or R$ 1.792 bi depending on the snippet [UNVERIFIED]. Cash. Eletrobras got R$ 1.065 bi because it held 50.93%. The MME official said proceeds would help recover Eletrobras.
- **BNDES:** none found. The money went to Eletrobras, the federal holding, not to Goias.
- **After:** in Sep 2022 Enel sold Celg-D to Equatorial for **R$ 1.58 bi**, with Equatorial taking on R$ 5.7 bi of debt. Celg-D was missing regulatory quality targets and risked its concession (https://investnews.com.br/negocios/equatorial-compra-celg-d-da-enel-por-r-158-bilhao/amp/). A snippet ranks Celg-D 3rd-worst of 29 in Aneel's 2021 continuity ranking [UNVERIFIED].
- **Counter-view:** the state said about R$ 8 bi would be invested (R$ 2 bi purchase, R$ 4 bi debt, R$ 2 bi expansion) [UNVERIFIED, snippet].

## CEB (DF), 2020

- **What:** Companhia Energetica de Brasilia's distribution unit, auctioned 4 Dec 2020. Source: https://forbes.com.br/negocios/2020/12/grupo-neoenergia-leva-distribuidora-ceb-em-privatizacao-de-r-25-bi/
- **Price / buyer:** Bahia Geracao (Neoenergia, controlled by Iberdrola) bid **R$ 2.515 bi**, a 76.63% agio over about R$ 1.4 bi. CPFL bid R$ 2.508 bi and Equatorial opened at R$ 1.485 bi. CEB risked caducidade, and liquidating it could have cost the DF about R$ 1 bi (the article's own estimate).
- **BNDES:** BNDES modelled the sale, and its president Montezano estimated R$ 5 bi of investment (Forbes link). No BNDES financing found.
- **Legal:** a court injunction from the night before was lifted by STJ president Humberto Martins, who found that the sale did not need prior enabling legislation (https://eixos.com.br/energia-eletrica/presidente-do-stj-derruba-liminar-e-permite-leilao-da-ceb-distribuicao/). The STF (Nunes Marques) kept an injunction. A later "STF annulled" claim is [UNVERIFIED].
- **After:** contract signed 2 Mar 2021 [UNVERIFIED, snippet]. Tariff history not found.

## Copel (PR), 2023 follow-on

- **What:** not a control sale. A share offering that cut the state's stake and created a company without a controller. Source: https://braziljournal.com/copel-lanca-oferta-de-r-5-bi-e-se-aproxima-da-privatizacao
- **Price:** base offer R$ 4.3 bi (R$ 4.95 bi with 15% greenshoe) at reference price R$ 7.85. 41.9% primary. Parana's stake falls from 31.1% to 15.6%. BNDES held 24% and did not buy. A golden share was modelled on Eletrobras. Primary proceeds of R$ 1.9 bi fund a R$ 3.7 bi outorga for renewing the Foz do Areia, Segredo and Salto Caxias plants (4.1 GW, 60% of capacity). Coordinators: BTG, Itau BBA, Bradesco BBI, UBS BB, Morgan Stanley.
- **Final numbers [UNVERIFIED]:** R$ 5.2 bi (US$ 1.06 bi) raised and a 10% voting cap, from Brazilian Report (https://brazilian.report/?p=129932) and Cleary Gottlieb snippets. The pages were paywalled or landing pages.
- **After:** not researched (too recent for tariff effects).
- **Counter-view:** unions and some deputies said the state gave up control of the water and power it used for its own industrial policy. The Brazil Journal piece frames the state as keeping a veto through the golden share.

## Corsan (RS), 2022

- **What:** first state sanitation company privatized under the 2020 sanitation law. B3 auction on 20 Dec 2022. Source: https://neofeed.com.br/economia/corsan-e-a-1a-estatal-de-saneamento-privatizada-por-r-41-bi-mas-marco-legal-corre-riscos
- **Price / buyer:** Aegea, sole bidder, **R$ 4.151 bi**, a 1.15% agio over the R$ 4.1 bi minimum. Corsan served 307 municipalities and about 6 million people, with 5,995 employees and a pension liability of R$ 943 mi. Aegea must invest R$ 12 bi to reach universal service by 2033. 198 of 317 municipalities had not signed regularization terms (38% of revenue). A union request to postpone was rejected by the TST. Iguá, Aguas do Brasil and Equatorial visited but did not bid.
- **BNDES:** no BNDES role found.
- **After:** Aegea took control in Jul 2023 with 5,600 employees, about 2,000 had left by Mar 2024, and 18 months of job stability was agreed. Investment about R$ 2 bi a year against about R$ 500 mi a year under the state. More than 200 contracts renegotiated. A TCE case seeks annulment (https://www.bloomberglinea.com.br/negocios/privatizacao-da-sabesp-as-licoes-da-aegea-a-frente-da-corsan-segundo-o-ceo/). Tariff adjustments of 6.46% (Jan 2025) and 4.68% (Jan 2026) [UNVERIFIED]. A TJ-RS suspension of a 23.01% adjustment, undated [UNVERIFIED].
- **Counter-view:** Jerson Kelman: profit funds expansion (NeoFeed). The opposing side, from the Sindiagua president (advocacy): the TCE/RS rapporteur found the price underestimated and the 1a Camara recommended annulment (https://outraspalavras.net/?p=3093029).

## Cedae (RJ), 2021 concessions

- **What:** water and sewage concession of the Rio metropolitan region in four blocks, auctioned 30 Apr 2021 on B3. BNDES designed the model. Cedae stays as bulk water seller and about 30 municipalities stayed out. Source: https://www.infomoney.com.br/mercados/leilao-da-cedae-arrecada-r-22-bilhoes-com-concessao-de-3-blocos-e-supera-projecoes/
- **Price / buyer:** total R$ 22.7 bi for three blocks against R$ 10.6 bi minimum outorga. Block 1: Aegea R$ 8.2 bi (minimum R$ 4.036 bi, agio 103.13%). Block 2: Iguá R$ 7.286 bi (minimum R$ 3.172 bi, agio 129.68%). Block 4: Aegea R$ 7.2 bi (agio about 188%). Block 3 had no winner and was re-auctioned to Aguas do Brasil for about R$ 2.2 bi [UNVERIFIED, InvestNews snippet]. 35-year contracts, about R$ 30 bi of investment, about 13 million people.
- **BNDES:** structurer, not financier, as far as found.
- **After:** Iguá owed a final outorga installment of R$ 1.8 bi due early Feb 2025, sought arbitration to deposit only R$ 828 mi citing losses and social-tariff counts above the tender estimates, and the state regulator Agenersa denied suspension (https://www.correiodamanha.com.br/especiais/2025/01/179722-igua-usa-pedido-de-suspensao-da-outorga-para-dar-calote-de-rs-1-8-bilhao-no-estado-do-rio.html; "calote" is the outlet's word). In Oct 2025 a conciliation term cut the wholesale water cost by 24.13% over the sewage-coverage gap; Cedae withdrew in Dec 2025 and the TJ-RJ suspended the agreement. A 7.10% rebalancing increase was approved, and the Feb 2026 adjustment was 9.75% (Block 1) and 8.09% (Block 4). Aguas do Rio serves about 10 million people in 27 municipalities (https://diariodorio.com/justica-suspende-acordo-entre-cedae-e-aguas-do-rio-e-conta-pode-subir-7-em-27-municipios-do-rj/).
- **Unverified:** TCE-RJ suspension and clearance dates, and 10.97%/15.89% increases from Dec 2025, which conflict with the above.
- **Counter-view:** the bids more than doubled the minimum, which supporters read as competition and critics read as a promise that tariffs or renegotiation would have to pay for.

## Sao Paulo highway concessions (1998 onward)

- **What:** under Covas (1995-2001), 12 lots (more than 3,500 km) were granted in 1998. First stage ran 20 years, but contracts were extended on average by about 5 years, up to 28 years, after concessionaires' rebalancing claims. In 11 of 12 tenders the winner was chosen by highest outorga. Second stage (2007-11) added 2,700 km, chosen by lowest toll, with 30-year terms. Tamoios PPP began 2015. As of Feb 2018 there were 22 concessionaires, 149 toll plazas and 8,292 km of 22,095 km (37.5%) of state highways. ARTESP was created in 2002. Source: Gallo and Bastiani, Boletim Campineiro de Geografia v.7 n.2, 2017, https://publicacoes.agb.org.br/boletim-campineiro/article/download/2665/2017v7n2_FGallo_PBastiani/8496
- **Price:** outorga totals and 1998 toll values not found. (The TCU document I fetched, revista.tcu.gov.br RTCU 944, concerns federal DNER lots in MG/SP/PR/SC, not the state programme, so it is not used.)
- **BNDES:** not established.
- **Corruption [UNVERIFIED]:** snippets (Monitor Mercantil, BNamericas; both returned 403) say Ecovias admitted cartel and bribery in all 12 contracts from 1998 to 2015 and will pay R$ 650 mi to the MP-SP. A 2025 TCE-SP note says 21 monitored concessions total R$ 148.33 bi.
- **Counter-view:** extensions were granted as rebalancing after traffic and cost changes, which is the concessionaires' standard defense. The outorga-first rule in 11 of 12 tenders pushed value to the state up front and into tolls later (the paper's own reading).

## Patterns

1. **Same buyers, repeated.** Iberdrola (via Coelba, Celpe, Neoenergia/CEB), Enel (Coelce, Celg), Equatorial (CEEE-D, Celg-D), CPFL, Aegea and Itau/Bradesco recur across states.
2. **Federal money behind state sales.** PROES bonds of R$ 61.4 bi dwarfed bank sale proceeds (R$ 36.1 bi to SP alone versus about R$ 7.1 bi for Banespa), and BNDES financed buyers (Cemig, post-sale loans to distributors). The Union kept the risk.
3. **Single bidders and huge premiums coexist.** Cemig (one bidder), CEEE-D (one bidder, R$ 100,000), Corsan (one bidder, 1.15% agio) versus CEB (76.63% agio), Banespa (281%) and Cedae (103%-188%).
4. **Good assets sold dear, bad assets sold for nothing.** CEEE-T R$ 2.67 bi, CEEE-D R$ 100,000. Debts and pension liabilities decided the price more than the wires.
5. **Premiums were later renegotiated.** Cemar (PPL exit within two years), Cedae/Iguá (outorga dispute, rebalancing), Celg-D (resold at R$ 1.58 bi after paying R$ 2.187 bi), CRT (resold after 2 years).
6. **Courts mostly let auctions go ahead.** STJ lifted the CEB injunction; the Cemig agreement stayed contested for years.
7. **Service after sale is mixed.** Coelce and Corsan have investment claims by the operator; CEEE-D, Celg-D and Coelce show poor continuity rankings or tariff shocks. All claims here come from interested parties or rankings, so compare with ANEEL data before quoting.
8. **Where states sold too late, the price was liability relief.** CEEE-D, Celg and CEB were sold with caducidade risk in the background.
