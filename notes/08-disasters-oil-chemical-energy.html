Unconfirmed Eletronuclear denies it. Treat as a claim, not a fact.

FieldDetail

Angra 1: radioactive water leak, September–October 2022

Eletronuclear first told authorities that contaminated water had not reached the sea. It admitted the leak on 11 October 2022.

FieldDetail
Date / placeLate Sept – 11 Oct 2022, Angra dos Reis, RJ
OperatorEletronuclear (see note on Angra 2 above)
Deaths / people affectedNone reported in what I found.
EnvironmentFederal Police put the volume of "lesive material" at about 90 litres.
Fines / casesIbama fined Eletronuclear R$ 2.1 million for the leak and for delay in reporting it. Ibama said the company took about 4 months to admit the accident.

Sources: Revista Oeste, Ibama fine report · Movimento Econômico, Ibama on the 4-month delay · Revista Oeste, PF searches Angra 1

Amapá blackout, November 2020 (private transmission concession)

A transformer fire at the Macapá substation on 3 Nov 2020 led to a state-wide outage. ANEEL blamed repeated maintenance failures. The utility was private at the time.

FieldDetail
Date / place3 Nov 2020 onward, Amapá (about 22 days without full power, per one source)
Operator / ownershipLMTE (transmission line operator). Gemini Energy controls it through investment funds Starboard and Perfin, reported as holding 85% of the line. The asset was transferred to Gemini in early 2020. Concession award date not verified.
DeathsNot researched. The search returned nothing on hospital or oxygen deaths. This is a gap.
Fines / casesANEEL fined LMTE R$ 3.6 million (3.54% of prior-year revenue per one source; 3.45% per another). The company appealed. Amapá Procon fined LMTE and Gemini a combined R$ 450 thousand. An MPF lawsuit seeks R$ 70 billion in compensation (a claim, not a ruling). Defendants include the Union, ANEEL, ONS and others.

Sources: Poder360, ANEEL fine of R$ 3.6 million · Poder360, ANEEL on maintenance failures · Poder360, appeal and 22 days · Revista Oeste, Procon fines · Cenário Energia, MPF R$ 70 billion claim

Northeast Brazil oil spill, 2019 (origin: Venezuelan crude, cause disputed)

Oil was Venezuelan crude. The ship responsible was not confirmed.

FieldDetail
Date / placeAug–Nov 2019, coasts of 9 Northeast states
Operator / ownershipUnknown. Petrobras and the Navy matched the oil to Venezuelan crude. Ibama's president named ship-to-ship transfer at sea as the most likely cause. On 1 Nov 2019 the Navy and Federal Police named a Greek-flagged tanker as a suspect. The Federal Police searched firms linked to its owner.
EnvironmentOil reached at least 138 points across 9 states (as of the reporting date).
Deaths / people affectedNot confirmed in what I found. A fisher-death count was not found.
Fines / casesFederal Police inquiry. No final charge or fine found.

Sources: Brasilagro, Petrobras on Venezuelan oil mix · Eixos, Ibama on ship-to-ship transfer · BNews, investigation timeline · Aos Fatos, not from the sunken platform

Chevron, Frade field leak, November 2011 (Bacia de Campos)

Chevron operated the field under a concession. ANP and the Rio state government gave very different barrel counts. Fines and settlements ran from R$ 50 million to R$ 311 million.

FieldDetail
Date / placeNov 2011, Campo de Frade, Bacia de Campos, RJ
Operator / ownershipChevron (private, US). Partner: Petrobras (smaller stake, as reported in press; not verified here). Field granted under the 1999 concession round (not verified here).
Spill size (disputed)ANP: about 3.7 thousand barrels (its figure as of its report). Chevron: first 2.4 thousand, later revised to 1.2 thousand. Rio state government: about 25 thousand barrels. ANP later said at most about 3 thousand.
Deaths / people affectedNone reported in what I found.
Fines / settlementsIbama fine of R$ 50 million (its maximum under federal law, per press). ANP fine reported around R$ 40 million (one source). A Sept 2012 TRF-2 injunction suspended operations, with a R$ 500 million daily penalty. In Dec 2012 MPF and Chevron agreed a TAC of R$ 311 million. Another article gives a total of US$ 33.4 million in fines. That figure is not verified.

Sources: IHU/Unisinos, government fines · JB, environment minister on up to R$ 150 million · Olhar Direto, Chevron estimates · Vermelho, MPF R$ 311 million · Exame, same TAC

Ultracargo tank farm fire, Santos, April 2015

An eight-day fire in fuel tanks at the Alemoa terminal. It was described as one of Brazil's largest environmental disasters. Four years later a first civil deal was reached.

FieldDetail
Date / place2–10 Apr 2015, Vila Alemoa, Santos, SP
Operator / ownershipUltracargo (private terminal operator). Status at the time: private (per press; not verified here).
DeathsNone found in the sources I read.
EnvironmentA study cites about 9 tonnes of dead fish (figure from a local fisher study; not independently verified).
Fines / casesSão Paulo state prosecutors opened a civil inquiry. The portaria cited "serious reasons" for failure in the emergency plan. MPF reportedly traced the start of the fire to an operational error. An initial civil agreement of R$ 67.3 million was reached (about 4 years later). The insurance cap for fire and explosion was reported as R$ 550 million (Valor, via Legismap).

Sources: UEPG, "Desastre da Ultracargo e a reparação inter partes" · Gazeta SP, R$ 67.3 million deal · Legismap, insurance cap · Diário de Pernambuco, impacts

FPSO Cidade de São Mateus explosion, February 2015 (Camarupim field)

An explosion on a floating production vessel killed workers. The death toll rose over several days.

FieldDetail
Date / place11 Feb 2015, about 120 km off Espírito Santo
Operator / ownershipBW Offshore owned the vessel. Petrobras chartered it and ran the Camarupim fields (Petrobras 100%, per one source). Ownership is private (BW Offshore) for the vessel. Petrobras is state-controlled.

2001 power rationing ("apagão"), July 2001 – February 2002

Rationing was imposed on the country. The GDP cost estimates differ widely by method.

FieldDetail
Date / place1 Jul 2001 – 19 Feb 2002, nationwide
Operator / ownershipMixed. Generation and distribution were largely privatized by then; ANEEL regulated. Exact split not verified.
Deaths / social damageNot researched.
Costs (disputed)Target reduction: about 20% of consumption. Penalty on the bill: 50%–200% for missing the target. GDP growth fell from 4.4% (2000) to 1.4% (2001). A FGV estimate cited about 2% of GDP (R$ 60 billion). A TCU audit estimated losses of R$ 45.2 billion, mostly passed to consumers.

Sources: Tribuna do Sertão (rationing dates) · Suno, rationing context · Instituto Acende Brasil (private-sector losses study) · The TCU audit and FGV estimates are cited second-hand; check the TCU report directly.

P-36 platform sinking, March 2001 (Campos Basin)

The platform was a semi-submersible at the Roncador field. Eleven workers died. The company, Petrobras, is state-controlled.

FieldDetail
Date / placeMarch 2001 (most sources; one says 15 Mar with two explosions). Campos Basin, Roncador field.
Operator / ownershipPetrobras (state-controlled). Conversion and contractor work: Maritima was responsible (press, not verified).
Deaths11, all from the emergency team (per several sources; Brazilian Supreme Court (STJ) also records 11).
CausesTribunal Marítimo saw signs of error in the conversion project and management failures. ANP and Navy reports described a chain of operating, design and maintenance failures.
Fines / casesSTJ upheld an Ibama fine of R$ 5 million for the oil spill. Petrobras has contested Ibama and MPF penalties in court. In 2017 Petrobras estimated a possible cost of R$ 1.469 billion for the P-36 incident (per Eixos; check the company filing).

Sources: ConJur, "Tragédia histórica" · Eixos, P-36 penalties · Offshore Magazine, engineering council blames Petrobras · Folha Vitória (note: its dates conflict)

REPAR (Araucária, PR) oil spill into the Iguaçu, July 2000

The spill reached the Barigui and Iguaçu rivers. Court-ordered payments are still tied up in a dispute over use. Amounts conflict across sources. Treat every number as disputed.

FieldDetail
Date / place16 Jul 2000, Araucária, PR (Rio Iguaçu, Barigui)
Operator / ownershipPetrobras (state-controlled), REPAR refinery
Spill sizeAbout 4 million litres of crude (press). Leak ran about 2 hours before it was noticed. Petrobras announced it the next day (per press).
Deaths / people affectedNot researched.
Fines / settlements (disputed)Federal court ordered a fine of about R$ 610 million (one source; another says about R$ 1.4 billion total with cleanup). Petrobras appealed. A court-approved settlement set aside R$ 120 million for environmental projects. About R$ 900 million was reported as deposited but stalled over how it is used. A 2013 study found petroleum in river sediments 13 years later.

Sources: Tribuna PR, spill anniversary · CUT, 20 years later · Band Paraná, funds undecided after 24 years · Gazeta do Povo, R$ 120 million · UTFPR study

REDUC (Duque de Caxias) pipeline rupture, Guanabara Bay, January 2000

A fatigue fracture on a pipeline from the REDUC refinery to the Ilha d'Água terminal spilled about 1.3 million litres into the bay. Compensation for fishers is still disputed.

FieldDetail
Date / place18 Jan 2000, Guanabara Bay, RJ
Operator / ownershipPetrobras (state-controlled)
Spill sizeAbout 1.3 million litres of fuel oil. Slick reportedly spread over about 40 km² (one source).
Cause (disputed)ANP: fatigue fracture from thermal expansion and loss of ground support. A COPPE/UFRJ report blamed Petrobras negligence.
Fines / settlements (disputed)Fine reported as R$ 35 million to Ibama plus R$ 15 million for bay restoration (one source). Another source says R$ 50 million (Petrobras was then the only company to get the maximum fine). A Commitment Term (TAC) with MPF was signed Nov 2000. In 2016 about 20 thousand fishers had still not been compensated (JB). A court ruled Petrobras must pay the Colônia Z-12 fishers.

Sources: Wikipedia, 2000 Guanabara Bay oil spill · JB, fishers not compensated after 16 years · Migalhas, Colônia Z-12 ruling · OGJ, Petrobras faces big tab

FieldDetail
Date / place13 Sep 1987, Goiânia, GO
Operator / ownershipPublic body / clinic (abandoned radiotherapy institute). Owner status not researched.

Environment / wasteAbout 6,000 tonnes of waste stored at Abadia de Goiás (per Uninter / BBC summary). Source capsule about 50.9 TBq.

Vila Socó fire, Cubatão, February 1984 (Petrobras pipeline)

About 700,000 litres of gasoline leaked from a Petrobras pipeline under stilt houses. It ignited. The official count is 93. The Public Prosecutor's office estimated 508. Other estimates reach about 700.

FieldDetail
Date / place24 Feb 1984, Vila Socó, Cubatão, SP
Operator / ownershipPetrobras (state-controlled), pipeline
Deaths (disputed)93 official. 508 per MPSP. Up to ~700 in other estimates (apublica.org). The true count is not settled.
People affectedAbout 6,000 residents lived in the stilt houses (per Alesp / Gazeta SP)
Warnings ignoredA Petrobras engineer had warned of fire risk and asked for evacuation (per Agência Pública).
Fines / casesNo one was held responsible by Brazilian courts (per Agência Pública). Case raised before the Comissão da Verdade (2014) and a possible petition to the Inter-American Court.

Sources: Alesp, Comissão da Verdade on Vila Socó · Gazeta SP, 40 years · Agência Pública · Agência Brasil, Inter-American Court

Cubatão "Valley of Death" pollution, 1970s–1980s

Cubatão had a steel plant, refineries and chemical plants. Press called it the most polluted city in the world in the 1980s. Specific health counts are weakly sourced.

FieldDetail
Date / place1970s–1980s, Cubatão, SP
OperatorsCosipa (steel, state-linked), Refinaria Presidente Bernardes (Petrobras), Rhodia and others. Ownership status of each at the time: not verified.
Deaths / health (weak)A municipal opinion piece cites 37 stillbirths out of about 1,800 births (Oct 1981–Apr 1982). This is not a technical study. Treat as a claim. Anencephaly cases are also reported without data.
ResponseThe state's pollution control programme (Montoro government) followed the 1984 disaster. The UN recognised recovery in 1992 (per one source).

Sources: Economic News Brasil, Cubatão today · Mongabay, "Poisoned city" · SESC-SP, environmental liabilities

Rhodia Cubatão and Samaritá waste (1976–1995)

Hazardous chemical waste was stored and dumped. Plants were closed in 1993. A 1995 settlement set obligations. About 33 thousand tonnes remained stored in 2005.

FieldDetail
Date / placeWaste from ~1976 onward; case public 1984; plant closed 1993; settlement 1995. Cubatão, SP, and Samaritá (São Vicente, SP).
Operator / ownershipRhodia (French chemical group, private). Bought the Clorogil operation in 1976, which had inherited clandestine dumps (per one study).
ContaminantsHexachlorobenzene, sodium pentachlorophenate
WasteAbout 33 thousand tonnes held at Samaritá (per 2005 report)
Fines / casesFactory and incinerator shut 1993. Settlement 1995 in Cubatão civil case no. 0000269-06.1993.8.26.0157. In 2004 a planned transfer of the waste to Bahia was blocked by a Bahia court. A 2024 São Paulo state bill asked about discontinued blood tests for hexachlorobenzene.

Sources: Christian Science Monitor, 1992 · SESC-SP, 2005 article · Bahia Notícias, waste transfer · ALESP bill, 2024 · USP master's thesis (Mesquita, 1994)

Cidade dos Meninos HCH contamination, Duque de Caxias, RJ

Pesticide waste from a 1950s–60s HCH factory was dumped in a residential area. It was rediscovered in 1989. Courts have ruled on compensation, but the outcome varies by case.

FieldDetail
Date / placeFactory operated 1950–1965 (per one source). Rediscovered 1989. Cidade dos Meninos, Duque de Caxias, RJ
Operator / ownershipA malaria institute of the former Ministry of Education and Health ran the factory, per one source. The land belongs to the Union. The Rhodia or Pesticidas link asked about was not found in any source I read.
People affectedResidents exposed to HCH; numbers not found.
CasesTRF-2 ruled on material and moral damage in one case: damages proven only with blood contamination evidence. One resident won R$ 100 thousand on appeal; the Ministry of Health appealed. A bill to make the Union pay families was mentioned.

Sources: UFPR, "Exposição a riscos químicos ... HCH na Cidade dos Meninos" · TRF-2 ruling 2006.51.01.012213-8 · Cadernos de Saúde Pública (2008)

Shell and Basf, Paulínia (SP): groundwater contamination, date not confirmed

Pesticide plant workers were poisoned, and groundwater was contaminated. Labour courts ordered treatment. A 2013 collective settlement reached R$ 200 million.

FieldDetail
Date / placePlant operated decades before 2007 (exact dates not confirmed). Paulínia, SP.
Operator / ownershipShell and Basf (private multinationals). Ownership changes in the 1990s sale are mentioned in the press.
ContaminantsAldrin, endrin, dieldrin (in soil and groundwater; Shell admitted)
People affected1,068 workers recognised for health care (per the 2013 agreement)

Sources: TRT-15, April 2011 ruling · TRT-15, TST agreement · TRT-15, R$ 200 million · TRT-15, 2019 seminar

Patterns (preliminary)

  1. Ownership. State-run Petrobras appears in the most cases (Socó, REDUC, REPAR, P-36, the 2015 FPSO charter). Private firms show up too (Chevron, Rhodia, Shell/Basf, Ultracargo, Gemini/LMTE).
  2. Warnings ignored. Socó (engineer's warning), P-36 (design and maintenance failures), Amapá (repeated maintenance failures), Angra 1 (delayed disclosure).
  3. Regulator capacity. Many cases show slow disclosure, long appeals, and fines paid years later. Compensation is often still open (REPAR, REDUC).
  4. Who paid. Workers and residents paid the health cost first. Consumers paid the energy-sector costs (2001 rationing). Fines and settlements were often contested for a decade or more.
