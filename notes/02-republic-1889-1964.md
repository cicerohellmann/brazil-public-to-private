# Old Republic through Populist Republic, 1889-1964

Method note: researched with web search plus page fetches. Many Wikipedia pages were thin on terms; figures below are only those a fetched or searched source stated. "Not found" means I did not find it, not that it does not exist. Where sources differ, both are given. Several search-tool summaries were read as snippets, not full papers; flagged "(snippet)".

## 1. Bolivian Syndicate lease of Acre (1901-1903)
- **Years / government:** lease signed 11 June 1901 by Bolivia; undone under Rodrigues Alves (Brazil) in 1903.
- **Transferred:** Bolivia leased the Acre region (rubber territory, about 191,000 km2 ceded later to Brazil) to a New York/Jersey City company for 30 years, "almost total control".
- **Who:** the Bolivian Syndicate; shareholders reportedly included the King of Belgium and relatives of US President McKinley.
- **Price/terms:** Lease tax/customs/police terms not found. Brazil ended it by paying the Syndicate GBP 110,000 (Wikipedia) or GBP 114,000 (InfoEscola, distrato 26 Feb 1903): sources disagree.
- **Legal mechanism:** foreign-state lease/charter; later negotiated buy-out and Treaty of Petropolis (17 Nov 1903): GBP 2 million to Bolivia, territory swap, and promise to build Madeira-Mamore railway (see case 2).
- **Costs borne:** Treasury (indemnity plus GBP 2m plus railway); rubber tappers in the Acre War.
- **After:** Acre became Brazilian; the railway promise created case 2.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_War ; https://portalamazonia.com/amazonia/brasil-x-bolivia-saiba-o-que-foi-o-tratado-de-petropolis/ ; https://www.infoescola.com/historia/tratado-de-petropolis/

## 2. Madeira-Mamore Railway (1907-1912, abandoned 1972)
- **Government:** Afonso Pena / Nilo Pecanha era; contract tied to the 1903 treaty.
- **Transferred:** construction and operation of the rail link around the Madeira rapids to Farquhar's group (third attempt; Collins 1878 built only ~7 km).
- **Who:** Percival Farquhar (US financier). Construction began 1907; line completed 30 April 1912 (Wikipedia) / 15 July 1912 (another source): sources differ.
- **Terms:** concession financing terms (cost per km, interest guarantee) not found. Farquhar's businesses are described as living on "government concessions and privileges, including guarantees" of returns (Wikipedia, weakly sourced).
- **Costs borne:** workers. Death toll uncertain: 3,500-7,000 (Brown Univ. source), 6,000 (Brazilian government), 7,000-10,000 (Uys), over 30,000 (Picoli).
- **After:** rubber boom collapsed (Asian plantations), line redundant; abandoned after BR-364 in 1972.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira-Mamor%C3%A9_Railroad ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percival_Farquhar ; Brown Univ. Brasiliana page via search: https://library.brown.edu/create/brasiliana/?p=169

## 3. Brazil Railway Company / Farquhar syndicate (1906-1917, again 1920s)
- **Years:** founded 9 Nov 1906; bankrupt by October 1914 (WWI financing cut); control lasted to about mid-1917.
- **Transferred:** control of about 11,000 km of the national network's 23,400 km (about 47%) within ten years, plus Sorocabana, Paulista, Mogiana, Madeira-Mamore, Sao Paulo-Rio Grande, Vitoria-Minas lines, tram services in Salvador, Sao Paulo, Belem, Rio Grande, ports, Rio Light interests, telephones, lumber, colonization, meatpacking, hotels.
- **Who:** Percival Farquhar, with Belgian/French/British capital.
- **Terms:** government interest/revenue guarantees; "often through personal bribery" (Wikipedia on Farquhar; weak, cites Carone). Guarantee percentages not found.
- **Costs borne:** treasury (guarantees), investors, Indigenous peoples and settlers (Wikipedia says "thousands" of Indigenous deaths, no figures given).
- **After:** bankruptcy 1914-17; partial recovery after the war; fail again after 1929; Vargas restricted his activities after 1930 and he left Brazil.
- **Sources:** https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_Railway_Company ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percival_Farquhar

## 4. Sao Paulo-Rio Grande railway land strip, Lumber company, Contestado (1908-1916)
- **Government:** Afonso Pena (line commissioned 1908); Hermes da Fonseca (army campaign).
- **Transferred:** concession included a strip 15 km on each side of the railway (about 30 km wide); associated Southern Brazil Lumber & Colonization Co. held rights to extract timber and resell the land. Total hectares: not found in sources I read.
- **Who:** Brazil Railway Co. (Farquhar, North American).
- **Costs borne:** subsistence farmers (posseiros) expelled; construction workers dismissed once the railway finished, many joined the rebel movement of monk Jose Maria. Contestado War Oct 1912-Aug 1916: 10,000-20,000 total deaths per Wikipedia infobox (5,000-8,000 rebel dead/wounded/missing; 800-1,000 government casualties).
- **After:** land fights; Lumber logged the araucaria forest of the region (environmental cost, no figures found).
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contestado_War ; https://idents.paginas.ufsc.br/2020/04/15/the-contestado-war-brazils-biggest-civil-war/ (snippet)

## 5. Light: Sao Paulo Tramway, Light and Power / Rio Light (1899-1979)
- **Years:** SPTL&P founded 7 Apr 1899 in Toronto by William Mackenzie and F.S. Pearson; first tram line 1900; Santana de Parnaiba plant opened 1905. Rio Light founded 1904 Toronto, authorised 1905; both under Brazilian Traction (from 1912).
- **Transferred:** monopoly-type concessions for urban power and trams in the two biggest cities. Duration/tariff terms (gold clause etc.) not found in sources read.
- **Who:** Canadian (with US/European) capital; later Brascan/Brookfield.
- **Costs borne:** consumers; flood victims (see case 6).
- **After:** tramways bought by Companhia Municipal de Transportes Coletivos 1946 (Wikipedia); Brazilian assets passed to Brazilian ownership 1979 (purchase price not found); Eletropaulo created 1981. Later privatized again (1996, outside this era).
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_Tramway,_Light_and_Power_Company ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_S.A.

## 6. Light's Billings project: Pinheiros reversal and Cubatao (1927-1950s)
- **Government:** 1927 state/federal contract (Washington Luis era).
- **Transferred:** right to divert Tiete surplus flow and reverse the Pinheiros river to feed Henry Borden plant at Cubatao, plus right to expropriate wetlands, with buy-back priority for former owners.
- **Costs borne:** Feb 1929 flooding caused 38 deaths; company marked 20,779,443 m2 for expropriation; through side agreements only 10% of expropriated properties returned to original owners; never formally indicted (Wikipedia). Pollution: reversal ran until 1992, suspended because of pollution.
- **After:** Billings reservoir became a metropolitan water source; polluted. Water rights questions persist.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_Tramway,_Light_and_Power_Company ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billings_Reservoir ; https://www.dgabc.com.br/Noticia/4212771/criada-para-gerar-energia-represa-billings-hoje-fornece-agua-para-a-regiao

## 7. Coffee valorization (1906, repeated 1917, 1921, permanent 1924) and Taubate Agreement
- **Government:** signed by Sao Paulo, Minas, Rio governors Feb 1906 (SP law 990, 4 Jun 1906); federal backing 1907 under Afonso Pena; 1924 Coffee Institute of Sao Paulo.
- **Transferred:** public credit and later the national debt to buy surplus coffee for planters.
- **Who:** planters; foreign banks and traders (Crossman & Sielcken NY; Rothschild contract 28 Jan 1908).
- **Terms:** foreign loans repaid from a new gold-payable export levy per bag. Bags purchased, loan sizes: not found (my searched claims of 8m bags / GBP 15m were NOT confirmed).
- **Costs borne:** treasury and consumers; prices stayed artificially high, production kept growing; after 1929 crash Vargas government assumed SP's foreign debts (1930).
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taubat%C3%A9_Agreement ; https://www.al.sp.gov.br/repositorio/legislacao/lei/1906/lei-990-04.06.1906.html ; https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1906p1/d101

## 8. Mate Laranjeira lease, Mato Grosso (1882-1943; republican period 1889-1943)
- **Transferred:** lease of public (devolved) land for yerba mate; estimates over 5,000,000 ha, or 5,400,000 ha (Barros 1996), about 2,000,000 ha actually worked: sources differ. Legal monopoly of native mate extraction for ~50 years.
- **Who:** Thomaz Laranjeira (1882), after 1902 a Buenos Aires firm with foreign capital (Francisco Mendes & Cia).
- **Costs borne:** workers (mostly Paraguayan, debt-peonage claims common in the literature but not verified here); Indigenous Guarani/Kaiowa lands (not sourced here).
- **After:** monopoly ended 1943; Colonia Agricola Nacional de Dourados created (Vargas); details not confirmed.
- **Sources:** academic papers via search (snippets): https://periodicos.ufms.br/index.php/AlbRHis/article/download/4029/3214 ; https://ojs.ufgd.edu.br/FRONTEIRAS/article/download/4549/2328/13983

## 9. Transfer of public lands to the states, Constitution of 1891
- **Transferred:** Art. 64 moved ownership of "terras devolutas" from the Union to the states; Union kept frontier, fortification and federal rail lands.
- **Who gained:** state oligarchies, land speculators; later colonization and railway companies bought or got grants from states (case 11).
- **Costs borne:** posseiros, Indigenous peoples; grilagem (forged titles) via notaries, surveyors and judges.
- **Sources:** https://www2.senado.leg.br/bdsf/bitstream/handle/id/602494/Regularizacao_fundiaria_experiencias_regionais.pdf ; https://revistaperseu.fpabramo.org.br/index.php/revista-perseu/article/download/303/250 (snippets)

## 10. Companhia Docas de Santos port concession (1888/1890-1980)
- **Transferred:** build and operate Brazil's main coffee port with monopoly over the organised port.
- **Who:** Gaffree and Guinle (Eduardo Guinle family).
- **Terms:** concession won in 1888 tender; term signed 7 Nov 1890; length reported as 90 or 92 years. Ended 7 Nov 1980, administration to CODESP (federal). Tariffs/profit: not found. I found no source confirming an extension.
- **After:** company repressive toward workers after 1964 (reported).
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Palassin_Guinle ; https://setcesp.org.br/noticias/porto-de-santos-celebra-124-anos-mas-com-5-seculos-de-historia/ (snippets)

## 11. Companhia de Terras Norte do Paran (1925-1944/51)
- **Transferred:** state land in northern Parana/Sao Paulo state sales; about 515,000 alqueires paulistas (one source, hectares not computed); bought Sao Paulo-Parana railway project 1928.
- **Who:** British Paranaa Plantations / Brazil Plantations Syndicate (Lord Lovat, 1924); founded Londrina.
- **After:** British left 1944; Brazilian shareholders; renamed Melhoramentos Norte do Parana 1951. Costs to existing posseiros/Indigenous (Kaingang): not sourced here.
- **Sources:** https://www.canninghouse.org/canning-insights/the-british-in-parana ; https://repositorio.londrina.pr.gov.br/index.php/ippul/eivs-digitais/23107-113-eiv/file (snippets)

## 12. Fordlandia and Belterra (1927-1945)
- **Government:** Para state (Governor Dionisio Bentes; approval 20 or 30 Sept 1927, sources differ); Ford sold to federal government under Dutra era, Dec 1945.
- **Transferred:** Henry Ford: area of about 10,100 km2 (about 1m ha; The Henry Ford says 1m ha, a municipal source cites 14,568 km2: sources differ). Granted free; export-tax exemption; Ford to pay 7% of profits to government and 2% to municipalities.
- **Price:** sold 1945 for US$250,000 (several sources) after ~US$20 million invested ("loss of over US$20m"). One source claims commission to an intermediary US$125,000 (Grandin via UAI).
- **Costs borne:** workers recruited from the Northeast; plantation failure (blight); Ford bore the financial loss. Indigenous land impact not sourced.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordl%C3%A2ndia ; https://www.thehenryford.org/explore/blog/ford-rubber-plantations-in-brazil ; https://www.uai.com.br/?p=157297 (snippet)

## 13. Itabira Iron Ore Co. (Farquhar) and its end: Vale do Rio Doce (1911-1942)
- **Years:** Farquhar bought the Brazilian Hematite Syndicate in 1911, renamed it Itabira Iron Ore Co.; contract of 1920 signed for the government by Epitacio Pessoa; opposed by Artur Bernardes (Minas); Farquhar worked on the project 1919-1941.
- **Transferred (sought):** Itabira ore deposits, railway to a port near Vitoria, export rights. Financing: Cr$30,000 per km at 6% gold interest (footnote in Rogers 1959; read second-hand, ambiguous).
- **After:** Vitoria-Minas line linked 1936; ore first exported 1940 (5,740 t on the Modesta); Decree-law 4,352 (1 Jun 1942) created CVRD, capitalised Cr$200,000,000 (Rogers 1959 footnotes). Company "Brazilianized" 1930s into Companhia Brasileira de Mineracao e Siderurgia. Compensation to Itabira holders: not found.
- **Context:** CVRD created in a state-led counter-move; privatized in 1997 (later era).
- **Sources:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-inter-american-studies/article/brazils-rio-doce-valley-project/926AD0E9227DFA3A539C0727DB8FB498 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companhia_Vale_do_Rio_Doce ; https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/92489 (not readable)

## 14. Hanna Mining and the Minas iron-ore fight (1957-1965)
- **Years / governments:** JK decree 49,889 (12 Jan 1961 per Senate text; date inconsistent) authorising Mineracao Hanna do Brasil to mine iron at Nova Lima, MG; Janio Quadros annulled it 21 Aug 1961 and returned deposits to the national reserve; Quadros resigned 25 Aug. Goulart tried to revoke again (Wikipedia pt).
- **Who:** M.A. Hanna Co. (Cleveland), George Humphrey (director, ex-US Treasury Secretary); 1957 bought British St John del Rey Mining shares for US$6 million.
- **After:** after the 1964 coup, MBR formed 1965 by CAEMI (Antunes group) with Hanna (Hanna held 34% in one report); second biggest Brazilian producer since 1971. Vale bought Caemi; Caemi ended Dec 2006. Claims that Hanna fed the coup are in Wikipedia pt only; treat as contested.
- **Sources:** https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanna_Mining_Company ; https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/472471/publicacao/15661805 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caemi ; https://search.library.wisc.edu/digital/AC7YH2GVPR5IDL8M/text/AFJS6L7ISYQNCM8F

## 15. ICOMI manganese, Serra do Navio, Amapa (1945/47-1997)
- **Government:** deposits declared national reserve 1945; concession 1947 (Dutra); 50-year mining contract.
- **Who:** ICOMI (Augusto Antunes group) with Bethlehem Steel at 49% (UERJ article, citing a critique).
- **Terms:** royalty rate and reserve estimates not found. Production 1957 to 1997 (SGB) or 1998 (HCS-Manguinhos): sources differ. Ore hauled 192 km by rail to Santana port.
- **Costs borne:** arsenic: ore 0.17-0.18% As; tailings in lagoon released arsenic; tailings used as street fill in Santana. Academic summary: limited regional development.
- **Sources:** https://periodicos.ufpa.br/index.php/pnaea/article/download/11942/8259 ; https://sgb.gov.br/documents/d/guest/scarpelli_arsenioemmineriomanganes-pdf ; https://doaj.org/article/53c2d7e185c24575b5c5556e6e5014c1 ; https://www.e-publicacoes.uerj.br/maracanan/article/download/83950/56271/366185 (fetch failed, snippet only)

## 16. Counter-move: Amforp/ITT takeovers by Brizola and the 1964 Eletrobras buy (1959-1964)
- **1959:** Rio Grande do Sul governor Brizola took over Bond and Share's CEERG (13 May 1959) after the company's monopoly over Porto Alegre power; **1962:** decree 13,186 cancelled ITT's Companhia Telefonica Nacional concession.
- **1964:** AMFORP sold its ten Brazilian subsidiaries plus CAEEB to Eletrobras (Nov 1964, World Bank text). Price: not confirmed (my US$135m figure was NOT found in any source).
- **Compensation:** one account says CEERG was valued at Cr$1.35 billion then reduced to Cr$70 million; the same source claims US$470m taken: uncorroborated.
- **Sources:** https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/360731468004223801/text/multi0page.txt ; https://www.extraclasse.org.br/?p=12281 ; https://www.mobiletime.com.br/museu-movel/15/12/2023/operadoras-do-passado-crt/

## 17. Counter-moves (context; later privatized)
- **CSN, 1941:** state company created 9 Apr 1941 under Vargas after a US-Brazil agreement; Volta Redonda began production 1946; 91% stake sold in 1993-94 privatization. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companhia_Sider%C3%BArgica_Nacional
- **Vale, 1942:** see case 13; privatized 1997 (later era).
- **Petrobras, 3 Oct 1953:** Vargas; legal monopoly on oil; production only about 2,700 bpd in 1953; monopoly ended by Law 9.478 of 1997. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrobras
- **Eletrobras, 11 Jun 1962:** under Goulart; first CEO Paulo Richer. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eletrobras

## Patterns
1. **Concessions bundled land and monopoly with risk shifted to the public:** interest or revenue guarantees, land strips (15 km each side of rail), export-tax exemptions, and later public buy-outs or debt assumption when ventures failed (Brazil Railway, Light's expropriations, coffee loans taken over 1930, Ford sold at a fraction of cost).
2. **Infrastructure for extraction:** railway/port/power concessions served raw-material exports (rubber, coffee, ore, manganese); when the commodity boom ended the asset was abandoned or the state took it over (Madeira-Mamore, Itabira to Vale).
3. **Costs fell on the weakest parties:** workers (Madeira-Mamore deaths, debt labour), settlers and Indigenous peoples (Contestado, mate lands, Norte do Parana), consumers/treasury, and environment (arsenic at Santana, Pinheiros pollution).
4. **Nationalist backlash cycles:** reforms and counter-moves (Bernardes veto, Vargas 1941-43, Petrobras, Brizola 1959-62, Janio/Goulart on Hanna) provoked foreign pressure and ended in or near 1964.
5. **Foreign capital often arrived via small fixer/intermediary networks** (Farquhar, Guinle, Laranjeira) with documented political access; bribery claims are weakly sourced here.
