## ANP concession rounds 5 to 13 (2003-2015)

- **Years:** Aug 2003 (R5) to Oct 2015 (R13). Lula, then Dilma.
- **What was transferred:** exploration and production rights over offshore and onshore blocks (Union-owned subsoil), by concession: the winner owns the oil it produces and pays royalties and special participation. Scale per round below.
- **Who got it:** Petrobras plus foreign majors and, from R9 onward, many small Brazilian firms. Winners by name are documented only for R7 (Petrobras, Petrogal, Devon, ENI, Shell, Koch, Repsol YPF, Statoil, Synergy, Partex, Amerada Hess, Starfish, others; Y2006). Financing: not documented in the sources opened.
- **Price vs value:** signing bonus is the only up-front price. Nobody valued the blocks in the sources opened; ANP reports bonus, minimum exploration programme (PEM) and local-content (CL) offers only. The 2013 R11 bonus came after the pre-salt discoveries, in a year when Libra was priced at R$ 15 bn for one block (see Libra below).
- **Legal mechanism:** Lei 9.478/1997 concession model; from R5 the bonus, PEM (in work units, UT) and CL offer were scored together, and ANP set minimum CL: R5 deep water 30%, shallow 50% exploration and 60% development, onshore 70% (Y2004). R6 minimum 30% to 70% by operator class A/B/C (Y2005). R12 weighting: 40% bonus, 40% PEM, 20% CL (Y2014).

- **Who bore costs / what happened after:** Union gave up subsoil rights for bonuses of R$ 27 m (R5) to R$ 2.48 bn (R11); R8 was stopped by court action. No rounds were held 2009-2012 (Y2011: "ANP nao realizou Rodadas" in 2010).
- **Counter-view:** the R5-R7 design (PEM and CL scored in the bid) was ANP's stated way to raise exploration and local supply ([Y2004](https://www.gov.br/anp/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/anuario-estatistico/arquivos-anuario-estatistico-2004/textos/anuario-2004-texto_s5.pdf)); the low early bonuses reflect frontier-risk pricing before the pre-salt was known [UNVERIFIED, interpretation].
- **Not found:** local-content waivers granted per round, and winner lists for R5, R6, R9-R13.

## Airport concessions - additions (Galeão and Confins)

- **Years:** auction 22 Nov 2013 (Dilma); Galeão returned Feb 2022; relicitated 30 Mar 2026.
- **What was transferred:** operation of Rio Galeão and Belo Horizonte Confins airports, in a concession with 49% held by Infraero in Galeão.
- **Who got it:**
  - Galeão: Odebrecht Transport-led consortium with Changi (Singapore). Bid R$ 19.018 bn, ágio 293.9%. Confins: AeroBrasil led by CCR (75%), Flughafen Zurich (24%), Munich (1%). Bid R$ 1.82 bn, ágio 66%. Combined R$ 20.839 bn ([Estado de Minas, 22 Nov 2013](https://www.em.com.br/app/noticia/economia/2013/11/22/internas_economia,472718/dilma-comemora-resultado-de-leilao-de-aeroportos.shtml)).
  - Financing: not established in the pages opened [UNVERIFIED].
- **Price vs value:** minimum bids were revised before the auction. Exame reports Galeão minimum outorga raised from R$ 4.65 bn to R$ 4.729 bn and Confins lowered from R$ 1.56 bn to R$ 994 m, with investment requirements R$ 5.8 bn (Galeão, 25 years) and R$ 3.6 bn (Confins, 30 years) ([Exame](https://exame.com/economia/governo-atualiza-valores-para-concessao-de-galeao-e-confins-2/)). The ágio figures imply a Galeão minimum near R$ 4.83 bn (19.018 / 3.939), which matches a search-summary figure of R$ 4.828 bn [UNVERIFIED]; the Exame numbers and the ágio math do not agree, so the true edital minimum is uncertain. Confins ágio 66% implies about R$ 1.10 bn, not R$ 994 m.
- **Legal mechanism:** federal concession (ANAC); outorga fixed plus variable contribution; Lei 13.448/2017 relicitation framework later used for Galeão.
- **What happened after (Galeão):**
  - Changi bought out Odebrecht in 2017 and held 51% with Infraero 49%. On 10 Feb 2022 it formally asked ANAC to return the concession, citing pandemic losses and demand below the 2013 forecast. It had claimed an R$ 7.5 bn imbalance, to be deducted from monthly outorgas over 19 years. ANAC denied the rebalancing about a week earlier ([Poder360, 10 Feb 2022](https://www.poder360.com.br/economia/aeroporto-do-galeao-sera-relicitado-com-santos-dumont/)).
  - ANAC's rapporteur said demand risk belongs to the concessionaire. Changi would be compensated for termination in proportion to its stake (same page).
  - A repactuação auction on 30 Mar 2026 gave Galeão to Aena with a R$ 2.9 bn bid (ágio 210.88% on a R$ 932 m minimum), against Zurich and the incumbent RioGaleão (Changi and Vinci); the contract requires a variable contribution of 20% of gross revenue until 2039 ([Mercado e Consumo, 30 Mar 2026](https://mercadoeconsumo.com.br/30/03/2026/economia/aena-arremata-aeroporto-do-galeao-em-leilao-por-r-29-bi/?noamp=mobile)). One search headline read "R$ 29 bi" but the body says R$ 2.9 bn, which is the figure used here.
  - The 2013 bid (R$ 19 bn) and the 2026 bid (R$ 2.9 bn) are not directly comparable (different structures, 13 years of operation, repactuação), but they show how far the original price was from what bidders would pay later.
- **What happened after (Confins):** not returned. In Nov 2022 ANAC (Decision 569, 16 Nov 2022) approved an extraordinary review recomposing R$ 60,721,357.66 in favour of BH Airport for COVID losses, applied as discounts on its outorga payments ([Motiva RI](https://ri.motiva.com.br/en/noticia/materal-fact-extraordinary-review-and-amendment-to-the-concession-agreement-of-the-bh-airport-2/)).
- **Who bore costs:** public side: forgone outorga (the R$ 7.5 bn claim; the R$ 60.7 m recomposition) and Infraero's 49% exposure. Private: Odebrecht exited in 2017; Changi's equity, size unknown [UNVERIFIED].
- **Counter-view:** ANAC held the demand shortfall was the concessionaire's risk and refused the Galeão rebalancing (Poder360 above); it did grant a smaller one to Confins.
- **Not found:** actual indemnity paid to Changi/Odebrecht; Confins operating returns.

## Madeira dams (Santo Antonio and Jirau) - additions

- **Years:** auctions 10 Dec 2007 (Santo Antonio) and 19 May 2008 (Jirau, [UNVERIFIED]); suits 2014 and 2025. Lula.
- **Santo Antonio auction terms:** Madeira Energia consortium won at R$ 78.87/MWh ([Congresso em Foco, 10 Dec 2007](https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/1538/furnas-e-odebrecht-vencem-leilao-de-usina-do-madeira)); Exame gives R$ 78.90, a ceiling of R$ 122/MWh (35% below), 16.1% below the runner-up (Camargo Corrêa consortium with CPFL, Endesa and Chesf at R$ 94; Suez-Eletrosul R$ 98) ([Exame](https://exame.com/negocios/odebrecht-cobrou-16-menos-que-2o-colocado-no-leilao-do-rio-madeira-m0145979); the page's own date reads 10 Oct 2010 but it describes the 2007 auction, so the date is treated as wrong). Capacity 2,218 MW (3,150.4 MW potential), investment estimated about R$ 9.5 bn.
- **Who got it:** Furnas 39%, a Banif/Santander fund 20%, Odebrecht Investimentos 17.6%, Andrade Gutierrez 12.4%, Cemig 10%, CNO 1% (same sources). Furnas is state-owned, so the public share was 49% with Cemig.
- **Price vs value:** the 35% deságio came with an ANEEL ceiling; Exame notes the winner bid 16% below the second bidder. No independent valuation found. Counter-view in the sources: low tariff won the consumer a cheaper energy price; later cost overruns (not quantified here) show the bid may have been unrealistic [UNVERIFIED].
- **Corruption allegation:** Odebrecht and Andrade Gutierrez allegedly paid about R$ 80 m in bribes tied to Santo Antonio, to PMDB, PSDB and PP politicians (Eduardo Cunha about R$ 20 m, Edison Lobão R$ 5.5 m, others), per plea-deal testimony reported by Estadão on 13 Apr 2017; the accused dispute it ([Tribuna PR](https://www.tribunapr.com.br/noticias/politica/propina-em-usina-no-rio-madeira-foi-de-r-80-milhoes/amp/)). These are allegations, not findings.
- **MPF and court suits:**
  - 6 Mar 2014: civil public action by MPF, state/federal public defenders and OAB-RO after the 2014 flood. On 10 Mar 2014 the 5th Environmental Federal Court (Judge Herculano Martins Nacif) ordered both consortia to assist about 10,500 displaced people, redo the environmental impact studies within 90 days, with a R$ 100,000/day fine and possible licence suspension ([Congresso em Foco, 11 Mar 2014](https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/54268/justica-determina-novos-estudos-de-impacto-nas-usinas-de-jirau-e-santo-antonio)). Final outcome not found.
  - 29 Jul 2025: MPF civil public action 1013103-37.2025.4.01.4100 against Jirau for exceeding its licensed flood quota into Mapinguari National Park (over 2,000 ha, headline 2,884 ha), asking R$ 1.3 m material and R$ 500,000 collective moral damages plus compensation ([MPF](https://www.mpf.mp.br/o-mpf/unidades/pr-ro/noticias/mpf-cobra-na-justica-que-hidreletrica-de-jirau-respeite-cota-de-inundacao-e-compense-danos-ao-parna-mapinguari-em-rondonia)).
  - The 2008 MPF suit against the Jirau auction itself: outcome [UNVERIFIED], not found.
- **Who bore costs:** displaced riverside residents; the Union through Furnas' stake and BNDES financing [UNVERIFIED, BNDES amounts not opened].

## Sete Brasil - additions (losses by investor)

- **Years:** 2010-2020. Lula and Dilma.
- **What was transferred / size:** shareholders' contributions R$ 8.3 bn, potentially lost entirely; expected loss about R$ 2.5 bn, equal to a 30% write-down of the FIP Sondas quotas ([Istoé Dinheiro, 9 May 2015](https://istoedinheiro.com.br/fundacoes-e-bancos-tem-perdas-de-r25bi-com-sete)).
- **By investor (same page, press estimates):** Funcef about R$ 500 m; Petros 29% cut in value of its Sete investment per an independent appraisal; FI-FGTS about R$ 640 m invested; Previ under 1% of its assets; Bradesco 3% via a private equity fund; BTG Pactual provisioned 25%; Santander declined to say; Valia not disclosed. The article notes the fund administrator (Caixa) had not announced the quota values.
- **Later estimate:** Greenfield complaint (29 Dec 2019) put the loss borne by pension-fund participants at R$ 5.5 bn and sought R$ 16 bn reparation; Valia held 2.56% of FIP Sondas; funds kept investing after the schedule slipped, and the money was fully applied by 2016 ([Gazeta do Povo, 9 Jan 2020](https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/republica/operacao-do-mpf-denuncia-29-executivos-e-pede-reparacao-de-r-16-bilhoes/)). The two figures (R$ 2.5 bn in 2015, R$ 5.5 bn in 2020) are different dates and bases; no per-fund figure was found in the second source.
- **Who bore costs:** pension-fund participants (Petros, Funcef, Previ, Valia), FI-FGTS (worker FGTS money), private banks and funds.
- **Not found:** Bradesco, Santander and BTG exact losses.

## Bolsa Empresario / PSI - additions (cost to the Treasury)

- **Years:** 2009-2015 (PSI ended 31 Dec 2015). Lula then Dilma.
- **Size:** Treasury injections into BNDES: R$ 441 bn in 2008-14 (10.7% of GDP) per FGV IBRE; the Gazeta do Povo reports R$ 455 bn over seven years. Range R$ 441-455 bn, differing definitions ([FGV IBRE blog, 2 May 2023](https://blogdoibre.fgv.br/node/1648); [Gazeta do Povo, 30 Dec 2015](https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/economia/bndes-encerra-o-psi-uma-das-origens-das-pedaladas-7tv9rumumwmt1p5tyesx2lkqi)).
- **Cost, estimated by whom:**
  - IBRE (2008-22): implicit subsidy R$ 145.9 bn plus explicit equalization R$ 59.7 bn = R$ 205.6 bn (3.7% of GDP); present value R$ 329 bn; net of corporate tax return R$ 217 bn. It also notes a favourable nominal cash flow to the Treasury of R$ 248 bn (withdrawals R$ 689 bn vs deposits R$ 441 bn). These cover all Treasury-BNDES loans, not PSI alone.
  - SPE (Fazenda), Apr 2013: R$ 11.785 bn (2011) and R$ 12.686 bn (2012), preliminary, under a new method; the Treasury (STN) method gave R$ 19.2 bn for 2011 and SPE called that wrong ([Olhar Direto, 18 Apr 2013](https://olhardireto.com.br/agro-e-negocios/noticias/subsidios-do-tesouro-a-bndes-e-psi-chegam-a-r-12-7-bi-em-2012)). Range for 2011: R$ 11.8-19.2 bn, depending on method.
- **Equalization debt:** R$ 27.2 bn owed to BNDES by mid-2015; government paid R$ 15.1 bn in Dec 2015; TCU classed deferred equalization as a fiscal "pedalada" (Gazeta do Povo above).
- **TCU PSI-specific cost:** no TCU ruling quantifying total PSI cost was found. A search of TCU rulings returned none for PSI [UNVERIFIED that none exists].
- **Who got it:** borrowers of subsidized machinery and capital-goods credit; named beneficiaries not extracted.
- **Counter-view (IBRE authors):** the subsidy may have generated GDP, jobs and tax revenue offsetting the cost, but they give no estimate.

## Angra 3 contracts

- **Years:** works resumed 2008; Angramon contracts 2009-2014; halted Oct 2015; TCU audit 2017. Lula, Dilma, Temer.
- **What it is:** Eletronuclear (Eletrobras) nuclear plant under construction since 1984.
- **Size:** original estimate R$ 8.3 bn in 2008; Eletronuclear later estimated over R$ 25 bn including sunk costs, about R$ 17 bn more to finish, about R$ 12 bn if abandoned (loans, dismantling, environment) ([TCU press, Aug 2017](https://portal.tcu.gov.br/imprensa/noticias/conclusao-de-angra-3-pode-custar-r-25-bilhoes)). The TCU ruling records R$ 7.17 bn spent by 30 Sep 2015 ([TCU TC 016.991/2015-0](https://portal.tcu.gov.br/data/files/16/B5/85/0D/33CFA51010B6DEA51A2818A8/016.991-2015-0%20-%20angra%203.pdf)). A search snippet put a later BNDES study at about R$ 23 bn [UNVERIFIED].
- **Who got it:** electromechanical assembly tender GAC.T/CN-003/13 had only two bidders for two packages; the winning consortium included Camargo Corrêa, UTC, Andrade Gutierrez, Odebrecht, EBE and Queiroz Galvão, per prosecutor Athayde Ribeiro Costa ([Congresso em Foco](https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticias/presidente-licenciado-da-eletronuclear-recebeu-r-45-mi-em-propina-diz-mpf)). Financing: BNDES loans (amount not found).
- **Price vs value:** TCU found indications of overpricing in the tender of R$ 314 m (10.5%), reduced after Eletronuclear's reply to R$ 96 m (3.2% of R$ 2.98 bn), and reclassified the finding from "serious, halt" to "serious, may continue" (TCU ruling above). The tender allowed bids up to 5% above the budget. TCU separately noted about R$ 4.2 bn of overpricing found at Rnest, Repar and Angra 3 together.
- **Legal mechanism:** public tender under Lei 8.666/1993 by a state subsidiary; TCU imposed a maximum 5-year ban on cartel firms in the same ruling.
- **Corruption:** MPF said Eletronuclear's president Othon Luiz Pinheiro da Silva received R$ 4.5 m from consortium firms; Andrade Gutierrez allegedly paid since 2009 via a firm he owned; he denied it ([Congresso em Foco](https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticias/presidente-licenciado-da-eletronuclear-recebeu-r-45-mi-em-propina-diz-mpf)). Camargo Corrêa executive Dalton Avancini described the cartel (same page).
- **Who bore costs:** Eletrobras and electricity consumers via the tariff the TCU said the cost increase would affect.
- **After:** works halted Oct 2015; Eletronuclear seeking partners (TCU press above).
- **Not found:** per-contractor contract values.

## Banco Panamericano / Caixa (2009-2011)

- **Years:** Dec 2009 purchase; fraud found Sep-Nov 2010; 2011 BTG takeover; TCU 2020. Lula, Dilma.
- **What was transferred:** Caixa (via Caixapar) bought 35.54% of capital for R$ 739.272 m in Dec 2009 (70% paid then, the remaining 30% = R$ 221.8 m on 26 Jul 2010); BCB approved on 15 Jul 2010 ([TCU Acórdão 987/2020](https://www.gov.br/mj/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao/transparencia-e-prestacao-de-contas/relatorio-de-fiscalizacao-do-orgao-de-controle-externo-tcu-exercicio-de-2020/representacao/acordao-987-2020-plenario.pdf)). Caixa held 49% of voting capital ([Gazeta do Povo, 27 Jan 2011](https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/economia/panamericano-tem-rombo-superior-a-r-25-bilhoes-dph4jtn03la620cr4qu0bymvi)).
- **Who got it / financed:** Grupo Silvio Santos received the price. After the hole was found, FGC (the banks' deposit-guarantee fund) covered the first R$ 2.5 bn and Silvio Santos pledged personal assets as collateral (same Gazeta page). FGC loan amounts beyond that: [UNVERIFIED].
- **Price vs value:** BCB estimated a hole of R$ 2.5 bn (Gazeta above); new management put the total shortfall at R$ 4.3 bn with a Dec 2010 net loss of R$ 133.6 m ([JB, 16 Feb 2011](https://www.jb.com.br/economia/noticias/2011/02/16/amp/rombo-no-banco-panamericano-chega-a-r-43-bilhoes.html)). Prosecutors alleged balance-sheet fraud of at least R$ 3.8 bn in 2007-2010; a court convicted seven former directors (TCU ruling above). BCB's own inspector had flagged R$ 171.5 m of missing provisions on 12 Mar 2010, before the approval (TCU ruling).
- **Legal mechanism:** equity purchase approved by BCB; MP de Contas asked TCU on 31 Jul 2014 to audit the purchase ([JB, 2 Aug 2014](https://www.jb.com.br/economia/noticias/2014/08/02/amp/compra-do-panamericano-pela-caixa-economica-sera-investigada-pelo-mp.html)).
- **Outcome at TCU:** Acórdão 987/2020: technical area and rapporteur found the representation against BCB unfounded (improcedente); no irregular BCB action was established, though the MP de Contas argued the approval was rushed (TCU ruling).
- **What happened after:** BTG Pactual bought control of 51% of voting shares for R$ 450 m in 2011 and Caixa kept its stake (JB 2011 above). On 6 Apr 2021 CaixaPar agreed to sell its stake (49.2% of voting) to BTG's Banco Sistema for about R$ 3.7 bn (R$ 11.42/share); the article reports a net profit of R$ 2.02 bn to Caixa ([Folha PE, 7 Apr 2021](https://www.folhape.com.br/economia/caixa-vende-participacao-no-banco-pan-para-o-btg-pactual-por-r-37/179108/)). The article does not show how R$ 2.02 bn is derived from R$ 3.7 bn minus R$ 0.74 bn.
- **Who bore costs:** the 2010-11 hole fell on FGC and the group first; Caixa's loss was a mark-to-market loss that was recovered by the 2021 sale. This is the main counter-view: the TCU representation called the deal "ruinous" at the time, but Caixa ended up with a gain.

## Patterns

1. **Public price set low, then renegotiated:** Galeão's R$ 19 bn bid in 2013 became R$ 2.9 bn in 2026; Confins got a rebalancing; losses moved to the public side after the fact.
2. **The same firms appear in auctions and in cartel cases:** Odebrecht and Andrade Gutierrez in Santo Antonio and Angra 3 are also in leniency deals; the leniency sums (R$ 8 bn agreed, about R$ 1 bn paid by mid-2022) are small beside TCU's R$ 4.2 bn overpricing at three works.
3. **Public banks and pension funds fund the risk:** BNDES (EBX R$ 10 bn, Oi R$ 14 bn), Caixa (Panamericano), FI-FGTS and Petros/Funcef (Sete Brasil R$ 2.5 to 5.5 bn).
4. **Cost estimates depend on the method:** PSI 2011 cost R$ 11.8 bn (SPE) vs R$ 19.2 bn (STN); Sete Brasil R$ 2.5 bn (2015) vs R$ 5.5 bn (2020); Galeão minimum bid R$ 4.73 vs about 4.83 bn.
5. **Ex-post accountability is thin:** TCU found Panamericano not improper at BCB, Caixa later booked a profit; Madeira suits in 2014 and 2025 target environment, not the auction.
6. **Early concession bonuses were small:** R$ 27 m (R5) to R$ 1.1 bn (R7), versus R$ 15 bn fixed for one block (Libra) once the pre-salt was known.
