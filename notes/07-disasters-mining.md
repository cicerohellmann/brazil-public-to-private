## Barão de Cocais, Vale dam Sul Superior: preventive evacuation (Feb-Mar 2019)

- **Date:** 8 Feb 2019 evacuation; level 3 alert 23 Mar 2019.
- **Place:** Barão de Cocais, Minas Gerais (Socorro, Tabuleiro, Piteiras communities).
- **Operator and ownership:** Vale, the Gongo Soco mine. Privatized in 1997 **[no source]**.
- **Deaths:** none recorded in this entry.
- **People affected:** about 454 people removed from the self-rescue zone (Agência Minas). Other reports say about 500 or "more than 400" **[verify]**.
- **Environmental damage:** none quantified in sources reviewed.
- **Fines and outcome:** Vale says it paid R$ 527 million in reparations (per Carta Capital; not checked). Dam deactivated 2008; decharacterization planned for 2029 (per Vale).
- **Sources:**

## Brumadinho, Córrego do Feijão dam (25 Jan 2019)

- **Place:** Córrego do Feijão mine, Brumadinho, Minas Gerais; mud reached the Paraopeba river.
- **Operator and ownership:** Vale. Privatized 1997 **[no source]**.
- **Deaths:** **[verify]** 270 (most common count), 272 counting missing (IHU, Dec 2020). A later report gives 258 dead plus 11 missing (source: Outras Palavras, one year on). A 2022 article says "242 mortos" with no convictions (Congresso em Foco). The count differs by source and date.
- **People affected:** not quantified in sources reviewed.
- **Environmental damage:** **[verify]** Paraopeba river affected. One source estimates 13 million m3 of tailings released; another gives a mud area of 3.6 km2 over 10 km. Figures conflict.
- **Fines and settlements:** IBAMA fined Vale five times R$ 50 million, total R$ 250 million, plus a daily fine for failing to rescue fauna (per Folha Vitória). Courts blocked assets; sources give R$ 11 billion and R$ 16 billion **[verify]**.
- **Criminal case:** MPMG charged 16 people and two companies (Vale, TÜV SÜD) in Jan 2020 with 270 counts of qualified intentional homicide. The STJ annulled the state court's acceptance of the charges, and the STF ruled in Dec 2022 that federal courts have jurisdiction. The federal court accepted the charges in Jan 2023 (MPF). No conviction found in sources reviewed.
- **Regulator:** A 2019 report says ANM had 35 trained inspectors for 790 tailings dams **[verify]**. Federal police experts found Barragem I had a failure risk 20 times above the acceptable limit (Folha Vitória).

## Hydro Alunorte red-mud / bauxite waste overflow, Barcarena, Pará (Feb 2018)

- **Date:** Feb 2018 (flood-driven overflow; IHU article 2 Mar 2018).
- **Place:** Barcarena, Pará; Murucupi and other rivers and igarapés.
- **Operator and ownership:** Hydro Alunorte, Norwegian Hydro group (Norsk Hydro). Alunorte was controlled by Vale until 2011 **[verify]** (per a Chamber of Deputies report).
- **Deaths:** none recorded in sources reviewed. Local reports describe deaths and threats, unverified **[verify]**.
- **People affected:** communities on the rivers, including quilombolas (CUT articles). Not quantified.
- **Environmental damage:** heavy metals, including lead, detected in water by Instituto Evandro Chagas. Company admitted releasing rainwater from a pipeline without a licence (4-3 per one source).
- **Fines and orders:** Pará court ordered output cut by 50%, one basin banned, and R$ 1 million/day fine for non-compliance (per summary). State issued an infraction notice for new spills. The company was also fined R$ 20 million in 2018 for a new spill (separate report) **[verify]**. MP-PA filed a civil action seeking about R$ 250 million **[verify]**. Earlier, IBAMA fined R$ 17.1 million in 2009 for spills into the Murucupi river; per one report it was not paid as of 2018 **[verify]**.
- **Criminal case:** not found in sources reviewed.

## Braskem rock-salt mining subsidence, Maceió, Alagoas (2018-2025, ongoing)

- **Date:** mining from the 1970s; subsidence reported from 2018; the mine stopped in May 2019 after a CPRM (Geological Survey) report tied the subsidence to Braskem's activity.
- **Place:** Maceió, Alagoas; neighbourhoods including Pinheiro, Bebedouro, Mutange and others.
- **Operator and ownership:** Braskem, a private petrochemical company (Novonor/Odebrecht and Petrobras shareholders). Privatization: not applicable to Braskem as a company **[no source]**.
- **Deaths:** none recorded in sources reviewed.
- **People affected:** tens of thousands of residents forced out (figure not checked in this run).
- **Environmental damage:** ground collapse, cracks, flooding of the Mundaú lagoon area **[no source]**.
- **Settlements:**
  - July 2023: R$ 1.7 billion to Maceió city hall, for compensation and relocation (Safra, Forbes).
  - Nov 2025: R$ 1.2 billion with the State of Alagoas, paid over ten years (JB; DGABC). Victims' groups called it too low (Movimento Econômico).
- **Criminal and foreign cases:** a Rotterdam court ruled against Braskem in a Dutch case (per summary); appeal possible **[verify]**.
- **Sources:**
  - https://oespecialista.safra.com.br/braskem-indeniza-maceio/
  - https://forbes.com.br/forbes-money/2023/07/braskem-faz-acordo-de-r-17-bilhao-com-maceio/
  - https://www.jb.com.br/brasil/2025/11/1057573-braskem-fecha-acordo-de-rs-12-bilhao-por-desmoronamentos-em-maceio.html

## Caetité and Lagoa Real, INB uranium mine, Bahia (2015, ongoing)

- **Date:** INB admitted water contamination in 2015 (Lagoa Real wells, tested Oct 2014 and Mar 2015).
- **Place:** Caetité and Lagoa Real, Bahia; quilombola communities.
- **Operator and ownership:** INB (Indústrias Nucleares do Brasil), a state-owned federal company. Not privatized.
- **Deaths:** none recorded in sources reviewed.
- **People affected:** residents on private wells; quilombolas reported worsening water supply (CNDH mission, Nov 2025).
- **Environmental damage:** uranium above the WHO limit (15 µg/L) in at least three wells. INB denied the link, saying the wells are about 20 km away in another basin. Summary also mentions a 5 million-litre solvent spill **[verify]**.
- **Fines and outcome:** Ministry of Environment and IBAMA ordered an end to well use. Bahia took the case to the STF. Final outcome not found.

- **Place:** Bento Rodrigues, Mariana, Minas Gerais; mud reached the Rio Doce and Espírito Santo.

## Kinross (Paracatu) gold mine, Minas Gerais (1987-2025, ongoing)

- **Date:** Santo Antônio dam built 1987; Eustáquio dam operating since 2010.
- **Place:** Paracatu, Minas Gerais; Rio Paracatu.

## Patterns

1. **Ownership and privatization.** Most large tailings failures involved Vale or its partners (Brumadinho, Mariana via Samarco, Barcarena via Alunorte/Imerys), but the companies are different: state-owned (INB), privatized (Vale, Samarco), and small private firms (Herculano, Rio Verde). Privatization dates for Vale (1997) and Serra Pelada's land are not sourced in this run.
2. **Warnings ignored.** Brumadinho: federal police found the dam at 20 times the acceptable risk level. Herculano: a worker warned of problems (unverified). Barão de Cocais: the dam was not decommissioned until after evacuations.
3. **Regulator capacity.** A 2019 report says ANM had 35 trained inspectors for 790 tailings dams (Folha Vitória) **[verify]**.
4. **Who paid.** Brumadinho and Mariana were settled in billions, mainly paid by the companies, but most victims' groups say the settlements are too low. Criminal cases have been slow: Mariana had a 2026 conviction after an earlier federal acquittal (acquittal date not confirmed); Brumadinho had no conviction in sources reviewed.
