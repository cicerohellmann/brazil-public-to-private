# 17a. Tax privileges and resource rents as wealth transfer

Scope: eight cases on the tax side of the transfer from public to private hands. They complement notes 01-09 and do not repeat Redata, the Zona Franca origin story (note 03 #14) or the 2016-2026 tax-expenditure summary (note 06 #19-20). Notes 03 and 06 cover those; this note adds the series, the mechanisms and the counter-views.

Conventions: figures in nominal R$ of each year unless said otherwise. "[UNVERIFIED]" means I did not open a page that confirms the claim (planalto.gov.br, camara.leg.br/legin, monitormercantil, folhavitoria, mattosfilho, legjur and bhp.com were blocked or returned 403 when I tried). Search-snippet figures are marked as such.

Shared abbreviations: DGT = Demonstrativo dos Gastos Tributarios (Receita Federal); STR = Sistema Tributario de Referencia; RFB = Receita Federal do Brasil.

## 1. Federal tax expenditures (gastos tributarios) series, 2008-2024

- **Years:** 2008-2024 (DGT "bases efetivas" series; the 2024 value is a projection).
- **Government/ruler:** Lula II (2008-10), Dilma (2011-16), Temer (2016-18), Bolsonaro (2019-22), Lula III (2023-24). The series grows under all of them.
- **What was transferred and how big:** revenue not collected through exemptions, deductions, reduced rates and deferrals. DGT series, R$ bn (share of GDP): 2008 114.8 (3.82%); 2010 135.9 (3.60%); 2013 223.3 (4.19%); 2015 270.1 (4.50%); 2018 311.2 (4.44%); 2020 328.6 (4.32%); 2021 420.8 (4.67%); 2022 479.8 (4.76%); 2023 519.0 (4.78%); 2024 512.3 (4.44%). Share of federal tax collection rose from 17.4% in 2008 to 24.0% in 2021 ([RFB DGT, bases efetivas 2021, series 2019-2024](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/relatorios/renuncia/gastos-tributarios-bases-efetivas/dgt-bases-efetivas-2021-serie-2019-a-2024-base-conceitual-e-gerencial.pdf), Quadro XXXV, text extract read locally).
- **Range where sources disagree (2024):** DGT 2021-based series gives R$512.3 bn, 4.44% of GDP ([RFB DGT](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/relatorios/renuncia/gastos-tributarios-bases-efetivas/dgt-bases-efetivas-2021-serie-2019-a-2024-base-conceitual-e-gerencial.pdf)); an FGV study cites a later RFB estimate of R$563.5 bn, 4.8% of GDP, and says it was about 2% of GDP twenty years earlier ([FGV EESP report on tax expenditures and ZFM, June 2025](https://eesp.fgv.br/sites/default/files/files-eesp/relatorio_gastos_tributarios_e_zona_franca_de_manaus_v_18_de_junho2025.pdf)). The same FGV text gives R$578.4 bn as the updated RFB figure for 2025. The gap comes from vintage (effective-base series versus later projections), not from different concepts [partly UNVERIFIED].
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** in the 2021 breakdown, Simples Nacional 21.70% of the total (R$91.3 bn), agriculture and agroindustry 12.12%, exempt IRPF income 8.85%, regional development 7.91%, non-profits 7.56%; ZFM and free-trade areas R$25.2 bn (5.98%) ([RFB DGT](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/relatorios/renuncia/gastos-tributarios-bases-efetivas/dgt-bases-efetivas-2021-serie-2019-a-2024-base-conceitual-e-gerencial.pdf)). By tax, COFINS holds 28.56% (1.33% of GDP), IRPJ 20.97%, IRPF 14.71% (same source). Federal tax expenditures flow 62% to the Southeast and South ([FGV](https://eesp.fgv.br/sites/default/files/files-eesp/relatorio_gastos_tributarios_e_zona_franca_de_manaus_v_18_de_junho2025.pdf)). Financed by everyone else through higher rates or lower services.
- **Price/terms vs value (who estimated):** the DGT is the Receita's own estimate; it is not a market valuation. The DGT does not cost several large items (Repetro, dividends, JCP, presumed profit) because the STR treats them as part of the reference system or "technically impossible" to compute ([INESC](https://inesc.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/levantamento-inedito-mostra-renuncias-bilionarias-para-industria-petroleo-docx.pdf)). So the series is a floor, not a ceiling.
- **Legal mechanism:** hundreds of separate norms: Simples (LC 123/2006), PIS/COFINS zero-rates, IRPF exemptions, regional regimes. The legal basis of the DGT itself was not opened [UNVERIFIED]; the 2023 Portaria RFB 319 and LC 187/2021 allowed publishing beneficiaries by company ([INESC](https://inesc.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/levantamento-inedito-mostra-renuncias-bilionarias-para-industria-petroleo-docx.pdf)).
- **Who bore the costs:** other taxpayers; the DGT puts the 2021 federal expenditure at 24.03% of federal collection ([RFB DGT](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/relatorios/renuncia/gastos-tributarios-bases-efetivas/dgt-bases-efetivas-2021-serie-2019-a-2024-base-conceitual-e-gerencial.pdf)). Add states: FGV IBRE estimates state expenditures at 2.4% of GDP in 2024, so federal plus state may reach 7.2% of GDP ([FGV EESP](https://eesp.fgv.br/sites/default/files/files-eesp/relatorio_gastos_tributarios_e_zona_franca_de_manaus_v_18_de_junho2025.pdf)).
- **Counter-view / context:** Brazil is not an outlier. The RFB table for 2021 puts Brazil at 4.67% of GDP against a Latin American listing average of 5.26%; Canada is 7.94%, US 6.06%, Colombia 7.73%, Chile 2.49%, Peru 1.82% ([RFB DGT](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/relatorios/renuncia/gastos-tributarios-bases-efetivas/dgt-bases-efetivas-2021-serie-2019-a-2024-base-conceitual-e-gerencial.pdf)). The RFB itself says each country uses its own method, so the table is only a reference. FGV adds that Brazil's average over 2002-2022 was 3.0% of GDP, rising from 1.3% (2002) to 4.7% (2022), against Italy 7.0% and US 6.9% ([FGV EESP](https://eesp.fgv.br/sites/default/files/files-eesp/relatorio_gastos_tributarios_e_zona_franca_de_manaus_v_18_de_junho2025.pdf)). The dispute is about the pace (the nominal amount rose from R$114.8 bn in 2008 to R$519.0 bn in 2023 and the GDP share from 3.82% to 4.78%, about +25% ([RFB DGT](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/relatorios/renuncia/gastos-tributarios-bases-efetivas/dgt-bases-efetivas-2021-serie-2019-a-2024-base-conceitual-e-gerencial.pdf)); FGV's 20-year view says about 2% to 4.8%) and about who benefits, not about whether the level is unusual.
- **What happened after:** the 2024 reform (EC 132/2023) and later proposals targeted tax expenditures [UNVERIFIED, no page opened]. The DGT still excludes the largest special regimes.
- **Sources:** RFB DGT (link above); FGV EESP report (link above); INESC (link above).

## 2. Lei Kandir (LC 87/1996): ICMS exemption on exports of primary goods

- **Years:** 1996 onward. LC 87 dated 13 Sept 1996 (date from the Camara legin URL slug, text not opened: [UNVERIFIED](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/leicom/1996/leicomplementar-87-13-setembro-1996-348769-norma-pl.html)). Compensation fixed by LC 115/2002 and EC 42/2003; STF ruling 30 Nov 2016; settlement LC 176/2020.
- **Government/ruler:** Fernando Henrique Cardoso government [UNVERIFIED, no page opened naming the signatory]; the compensation fight ran under every later government.
- **What was transferred and how big:** the ICMS (a state tax) was waived on exports of primary and semi-finished goods. States and municipalities lost revenue. Estimates differ widely:
  - net state loss of R$25 bn in 2016 alone and R$268.9 bn accumulated 1997-2016, presented by the president of Fapespa (Para) at a hearing of the Kandir joint committee (method not described in the article; [Congresso em Foco](https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/45551/estados-perderam-r-25-bilhoes-devido-a-lei-kandir-em-2016-diz-estudo));
  - R$637 bn since 1996 for the 27 states, as of 2019, with Minas Gerais owed R$135 bn (21%); the article does not name who estimated it ([Hoje em Dia](https://www.hojeemdia.com.br/primeiro-plano/d%C3%ADvida-bilion%C3%A1ria-estados-se-mobilizam-para-recuperar-perdas-de-r-637-bilh%C3%B5es-da-lei-kandir-1.699524));
  - range: R$268.9 bn (1997-2016) to R$637 bn (to 2019). The windows differ and the methods are unknown; do not treat them as the same measure.
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** exporters of ore, soy, meat, pulp and similar goods (the waiver applied to the commodity export chain; named companies not opened) [partly UNVERIFIED]. Financed by states and municipalities: by 2016, highest absolute losses were Minas Gerais R$64.6 bn, Rio R$60.7 bn, Para R$35.7 bn, Mato Grosso R$30.8 bn, Espirito Santo R$28.3 bn; as a share of revenue Mato Grosso 26.01% and Para 14.58% ([Congresso em Foco](https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/45551/estados-perderam-r-25-bilhoes-devido-a-lei-kandir-em-2016-diz-estudo)).
- **Price/terms vs value (who estimated):** the Union's compensation to states was never based on measured loss. The STF found that Congress failed to regulate art. 91 of the ADCT for more than ten years after EC 42/2003 ([Conjur, 13 Dec 2016](https://www.conjur.com.br/2016-dez-13/congresso-foi-omisso-nao-editar-lei-compensacao-icms//?print=1)).
- **Legal mechanism:** LC 87/1996 art. 3 exemption; transitional compensation in art. 31 and annex, amended by LC 115/2002; EC 42/2003 wrote the exemption into the Constitution and created ADCT art. 91 (all per [Conjur](https://www.conjur.com.br/2016-dez-13/congresso-foi-omisso-nao-editar-lei-compensacao-icms//?print=1)). ADO 25, filed in 2013 by Para and 15 other states, rapporteur Gilmar Mendes ([Comsefaz](https://comsefaz.org.br/novo/sancionada-lei-que-garante-repasse-de-r-58-bilhoes-aos-estados-pelas-perdas-da-lei-kandir/)). On 30 Nov 2016 the STF unanimously found the omission, gave Congress 12 months (the request was 120 days) and said the TCU would set the amount if Congress failed ([Conjur](https://www.conjur.com.br/2016-dez-13/congresso-foi-omisso-nao-editar-lei-compensacao-icms//?print=1)).
- **Who bore the costs:** the states. Settlement: LC 176/2020 pays R$58 bn from 2020 to 2037: R$4 bn per year to 2030 (R$1 bn of it to municipalities), then falling R$500 mn per year for 2031-2037. Extra R$7.6 bn (R$3.6 bn conditional on PEC 188/2019, R$4 bn after the Atapu and Sepia oil auctions) brings the total to R$65.6 bn ([Comsefaz](https://comsefaz.org.br/novo/sancionada-lei-que-garante-repasse-de-r-58-bilhoes-aos-estados-pelas-perdas-da-lei-kandir/)). States had to drop lawsuits against the Union to receive it (same source).
- **Counter-view:** the Union argued it already transfers large sums to states and that the temporary scheme is constitutional ([Conjur](https://www.conjur.com.br/2016-dez-13/congresso-foi-omisso-nao-editar-lei-compensacao-icms//?print=1)). The rapporteur replied that social contributions, which are not shared through FPE/FPM, rose from 29% to 54% of federal revenue between 1994 and 2016, so the Union gained from the shift in the tax model (same Conjur). A Folha Vitoria headline says the Fazenda held that the Kandir law does not oblige full repayment; the page returned 403, so its arguments are [UNVERIFIED](https://www.folhavitoria.com.br/economia/fazenda-lei-kandir-nao-obriga-uniao-a-ressarcimento-integral-aos-estados/). The economic defence (exports and the trade balance rose) is not documented in a page I opened [UNVERIFIED].
- **What happened after:** the R$58 bn is far below either loss estimate (R$268.9 bn to 2016; R$637 bn to 2019), so on those numbers the settlement repays a fraction. Whether the exemption brought more exports than it cost is [UNVERIFIED].
- **Sources:** Comsefaz, Conjur, Congresso em Foco, Hoje em Dia (links above).

## 3. CFEM (mining royalty) and the 2017 reform, compared with other countries

- **Years:** CFEM created by the 1988 Constitution regime, regulated by Lei 7.990/1989 and Lei 8.001/1990 [UNVERIFIED, no page opened]; rates and base changed by MP 789/2017 converted into Lei 13.540/2017 (dated 18 Dec 2017 per the Camara URL slug, text not opened: https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/lei/2017/lei-13540-18-dezembro-2017-785987-norma-pl.html).
- **Government/ruler:** Michel Temer.
- **What was transferred and how big:** the public share of mineral rent. Before 2017 CFEM was charged on net revenue: iron ore 2%, gold 1%, bauxite/manganese/potassium 3%, precious stones 0.2% ([MME royalty report no. 9](https://www.gov.br/mme/pt-br/assuntos/secretarias/geologia-mineracao-e-transformacao-mineral/relatorios-de-apoio-ao-pnm-2030-projeto-estal-1/estudos-sobre-economia-e-o-setor-mineral-brasileiro/documentos/relatorio-ndeg9_analise-comparativa-de-royalties.pdf), a pre-reform description, up to 3% on net revenue). Collection: R$7.45 bn in 2024 on R$270.8 bn of mineral operations; iron ore supplied 75.01% of it (R$5.58 bn on R$160.7 bn) ([Giliad de Souza Silva, Camara CME hearing, 12 Aug 2025](https://www2.camara.leg.br/atividade-legislativa/comissoes/comissoes-permanentes/cme/apresentacoes-em-eventos/apresentacao-de-convidados-2025/12-08-2025-debate-sobre-a-compensacao-financeira-pela-exploracao-mineral-cfem/Giliad%20Souza.pdf)). The implied average rate on those figures is about 2.75%, and about 3.47% for iron ore (my division of the figures in the slides). Another source gives R$7.91 bn in 2025, up 6.3%, with a record of about R$10.3 bn in 2021 ([Brasil 61](https://brasil61.com/n/cfem-94-dos-municipios-brasileiros-receberam-recursos-da-cfem-em-2025-bras2615627)). Years 2017-2020 (R$1.84 bn, 3.04 bn, 4.50 bn, 6.08 bn) come from an unopened search snippet [UNVERIFIED].
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** miners keep the rent after CFEM. The same slides show Petrobras plus mining as 51.2% of the 18 "superlucro" companies listed (above R$5.5 bn profit); Petrobras alone is 33.9% ([Giliad slides](https://www2.camara.leg.br/atividade-legislativa/comissoes/comissoes-permanentes/cme/apresentacoes-em-eventos/apresentacao-de-convidados-2025/12-08-2025-debate-sobre-a-compensacao-financeira-pela-exploracao-mineral-cfem/Giliad%20Souza.pdf); profit year not stated in my extract). Company-level CFEM payments were not extracted [UNVERIFIED]. Financed by producing municipalities and the public that bears the environmental cost; see note 07 for the disasters.
- **Price/terms vs value (who estimated):** the 2017 law moved the base from net to gross revenue, less taxes on sale, and raised the iron ore ceiling from 2% to 3.5% (can be cut to 2%), gold 1% to 1.5%, diamond 0.2% to 2%, niobium 2% to 3%; potassium, phosphate and fertilizers at 0.2%; the rates in the joint-committee text differed from the final law (Congresso em Foco on the MP text; [Congresso em Foco, MP 789](https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/28953/camara-aprova-mp-dos-royalties-da-mineracao-texto-altera-distribuicao-dos-recursos)). Brazil's reference is a percentage of revenue; the compared systems tax margin or profit. Examples from a lawyers' article (FPA Advocacia, industry-aligned): Ontario 10% of net profit, Quebec 12%, Newfoundland 16%; Peru 1-12% of operating profit with 1% floor on sales; Chile since 2023 1% of sales plus 8-26% of operating margin; South Africa 0.5-7% ([Eixos](https://eixos.com.br/mineracao/cfem-antes-de-aumentar-royalties-e-preciso-comparar-bases-e-cobrar-eficiencia/)). Western Australia charges 7.5% of sales value on iron ore, lump and fines alike since July 2013 (fines were 5.625% under the 2010 deal; search-result summary, the official page was not opened: [WA government](https://www.wa.gov.au/service/resources/mining-and-petroleum/royalties-western-australia) [UNVERIFIED]).
- **Legal mechanism:** Lei 13.540/2017; the distribution in the old regime was 12% Union, 23% state, 65% producing municipality, with a 50% landowner share of the CFEM value under Lei 8.901/94 ([MME report](https://www.gov.br/mme/pt-br/assuntos/secretarias/geologia-mineracao-e-transformacao-mineral/relatorios-de-apoio-ao-pnm-2030-projeto-estal-1/estudos-sobre-economia-e-o-setor-mineral-brasileiro/documentos/relatorio-ndeg9_analise-comparativa-de-royalties.pdf)). Under the pre-2017 text the base was net of costs, which let firms shrink it.
- **Who bore the costs:** underpayment: the TCU found that about 69.7% of titleholders did not pay CFEM voluntarily in 2017-2022 and that those who paid under-reported by 40.2% on average (cited by the FPA lawyers; the TCU PDF itself was unreadable: [Eixos](https://eixos.com.br/mineracao/cfem-antes-de-aumentar-royalties-e-preciso-comparar-bases-e-cobrar-eficiencia/), [TCU report copy, not read](https://observatoriodamineracao.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Relatorio-completo-de-fiscalizacao-tcu.pdf) [UNVERIFIED]). They estimate better enforcement could lift collection to about R$16 bn with no rate change (same Eixos; author estimate).
- **Counter-view:** the industry-side argument (the Eixos piece) is that the nominal rates cannot be compared across regimes and that the rate on gross revenue can be "as or more onerous" than higher nominal rates elsewhere, and that the problem is enforcement, not rate. The opposite view: the Giliad slides on "renda mineral" frame the sector's profitability and "superlucro" as the base for a higher take (Giliad, link above). A rate-on-revenue royalty is also regressive for low-margin producers. Which view is right depends on the profit margin of each ore; the effective rate comparison cannot be settled from the pages I opened.
- **What happened after:** collection grew about fourfold between 2017 and 2024 (from the unverified R$1.84 bn snippet to R$7.45 bn confirmed); the extent due to the new base versus price and volume is not separated [UNVERIFIED]. The Chamber hearing of 12 Aug 2025 ("Debate sobre a CFEM") shows the fight is still open.
- **Sources:** links above.

## 4. Oil royalties and the participacao especial

- **Years:** royalties of 5% tied to Decreto-Lei 523/1969 by one source (the 1953 origin in Lei 2.004 is not confirmed); Lei 7.990/1989 and the Fundo Especial do Petroleo; Lei 9.478/1997 (Petroleum Law, creates ANP, participacao especial from 2000); Lei 12.351/2010 (production sharing in the pre-salt); Lei 12.734/2012 (redistribution) ([search-result summaries, pages not opened: UNVERIFIED](https://www.gov.br/anp/pt-br/assuntos/royalties-e-outras-participacoes/royalties)).
- **Government/ruler:** military (1969), Sarney (1989), FHC (1997), Lula (2010), Dilma (2012). Names of signatories not verified from opened pages [UNVERIFIED].
- **What was transferred and how big:** the question is how much of the oil rent the state kept. Lei 9.478 raised the basic royalty from 5% to up to 10% (the 10% is from a search snippet [UNVERIFIED]; the ANP page, opened, says the field rate "can vary from 5% to 15%" as set in each concession contract and cites Leis 9.478/1997 and 7.990/1989: [ANP](https://www.gov.br/anp/pt-br/assuntos/royalties-e-outras-participacoes/royalties)). The 5% rate in 1997 produced "only R$208 million in today's values", about 0.2% of today's revenue, per an FGV IBRE opinion piece. Royalties plus participacao especial grew 5,204% from 1999 to 2025 (about 53 times), and in 2022 first exceeded the ICMS on fuel ([FGV IBRE blog](https://blogdoibre.fgv.br/posts/royalties-do-petroleo-o-que-analise-das-series-historicas-revela), opened; opinion piece by the authors, not FGV's institutional view; its annual R$ table was an image and was not extracted). The TCU reports R$58.22 bn paid out as royalties and participacao especial in 2024 ([Eixos, Estadao Conteudo, 24 Apr 2025](https://eixos.com.br/politica/tcu-ve-fragilidades-na-distribuicao-de-royalties-de-petroleo-e-gas-e-cobra-ajustes-da-anp/), page opened). The ANP projects R$68 bn for 2025, and the TCU found weaknesses in the ANP's calculation and distribution ([Eixos](https://eixos.com.br/politica/tcu-ve-fragilidades-na-distribuicao-de-royalties-de-petroleo-e-gas-e-cobra-ajustes-da-anp/)). A second search snippet gave a much higher 2024 total that I could not source, so I use R$58.22 bn only.
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** the state kept more as production rose. Distribution is concentrated: ten municipalities hold half of what goes to municipalities and have under 9 million people; some get over R$20,000 per resident per year ([FGV IBRE blog](https://blogdoibre.fgv.br/posts/royalties-do-petroleo-o-que-analise-das-series-historicas-revela)). Lei 12.734 cut the "confronting states" share from about 30% to 20% and "confronting municipalities" from 20% to 4% (same source); the TCU found weaknesses in how the ANP calculates and distributes (Eixos link above).
- **Price/terms vs value (who estimated):** in the concession regime the rent is shared between company and state by rate, not by auction price alone. Bonus, royalty and participacao especial figures by field were not opened [UNVERIFIED].
- **Legal mechanism:** concession contracts (Lei 9.478); production sharing (Lei 12.351/2010); redistribution under Lei 12.734/2012. The STF suspended the redistribution rules in 2013 via ADI 4917 [UNVERIFIED, not opened], and the final status is also [UNVERIFIED].
- **Who bore the costs:** non-producing states and municipalities under the old distribution; producing ones after Lei 12.734 if it takes effect. The text above says the royalty model dates from 1985 and is not compensation for ICMS (FGV IBRE blog).
- **Counter-view:** the FGV piece argues that royalties are not an ICMS compensation, so producing states cannot claim them as a replacement; the producing states argue the opposite [the producing-state position is not documented in a page I opened: UNVERIFIED].
- **What happened after:** more revenue as production rose 61% between 2015 and 2025 and prices moved (FGV blog). The corruption and Petrobras context belongs to notes 05 and 06.
- **Sources:** FGV IBRE blog; ANP page; Eixos (all linked above).

## 5. REFIS and tax amnesties (parcelamentos especiais), 2000 onward

- **Years:** Refis 2000 (MP 2.004-6/2000, converted into Lei 9.964/2000), Paes 2003 (Lei 10.684/2003), Paex 2006, Refis da Crise 2009 (Lei 11.941/2009), later reopenings, Pert 2017 (the MP numbers and laws come from the TCU timeline chart: [TCU, Acordao 2.497/2018 audit](https://portal.tcu.gov.br/data/files/4D/E7/61/2B/42FD18102DFE0FF7F18818A8/Auditoria%20da%20Gestao%20da%20Divida%20Ativa%20Uniao.pdf)).
- **Government/ruler:** FHC (Refis 2000), Lula (Paes 2003, Paex 2006, Refis da Crise 2009), Dilma (reopenings), Temer (Pert 2017). The TCU chart lists the instruments; the signatories are not in the chart [UNVERIFIED].
- **What was transferred and how big:**
  - TCU: about 28 installment programs between 2000 and 2017; companies in the active-debt register are under 15% of active companies yet owe about R$1.30 trillion ([TCU audit](https://portal.tcu.gov.br/data/files/4D/E7/61/2B/42FD18102DFE0FF7F18818A8/Auditoria%20da%20Gestao%20da%20Divida%20Ativa%20Uniao.pdf)).
  - Receita Federal 2020 study cited by Netcpa: about 40 programs in 18 years costing R$176 bn ([Netcpa](https://netcpa.com.br/colunas/senado-e-economia-negociam-para-novo-refis-ser-aprovado-ate-o-fim-de-maio/10313)).
  - Range: 28 programs (TCU, 2000-2017) versus 40 programs (Receita, 18 years to 2020). The difference is the counting window and the definition of "program" (reopenings counted or not); the TCU count is the narrower source.
  - Pert: R$47.4 bn forgiven for 131 thousand taxpayers and R$59.5 bn put into up to 175 instalments; discounts up to 70% off fines and 90% off interest. Refis da Crise forgave R$60.9 bn, larger than Pert ([Jovem Pan / Estadao Conteudo, 21 Jan 2019](https://jovempan.com.br/brasil/temer-perdoou-r-474-bi-de-dividas-de-empresas-maior-anistia-em-10-anos/)).
  - Taxpayers who joined three or more programs owed over R$160 bn and about half defaulted afterwards ([Jovem Pan](https://jovempan.com.br/brasil/temer-perdoou-r-474-bi-de-dividas-de-empresas-maior-anistia-em-10-anos/)).
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** debtors, including large firms; named beneficiaries are not in the pages I opened [UNVERIFIED]. Financed by compliant taxpayers and by the social security budget. The TCU argues that repeated amnesties reward non-payment and punish those who paid on time (TCU audit, section on "distorcoes trazidas pelos parcelamentos especiais").
- **Price/terms vs value (who estimated):** the TCU says the payment terms of the Refis programs "find no parallel in the world", and charts the Brazilian terms against those of other countries (the bar values were not cleanly extractable, so the exact month counts are [UNVERIFIED]; TCU audit link above).
- **Legal mechanism:** each program is a medida provisoria converted into law with discounts on fines and interest and long instalment plans. Lei 9.964/2000 created the first Refis (TCU chart).
- **Who bore the costs:** the Treasury and the social security funds. The amount forgiven in Pert and Refis da Crise totals R$108.3 bn (47.4 + 60.9, my sum from the Jovem Pan figures), and the Receita puts the 18-year cost of all programs at R$176 bn ([Netcpa](https://netcpa.com.br/colunas/senado-e-economia-negociam-para-novo-refis-ser-aprovado-ate-o-fim-de-maio/10313)). These two sums overlap and cannot be added.
- **Counter-view:** the usual defence is that a collectible stream beats an uncollectible stock and that the recovery of the debt from firms in crisis is better than bankruptcy [UNVERIFIED, no page opened]. The Receita is quoted as not liking a new Refis in the Netcpa article, which shows the tax authority itself opposes it.
- **What happened after:** more programs followed after 2017, including 2020-2021 transaction regimes [UNVERIFIED]. Refis 2000 amounts were not opened [UNVERIFIED].
- **Sources:** TCU audit; Jovem Pan; Netcpa (all linked above).

## 6. Zona Franca de Manaus: cost estimates (extends note 03 #14)

- **Years:** DL 288/1967 created the regime; the 1988 Constitution kept it (ADCT art. 40) and ADCT art. 92-A extended the benefits to 2073 (text quoted in the Suframa/FGV material: [FGV EESP report](https://eesp.fgv.br/sites/default/files/files-eesp/relatorio_gastos_tributarios_e_zona_franca_de_manaus_v_18_de_junho2025.pdf)). The 1967 decree itself was not opened [UNVERIFIED].
- **Government/ruler:** military regime (1967, see note 03); the extension to 2073 is a later constitutional amendment whose year and government I did not verify [UNVERIFIED].
- **What was transferred and how big:** the RFB's own estimate swings: R$24.2 bn (2021), R$45.59 bn (2022), R$55.26 bn (2023), R$32.7 bn (2024) ([FGV EESP report](https://eesp.fgv.br/sites/default/files/files-eesp/relatorio_gastos_tributarios_e_zona_franca_de_manaus_v_18_de_junho2025.pdf)). The FGV report calls these "successive accounting manipulations" of the number, an interpretation by the authors. The DGT effective-base table gives R$25.2 bn (5.98% of the total) for ZFM and free-trade areas in 2021 ([RFB DGT](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/relatorios/renuncia/gastos-tributarios-bases-efetivas/dgt-bases-efetivas-2021-serie-2019-a-2024-base-conceitual-e-gerencial.pdf)).
- **Range (industry only):** the cost of the Polo Industrial de Manaus alone: R$7.5 bn in 2021 (RFB, from the FGV report), or R$5.94-9.89 bn potential in 2024 by FGV's company-level estimate, against RFB's R$8.74 bn projection for industry in 2024. The wide headline figure (R$22.95 bn in 2024, 4.1% of federal expenditure) includes commerce, services and agriculture (all [FGV EESP](https://eesp.fgv.br/sites/default/files/files-eesp/relatorio_gastos_tributarios_e_zona_franca_de_manaus_v_18_de_junho2025.pdf)). The older figure in note 03 (Senate, R$22.4 bn in 2011) is not comparable without a base-year adjustment.
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** firms in the PIM, whose 2024 turnover was R$204.9 bn, of which informatics 23.06%, electronics 18.08% and two-wheelers 17.8% (Suframa data in the [FGV report](https://eesp.fgv.br/sites/default/files/files-eesp/relatorio_gastos_tributarios_e_zona_franca_de_manaus_v_18_de_junho2025.pdf)). Financed by other taxpayers and, for the ICMS part, by other states. Federal expenditure flows mostly outside the North: 62% to the Southeast and South (FGV).
- **Price/terms vs value (who estimated):** the point of dispute is the counterfactual. Receita counts all the PIS, Cofins, IPI and import tax waived; FGV argues the real cost of the industrial pole is only the R$7.5 bn part.
- **Legal mechanism:** exemption of IPI, import tax, PIS/Cofins and others for firms with approved projects under Suframa (DL 288/1967); constitutional entrenchment (ADCT art. 40 and 92-A).
- **Who bore the costs:** the federal budget. FGV's table of tax collected by the Union in each state, as a share of the federal tax expenditure the state receives, has Amazonas at 1.74%; FGV says Amazonas returns more than it receives in that sense, and that only a few states, with Para lowest at 0.76%, return less than they receive. PIM firms add about R$6.0 bn a year in required counterparts, which FGV says lifts the Union's return to 2.24%, and about R$6.0 bn a year of ICMS to the state treasury ([FGV EESP](https://eesp.fgv.br/sites/default/files/files-eesp/relatorio_gastos_tributarios_e_zona_franca_de_manaus_v_18_de_junho2025.pdf)). FGV's method for the 1.74% was not checked [UNVERIFIED].
- **Counter-view:** the FGV report is itself the favourable side (it argues the real cost is lower than the headline; its other conclusions were not read in full [UNVERIFIED]). The critics (Senate TD 126 in note 03, and the Ipea-type cost-per-job argument) say the cost per job is high [UNVERIFIED, cost per job not opened].
- **What happened after:** the constitutional protection to 2073 means no sunset before then; the 2023 tax reform kept the ZFM advantage [UNVERIFIED].
- **Sources:** FGV EESP report; RFB DGT (links above).

## 7. Dividend tax exemption since 1996

- **Years:** profits earned from January 1996; Lei 9.249/1995 art. 10 (the planalto.gov.br and Camara texts were blocked; a 2021 Conjur article by Silber quotes the exemption for profits "apurados a partir do mes de janeiro de 1996" but writes the law number as 9.429/95, an evident typo: [Conjur 2021](https://www.conjur.com.br/2021-ago-20/silber-reforma-tributaria-tributacao-lucros-dividendos//?print=1); [Capital Aberto, Feb 2026](https://capitalaberto.com.br/insights/freitas-ferraz-advogados/2026/02/o-novo-regime-de-tributacao-de-dividendos-no-brasil-aspectos-juridicos-e-reflexos-da-lei-no-15-270-2025) confirms exempt since 1996 regardless of company size). Ended in part by Lei 15.270/2025, effective 1 Jan 2026 ([Capital Aberto](https://capitalaberto.com.br/insights/freitas-ferraz-advogados/2026/02/o-novo-regime-de-tributacao-de-dividendos-no-brasil-aspectos-juridicos-e-reflexos-da-lei-no-15-270-2025); dividends from profits up to fiscal year 2025 stay exempt; the 1 Jan 2026 date is from a LegJur snippet that returned 403 [UNVERIFIED]).
- **Government/ruler:** FHC (law of 26 Dec 1995 per the Camara URL slug [UNVERIFIED]); Lula III for Lei 15.270/2025.
- **What was transferred and how big:** profits distributed to owners escaped personal income tax. A study by Gobetti and Orair (Ipea) estimated in 2015 that a 15% tax on profits and dividends would raise R$43 bn a year, or R$50 bn in another version ([IHU/Unisinos](https://ihu.unisinos.br/546904-imposto-sobre-lucros-e-dividendos-recebidos-por-donos-e-acionistas-de-empresas-geraria-r-43-bi-ao-ano-diz-estudo), page opened; the R$50 bn variant is from a search snippet [UNVERIFIED]). The R$43 bn is 2015 money. Range: R$43 bn (IHU, opened) to R$50 bn (snippet, unopened) per year at a 15% rate, 2015 money, both by Gobetti and Orair.
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** owners and shareholders of companies. The IHU article also reports 71,440 Brazilians earning over R$1.3 million a year who declared an average of R$4.2 million and paid 6.7% in tax. A senator at a hearing compared a civil servant paying 27.5% on R$5,000 with an owner receiving R$300,000 in dividends paying nothing (Lindbergh Farias, as quoted in IHU, same source). Financed by employees and consumers through payroll and consumption taxes. The DGT does not report the dividend exemption as a tax expenditure because the STR treats it as outside the reference system (the DGT excludes items listed in [INESC](https://inesc.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/levantamento-inedito-mostra-renuncias-bilionarias-para-industria-petroleo-docx.pdf); dividends specifically: [UNVERIFIED]).
- **Price/terms vs value (who estimated):** Ipea (Gobetti and Orair) on the revenue; the claim that "only Brazil and Estonia fully exempt dividends" is a senator's statement at a hearing, reported by IHU; a search I ran did not confirm Estonia ([IHU](https://ihu.unisinos.br/546904-imposto-sobre-lucros-e-dividendos-recebidos-por-donos-e-acionistas-de-empresas-geraria-r-43-bi-ao-ano-diz-estudo)) [UNVERIFIED]. An OECD comparison was not opened [UNVERIFIED].
- **Legal mechanism:** art. 10 of Lei 9.249/1995 exempts distributed profits from withholding and from the recipient's tax base. The same law's art. 9 created interest on equity (JCP), deductible from corporate tax [UNVERIFIED, text not opened]. Lei 15.270/2025: 10% withholding when a company pays one resident individual more than R$50,000 in a month, applied to the whole amount and creditable in the annual return; non-residents pay 10% on any amount; a minimum tax (IRPFM) starts at R$600,000 a year and reaches 10% at R$1.2 million ([Capital Aberto](https://capitalaberto.com.br/insights/freitas-ferraz-advogados/2026/02/o-novo-regime-de-tributacao-de-dividendos-no-brasil-aspectos-juridicos-e-reflexos-da-lei-no-15-270-2025)). The 20% withholding with a R$20,000 microenterprise threshold belongs to the earlier PL 2337/2021, not the final law ([Conjur 2021](https://www.conjur.com.br/2021-ago-20/silber-reforma-tributaria-tributacao-lucros-dividendos//?print=1)).
- **Who bore the costs:** wage earners (labour income is taxed up to 27.5%, per the senator's example above) and the Treasury.
- **Counter-view:** the defence reported by IHU is that the exemption avoids taxing profit twice, at the company and again as personal income ([IHU](https://ihu.unisinos.br/546904-imposto-sobre-lucros-e-dividendos-recebidos-por-donos-e-acionistas-de-empresas-geraria-r-43-bi-ao-ano-diz-estudo)); the opposing argument is that the corporate rate cut in the same 1995 law and the JCP deduction already lower the corporate burden [UNVERIFIED]. The Capital Aberto lawyers' article is about the legal implementation, not the debate.
- **What happened after:** after about 30 years, Lei 15.270/2025 reintroduced a withholding ([Capital Aberto](https://capitalaberto.com.br/insights/freitas-ferraz-advogados/2026/02/o-novo-regime-de-tributacao-de-dividendos-no-brasil-aspectos-juridicos-e-reflexos-da-lei-no-15-270-2025)).
- **Sources:** Capital Aberto; IHU/Unisinos; LegJur; INESC (links above).

## 8. Repetro: customs regime for oil and gas equipment

- **Years:** Decreto 3.161/1999 (regulating art. 79 of Lei 9.430/1996) began the regime on infralegal acts; Lei 12.276/2010 and 12.351/2010 applied it to the pre-salt; Lei 13.586/2017 (arts. 5-8) extended it to 31 Dec 2040, from 2020 ([Conjur, 8 Jul 2025](https://www.conjur.com.br/2025-jul-08/repetro-impactos-da-reforma-tributaria-e-da-nova-lei-geral-aduaneira//?print=1), opened); INESC also says the 2017 law renewed it to 2040 and widened it ([INESC](https://inesc.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/levantamento-inedito-mostra-renuncias-bilionarias-para-industria-petroleo-docx.pdf)). Repetro-Sped and Repetro-Industrializacao are the current modalities (INESC).
- **Government/ruler:** FHC (1999) and Temer (2017) by date in office; neither page names the president [UNVERIFIED].
- **What was transferred and how big:** relief from import tax (II), IPI, PIS and Cofins (and the AFRMM freight surcharge) on equipment for oil and gas exploration and production (INESC). INESC crossed RFB data (obtained through the access-to-information law) with the 267 firms listed under Repetro: R$260.08 bn of incentives, exemptions and immunities in 2015-2023. By year, R$ bn: 2015 16.9; 2016 16.8; 2017 10.9; 2018 23.5; 2019 34.8; 2020 56.1; 2021 45.3; 2022 26.7; 2023 29.1 (all [INESC](https://inesc.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/levantamento-inedito-mostra-renuncias-bilionarias-para-industria-petroleo-docx.pdf)). Breakdown of the total: Cofins 107.7, II 114.3, PIS 23.0, IPI 9.3, Sudam/Sudene IRPJ 75% 5.6 (R$ bn).
- **Caveat on the number:** the table counts benefits received by Repetro-listed firms, including Sudam/Sudene and Zona Franca incentives, not only Repetro itself ([INESC](https://inesc.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/levantamento-inedito-mostra-renuncias-bilionarias-para-industria-petroleo-docx.pdf) says other routes exist). The Repetro-only part is therefore lower than R$260 bn, and the exact figure is not published [from the INESC text]. 2023 has no Sudam/Sudene data.
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** INESC states most of the 267 firms are transnationals; the top three include MODEC and Ventura Petroleo (INESC). Petrobras-operated fields produce 88-89% of Brazilian oil, in consortium or alone, so a share of the benefit also reaches Petrobras (same source). Financed by the Treasury; the beneficiaries' 2020 peak coincides with the oil and import cycle [not documented, UNVERIFIED].
- **Price/terms vs value (who estimated):** the Receita says it is technically impossible to compute Repetro as a deviation from the STR, so it does not appear in the DGT (INESC). INESC says the RFB holds robust data and could report it.
- **Legal mechanism:** special customs regime (suspension or exemption of taxes on goods entered for oil and gas activities, with re-export or permanent import options). Per Conjur: a special regime of suspension of federal taxes (II, IPI, PIS/Pasep, Cofins) on goods for oil, gas and hydrocarbon activities, which becomes exemption or zero rate once the goods are used as required; unused goods trigger payment with surcharges. LC 215/2025 kept Repetro among the relieved regimes ([Conjur](https://www.conjur.com.br/2025-jul-08/repetro-impactos-da-reforma-tributaria-e-da-nova-lei-geral-aduaneira//?print=1)).
- **Who bore the costs:** the federal budget; a "Repetro, renuncia fiscal bilionaria sem controle ou metas" article (Monitor Mercantil, 403 on fetch) makes the same criticism [UNVERIFIED, not read](https://monitormercantil.com.br/repetro-renuncia-fiscal-bilionaria-sem-controle-ou-metas/).
- **Counter-view:** the regime is defended as a way to avoid taxing imported capital goods that would be re-exported, which keeps Brazilian fields competitive; this position was not documented in a page I opened [UNVERIFIED]. The Receita's own treatment (not a deviation from the reference system) is the verified form of the same argument.
- **What happened after:** Lei 13.586/2017 raised the values from 2017 on (INESC: "values grew significantly from 2017"), though the table shows peaks in 2020 and a decline to 2022. The peak year is above the 2018-2019 base, which is consistent with INESC's claim, but the table alone does not prove cause.
- **Sources:** INESC (link above); Conjur and Monitor Mercantil (unread).

## Patterns

1. **The cost is measured by the party that gives it, with a moving baseline.** DGT numbers change with vintage (ZFM R$24.2 bn, 45.59 bn, 55.26 bn, 32.7 bn for 2021-2024), and the biggest regimes (Repetro, dividends, JCP, presumed profit) sit outside the series via the STR.
2. **Exemption first, compensation later or never.** Kandir waived a state tax in 1996, the compensation rule was not regulated for more than ten years after EC 42/2003, and the settlement of R$58 bn (2020-2037) is far below the loss estimates (R$268.9 bn to 2016).
3. **Rent is defined by the rate on revenue.** CFEM and oil royalties turn on the base (gross or net) and the rate; the 2017 CFEM reform moved the base and raised the iron-ore rate (how much of the later rise it explains is [UNVERIFIED]), while the TCU, as quoted by industry lawyers, finds heavy under-reporting.
4. **Amnesties repeat.** About 28 programs in 2000-2017 (TCU) or 40 in 18 years (Receita), each with longer terms and bigger discounts, which the TCU says punish those who paid on time.
5. **Entrenchment.** The ZFM benefits are in the Constitution to 2073 and Repetro runs to 2040, which makes later correction hard.
6. **The beneficiaries are often foreign or large.** Repetro's 267 firms are mostly transnationals (INESC); the PIM is dominated by informatics, electronics and two-wheelers.
7. **Counter-views are real and partly data-poor.** The industry's argument on CFEM and the Fazenda's on Kandir are documented only in part; those boxes remain open.
