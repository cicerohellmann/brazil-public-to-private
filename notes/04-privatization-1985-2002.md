## 1. Programa Nacional de Desestatizacao (Lei 8.031/1990) and the "moedas podres"

- Year / government: April 1990 law, Collor; expanded under Itamar (1993-94) and FHC (Lei 9.491/1997, Conselho Nacional de Desestatizacao).
- What: legal framework for selling or conceding federal firms. BNDES became the manager of the program.
- Payment mechanism: "moedas de privatizacao", federal debt securities accepted at face value in auctions. BNDES calls them heavily used in 1990-92, with more cash per deal from 1993-94 but "not less important". https://www.bndes.gov.br/wps/portal/site/home/transparencia/desestatizacao/processos-encerrados/Historico
- BNDES catalogued the regulation of "moedas, titulos e creditos" (two volumes, July and November 1991); a stated aim was to reduce public-sector debt. https://web.bndes.gov.br/bib/jspui/handle/1408/16900
- Academic view (Garcia, Unicamp): the Siderbrás sales brought US$6.33 bn in 1991-93 against US$7 bn expected, 65% from steel; "besides loans agreed with BNDES public money", the rush let "moedas podres" (public bonds worth less than face in the market) swell; cash was about 3% of the Siderbras sales (citing Passanezzi Filho 1992). https://www.eco.unicamp.br/images/arquivos/artigos/LEP/L11/LEP11_11_carlos.pdf
- Biondi's thesis [critical]: the "moedas podres" were themselves sold to bidders on long instalments financed by BNDES (the FPA review says up to 12 years). https://fpabramo.org.br/o-brasil-privatizado-um-balanco-do-desmonte-do-estado-2/ and https://siac.fpabramo.org.br/uploads/acervo/FPA_ColecaoBRU_LVR_0001.pdf. I did not find an independent BNDES/TCU document confirming the 12-year financing of the currencies themselves; treat as a claim to check.
- Regulator: none at first; sector agencies came later (ANEEL 1996/97, Anatel 1997, ANP 1997, ANTT/ANTAQ 2001; BNDES page only names ANEEL, ANTT, ANAC as concession agencies, with no dates).
- Who bore the cost: Treasury (discount on securities, BNDES credit), workers (see steel layoffs, case 4).
- Gap: share of "moedas" over the whole program (BNDES gives no total). Look at BNDES annual PND reports (1991-2002): https://web.bndes.gov.br/bib/jspui/handle/1408/3224

## 2. Reprivatizations of the 1980s (Sarney, Figueiredo tail)

- Year / government: 1981-89, mostly under Figueiredo and Sarney (the "Programa de Desestatizacao" of the 80s).
- What: 38 small companies, many that BNDES or banks had taken over for debts ("reprivatizadas"); about US$780 million total. Aim was to stop state expansion, not raise money. https://www.bndes.gov.br/wps/portal/site/home/transparencia/desestatizacao/processos-encerrados/Historico
- Buyers, prices per firm, regulators: not found. 
- Gap: this was the weakest-covered case; need a BNDES list of the 38 firms and the Sarney-era decree (Decreto 95.886/1988?; UNVERIFIED, from memory, check).

## 3. Usiminas (1991)

- Year / government: 24 Oct 1991, Collor. First sale under the PND. https://www.csmonitor.com/1991/0927/27071.html (CSM context on 1991 sales) and https://www.bndes.gov.br/wps/portal/site/home/transparencia/desestatizacao/processos-encerrados/Historico
- What: control of the Siderbras steel mill (Ipatinga, MG). Sources describe a 51% control sale to a consortium including Bozano Simonsen, steel distributors, employees, and Nippon Usiminas (Japanese mills). Source for buyers is a search summary of a secondary item; UNVERIFIED in detail.
- Price: US$1.49 bn raised, of which only US$39 million (about 2.6%) in cash, per Schmidt (1998) cited by Garcia. https://www.eco.unicamp.br/images/arquivos/artigos/LEP/L11/LEP11_11_carlos.pdf
- Valuation dispute: Schmidt, as cited, says the book equity of the privatized steelmakers exceeded the sale value by more than US$2.7 bn (same source). Garcia also cites Cotrim (1993): building mills of CSN's size would need over US$10 bn vs US$5.56 bn BNDES sale revenue for all steel firms.
- Financing: BNDES loans plus "moedas" (see case 1). Employee share about 10% of capital. 
- Costs: Usiminas employment fell from 13,838 (1989) to 8,448 (2000), -39%. Garcia argues many job cuts were driven by pre-sale restructuring and financial policy, not only technology. Same source.
- After: control fights among Nippon/Votorantim/Ternium (not in scope here).
- Regulator: none.

## 4. Siderbras steel: Cosipa, Acesita, CST, Acominas, CSN (1991-1993) and CSN 1993

- Year / government: Collor (Acesita 1992), Itamar (CSN, Cosipa, 1993). CSN auction April 1993.
- What: CSN control block, 60.1% of shares. https://clickpetroleoegas.com.br/a-privatizacao-da-csn-por-us-1-bilhao-o-leilao-que-transferiu-o-controle-da-maior-siderurgica-da-america-latina-btl96/ (secondary blog; US$1.057 bn).
- Buyers: Grupo Vicunha (Steinbruch), Bamerindus, Docenave (then a Vale subsidiary), same source. 
- Price vs value: Biondi's book [critical]: R$1.05 bn paid, of which R$1.01 bn in "moedas podres" sold by BNDES, financed over 12 years. https://siac.fpabramo.org.br/uploads/acervo/FPA_ColecaoBRU_LVR_0001.pdf (Currency mismatch between the two figures: US$ vs R$ in different sources; unresolved.)
- Costs: CSN employment 23,208 (1989) to 9,302 (2000), -59.9%; Cosipa 21,505 to 5,769, -73.1%; whole flat-steel sector -62.4% (1989-2000). https://www.eco.unicamp.br/images/arquivos/artigos/LEP/L11/LEP11_11_carlos.pdf
- BNDES sector total for 8 steel firms: sale revenue US$5.56 bn plus debts transferred US$2.63 bn. https://www.bndes.gov.br/wps/portal/site/home/transparencia/desestatizacao/processos-encerrados/privatizacao-federais-pnd
- Later: CSN's Vale stake story, see case 11.

## 6. Embraer (7 Dec 1994)

- Government: Itamar Franco.
- What: control of the state aircraft maker; edital PND-A-05/94 required a golden share held only by the Union. Bid reported as R$154 million after six postponements (Capital Aberto, https://capitalaberto.com.br/empresas/2008/05/sem-amarras); another report (Aviation Week) says 55.4% of voting shares for US$182.4 million (https://aviationweek.com/embraer-privatized). The two figures differ in currency and base: not reconciled.
- Buyers: Bozano Simonsen-led group; pension funds Previ and Sistel; DIEESE gives stakes Previ 12.2%, Sistel 5%, Bozano 9.9%, BNDESPar 13.9% (https://www.dieese.org.br/notatecnica/2018/notaTec198Embraer.pdf). Wasserstein Perella also named by AIN (https://backend.ainonline.com/aviation-news/business-aviation/2022-12-05/flashback-brazilian-banks-back-embraer-privatization).
- Legal mechanism: auction under PND, golden share (0.3% of capital, veto rights). The Union's golden share was the key lever in the 2018-19 Boeing deal, where the government approved it without using the veto. https://backend.ainonline.com/aviation-news/air-transport/2019-01-11/brazils-government-greenlights-embraer-boeing-deal
- Costs/after: not collected in this pass (the firm recovered and became a world exporter; the lead should decide whether it counts as a "good" outcome for contrast).
- Pattern: pension funds (state-company funds) and BNDESPar as part of the buyer group.

## 8. Light (21 May 1996)

- Government: FHC.
- What: Rio de Janeiro distributor. In Jan 1996 the company was split (LightRio vs LightPar for São Paulo), which freed it from a US$1.24 bn debt owed by Eletropaulo.
- Price: US$2.26 bn for the auction (Folha, via Wikipedia). Other sources say US$1.7 bn (Wikipedia on Light) or "over US$2 bn" (Congresso em Foco). Disputed/unreconciled. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_Servi%C3%A7os_de_Eletricidade and https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/4343/gestao-tucana-do-bndes-sob-suspeita
- Buyers: EDF, Houston Industries, AES (11.35% each, 34% total), CSN 7.25%, BNDESPar 9.14%; Eletrobras kept 30%.
- Later: Jan-Mar 2000 BNDESPar sold stakes to EDF (R$506.3 million for 9.23%); in 2001 CSN sold its stake to EDF and AES for US$362 million. Wikipedia citing Folha.
- Costs: debt transferred from the state side; later tariff history not researched (gap).

## 9. Eletropaulo (15 Apr 1998)

- Government: FHC (federal BNDES running the sale; São Paulo state under Covas owned the asset).
- What: major Sao Paulo distributor, bought through Lightgas (a vehicle created by Light's controllers) with AES, EDF, Houston/Reliant, CSN.
- Price: reports differ. One says sold at the minimum price with no capital contributed by the buyers, who borrowed from BNDES an amount equal to 50% of the minimum price plus a bank syndicate via a Cayman vehicle (https://www.eumed.net/cursecon/ecolat/br/11/vcg.htm, an academic-style item; no figures). Another says it was above the minimum "by several hundred million dollars" (https://www.latinnews.com/component/k2/item/22040.html). Contradiction unresolved. Price in reais: UNVERIFIED (~R$2 bn per a low-quality secondary source).
- TCU: found that BNDES exceeded its 50% limit by advancing money for debentures and shares (search summary of TCU ruling; the item itself was not opened). Congresso em Foco: https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/4343/gestao-tucana-do-bndes-sob-suspeita
- Collusion allegation: FT reported an AES-Enron agreement (April 1998) that Enron would not bid in exchange for gas contracts; AES denied it. https://www.ogj.com/general-interest/article/17257305/brazil-us-doj-to-investigate-aes-enron-collusion-suspicions
- After: AES defaulted on BNDES loans, about US$420 million unpaid in 2002 (same OGJ source); in 2003 BNDES renegotiated AES's debt above US$1 bn and took Eletropaulo shares in part payment (Congresso em Foco link above). The sale was later reversed in effect: BNDES ended up holding the assets (Brasiliana; JB: https://www.jb.com.br/economia/noticias/2007/03/14/amp/bndes-confirma-venda-de-acoes-da-brasiliana.html, not opened).

## 11. Companhia Vale do Rio Doce (6 May 1997)

- Government: FHC.
- What: 41.73% of voting shares (government kept 35.19% of ordinary shares) of the world's top iron-ore company, with Carajas and large mineral rights.
- Price paid: R$3.338 bn (about US$3.3 bn), 19.99% agio over the minimum. Wikipedia citing Folha: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatizations_under_the_Fernando_Henrique_Cardoso_presidency
- Estimated value (disputed):
  - Total company valued at US$9.3 bn (R$10.4 bn) at the time (Merrill Lynch-based, average of three months quotations); Congresso em Foco: https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/65831/privatizacao-sub-judice [critical-leaning press, reporting the TRF ruling]
  - Coppe-UFRJ report [commissioned by a Chamber committee]: ore reserves reported to the US SEC in 1995 were Carajas 4.970 bn t and Sistema Sul 7.918 bn t, but the edital used 1.8 bn t and 1.4 bn t (total gap 9.688 bn t); ore valued in situ rather than at the mine gate; Coppe estimated excluded reserves at R$1.094 bn to R$87.579 bn depending on method; 104 deposits valued at zero were worth US$114.5 million. https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/74159/principais-argumentos-contra-a-venda-da-vale
  - Conflict of interest: Merrill Lynch led the valuation and its subsidiary brokered for Anglo American, a rival bidder. TRF-1 judge Selene Almeida considered this impaired impartiality (same link).
  - Six valuers were paid R$63.7 million (Congresso em Foco, privatizacao-sub-judice link).
  - Titanium (92.5 Mt) and niobium (69.1 Mt) were not valued, per a DNPM document cited by the same article.
  - Defence side: BNDES/government argued the notice was approved by TCU; the STJ later relied on prior TCU approval (see lawsuits). I did not retrieve a TCU document itself: GAP.
- Buyers (Consorcio Brasil, via Valepar): CSN 16.30% (R$1.3 bn), Litel (Previ, Petros, Funcef, Fundacao Cesp) 10.43% (R$834.5 million), Eletron (Opportunity) 10% (R$800 million), Sweet River (NationsBank) 5% (R$400 million). Wikipedia citing Folha.
- Financing: critical press says BNDES gave subsidised financing to the winning group [critical] (https://www.cut.org.br/noticias/privatizacao-da-vale-25-anos-lucros-e-crimes-evidenciam-mau-negocio-para-o-pais-92a3/). Pension funds of state firms put in about R$834.5 million (Litel), shown above. No exact BNDES loan figure found: GAP.
- Legal mechanism: Lei 8.031 PND auction at the Rio stock exchange; Valepar created 9 Apr 1997.
- Regulator: none (mining stayed under DNPM).
- Costs: critics cite Brazil losing control of a strategic asset; Vale's market value later multiples of the price (R$84.4 bn by March 2006, over 13 times the control price, per Congresso em Foco). The 2006 market value is a market quote, not a valuation of what was sold: weigh accordingly.
- Lawsuits: dozens of ações populares from 1997; in Oct 2005 TRF-1 sent 69 suits back to Belem for technical expert review; 107 suits counted in 2007 (Estadao via Wikipedia); in 2010 STF Justice Gilmar Mendes suspended annulment cases. In Aug 2024 the STJ unanimously (rapporteur Mauro Campbell, IAC 7) held that the res judicata in one popular action binds all (erga omnes), applied the fait accompli doctrine and noted prior TCU approval; reported as ending 70+ suits. https://ofator.com.br/?p=13104 (single secondary source; look for the STJ's own page).
- Re-nationalisation: none. Annulment campaigns (e.g. Plebiscito Popular, 2007) unsuccessful.

## 12. Telebras breakup (29 Jul 1998)

- Government: FHC. Legal basis: EC 8/1995 (end of the state telecom monopoly), Lei Minima 9.295/1996 and Lei Geral de Telecomunicacoes 9.472/1997 (16 Jul 1997; created Anatel).
- What: 12 holdings auctioned in one day at the Rio stock exchange: 3 fixed-line, 1 long-distance (Embratel), 8 mobile (Banda A).
- Price: R$22.057 bn total, 63.74% agio over the government's combined minimum (about R$13.47 bn, my arithmetic). Per company (R$ and agio): Telesp 5.783 bn (64.28%); Tele Centro Sul 2.070 bn (6.15%); Tele Norte Leste 3.434 bn (about 1%); Embratel 2.650 bn (47.22%); Telesp Celular 3.588 bn (226%); others between 188 million and 1.36 bn. https://teletime.com.br/29/07/1998/telebras-e-vendida-com-agio-de-6374/ (trade press, contemporaneous)
- Buyers: Telefonica, Portugal Telecom, Telecom Italia, MCI (Embratel), Opportunity-linked groups, Brazilian consortium Telemar (AG Telecom/Inepar, with La Fonte) etc. (same source).
- Valuation dispute: the minimum price was set with BNDES-hired consultants (Brasilcom/Arthur D. Little won the BNDES contract: https://teletime.com.br/17/02/1998/brasilcom-e-d-little-vencem-licitacao-do-bndes/). Critics say sector assets were worth far more [critical: https://fpabramo.org.br/privatizacao-da-telebras-um-golpe-na-soberania-nacional/ and https://www.brasildefato.com.br/2019/03/07/nao-foi-a-privatizacao-que-ampliou-acesso-a-telefonia-foi-a-tecnologia]. I did not extract a number from them; do not quote "R$19 bn sold / R$11 bn BNDES loans", which appears in one interview and is a contested claim.
- Regulator: Anatel.
- Financing: see case 13 for Telemar.
- After: telephone access expanded (supporters credit privatization, critics credit technology: link above); Oi/Telemar went into judicial recovery in 2016 (outside this era; not sourced here).

## 13. "Grampo do BNDES" and the Tele Norte Leste/Telemar case

- Year: auction 29 Jul 1998; recordings published by Folha in Nov 1998-1999. - Money: Tele Norte Leste went to the Telemar consortium for R$3.434 bn (TCU document, quoted via search). A news site says "almost half a billion reais" to three companies: unreconciled (R$490 million vs R$686.8 million).
- MPF allegations: first-instalment funds came from BNDES, Banco do Brasil insurers and pension-fund-controlled firms, forming the consortium with "illegal loans". https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/economia/justica-confirma-que-privatizacao-de-teles-nao-teve-irregularidades-1gwnsm2tb0tzuu1fp7gvjk7ri
- TCU (Dec 1998): ordered BNDESPar to publicise financing in future sales, to analyse borrowers' finances, and to follow legal requirements (special auction) when selling Telemar shares. https://teletime.com.br/15/12/1998/tcu-analisa-relacao-bndespar-e-consorcio-telemar/
- Courts [defence outcome]: TRF-1 (judge Fernando Tourinho Neto) rejected the MPF improbity action in April 2010, relying on a TCU report that found no irregularity; "annulment would not serve the public interest". Defendants: Mendonca de Barros, Lara Resende, Pio Borges. Gazeta link above and https://www.conjur.com.br/2010-abr-28/trf-decide-nao-houve-improbidade-privatizacao-tele//?print=1 (Conjur says Previ's role was in the Opportunity consortium, not Telemar).
- Gap: transcripts quoted verbatim; there is no confirmed transcript in my sources.

## 14. End of the Petrobras monopoly: EC 9/1995, Lei 9.478/1997, ANP rounds

- Government: FHC.

- Petrobras itself was not privatised but had 2000 share sales: see case 15.
- Gap: royalties rules (up to 10%), "round zero" (Petrobras kept 115 fields), later pre-salt consequences. Worth a dedicated source.

## 15. Petrobras share sales and FGTS-funded Fundo Mutuo (Aug 2000)

- Government: FHC.
- What: workers could use up to half of their FGTS balance to buy Petrobras shares (with up to 20% discount per Suno); the fund held about 7.4% of ordinary shares (4.3% of capital). https://exame.com/economia/capitalizacao-petrobras-pode-tirar-r-2-bi-fgts-537619 and https://www.suno.com.br/noticias/?p=260177
- BNDES lists "Petroleo e Gas: Petrobras (sale of shares above the Union's controlling stake)": US$4.84 bn revenue. https://www.bndes.gov.br/wps/portal/site/home/transparencia/desestatizacao/processos-encerrados/privatizacao-federais-pnd
- Returns disagree by method: to Jan 2016 FGTS beat Petrobras funds (4.78% vs 4.32% per year, Economatica), while Genial to May 2022 shows +1,153% vs +185% (links in search summary: https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/economia/quem-colocou-dinheiro-do-fgts-na-petrobras-perdeu-dinheiro-b8djv4c3wzwukdnl1qdtkedps/ and https://www.bandab.com.br/economia/trabalhador-demitido-e-quem-se-aposentar-podera-vender-acoes-da-eletrobras-antes-tire-duvidas/amp/).
- Pattern: public asset share sold with workers' forced-saving money; the Union kept control.

## 16. Electricity crisis and the 2001 rationing ("apagao")

- Government: FHC, Jun 2001 to Feb 2002 (Camara de Gestao da Crise, GCE).
- Causes (disputed): delays in works, no long-term contracting rules, model changes, then drought (CanalEnergia/Acende Brasil report: https://acendebrasil.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/20110610_CanalEnergia.pdf). Union-leaning sources blame lack of supply investment and a ban on Eletrobras investing because of privatization plans [critical] (https://siac.fpabramo.org.br/uploads/acervo/Livro_Brasil-Cinco-Anos-de-Golpe_Cap26_2021.pdf).
- Official BNDES paper TD-97: compulsory consumption cuts of 20% (residential, commercial) and 20-25% (industry); in 1993 Lei 8.631 settled sector accounts at about US$26 bn paid by the Treasury; the 2001 General Agreement (Acordo Geral do Setor Eletrico, MP 14 of 21 Dec 2001, Lei 10.438 of 26 Apr 2002) set an Extraordinary Tariff Recomposition (RTE) of 2.9% a year for residential and 7.9% a year for industry, no raise for low-income users, with BNDES lending to the firms the amount to be recomposed. https://web.bndes.gov.br/bib/jspui/bitstream/1408/13816/2/TD-97%20As%20perspectivas%20do%20setor%20el%c3%a9trico%20ap%c3%b3s%20o%20racionamento._P_BD.pdf
- Who paid: consumers through RTE, taxpayers via BNDES finance (the paper states the mechanism; total amount not found: GAP). An uncited market blog says about 60% of the loss was passed to consumers: UNVERIFIED.
- Link to privatization: distributors (many privatized) lost revenue when consumption fell and were barred from raising prices, creating a liquidity crisis; the agreement shifted this to consumers and BNDES. 
- Gap: total cost to consumers (ANEEL/TCU figure). TCU did audit the agreement; to find.

## 17. PROER (1995-)

- Government: FHC (MP 1.179/1995, Nov 1995).
- What: Central Bank credit line for buyers of failing private banks (Nacional, Economico, Bamerindus etc.). 
- Cost: R$20.4 bn, 2.7% of average GDP 1995-97, per Maia (2003) cited in an academic paper (https://www.maxwell.vrac.puc-rio.br/6990/6990_2.PDF); FHC's own account says about R$30 bn to banks in 1995-2000, about 2.5% of GDP (same set of results). Unreconciled: different definitions.
- Per bank (uncorrected, Central Bank via press): Bamerindus R$3.2 bn, Economico R$5.2 bn, Nacional R$5.8 bn. https://congressoemfoco.com.br/economia/calote-de-bancos-socorridos-por-fhc-e-sete-vezes-o-recuperado-pela-lava-jato (critical framing). One report puts Nacional's outstanding PROER debt at R$20.659 bn; others (Bamerindus, Mercantil, Banorte, Pontual, Caixa) repaid. https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/economia/bancos-divida-proer/
- Buyer of Bamerindus: HSBC; of Nacional: Unibanco; (names from memory, UNVERIFIED in my sources except HSBC mention).
- Inquiry: CPI do PROER (Chamber, 51st legislature): https://www2.camara.leg.br/atividade-legislativa/comissoes/comissoes-temporarias/parlamentar-de-inquerito/51-legislatura/cpiproer/relatoriofinal/cap2proer.pdf (not read).

## 18. PROES and Banespa (2000)

## Patterns (for the lead)

1. Buyers were financed by the public sector: BNDES loans (Eletropaulo ~50% of the minimum price; Telemar ~20% of the control price in TCU's number), "moedas podres" bonds worth less than face (steel: about 3% cash by Garcia/Passanezzi; Usiminas US$39 million cash of US$1.49 bn), and state-company pension funds (Previ, Petros, Funcef, Sistel) in Vale, Embraer and telecom consortia.
2. The seller set a low minimum price and cleaned the debt first: debts moved to the Treasury or the state before sale (Light split from a US$1.24 bn Eletropaulo debt; RFFSA; steel debts transferred US$2.6 bn; state banks cleaned by PROES R$53 bn or more before sale; PROER for private banks). Valuation was done by consultancies with documented conflicts (Merrill Lynch/Anglo American in Vale).
3. The losses are socialised, the upside privatised: workers (steel -62% jobs 1989-2000; Banestado branches -76%), consumers (2001 tariff recomposition, toll rises) and taxpayers (PROER R$20-30 bn, PROES R$73 bn) paid; the buyers' later gains (Vale's rise, Telecom, distributors) were not shared. Distributors that were financed by BNDES later defaulted (AES, 2002) and BNDES took the assets.
4. Regulation came after or together with the sale and was weak or captured at first: no regulator for steel/Vale/petrochemicals; Anatel/ANEEL/ANP created 1996-97; rules on bidders' ties (BNDESPar financing the bidders) only tightened after TCU rulings.
5. Courts rarely undo anything: Vale had 100+ suits over 27 years and the STJ closed them in 2024 on fait accompli plus TCU approval; Telemar improbity case lost by MPF in 2010; CPIs blocked (Telebras 1999). The legal pattern is "TCU approval then fait accompli".
6. Same names recur: BNDES as seller, adviser and lender at once; Bozano Simonsen, Opportunity, Previ in the buyer groups; CSN/Vicunha; Merrill Lynch and Bradesco as valuers and then shareholders (Vale, per critics).
7. Foreign entry via constitutional amendments (EC 6, 8, 9 of 1995) that removed the legal barriers to foreign capital in minerals, telecom and oil.
