## 1. Amazon drought 2023–24 and the deforestation link

- **Date:** June–November 2023 (peak October 2023); drought continued into 2024.
- **Place:** Amazonas state (Rio Negro at Manaus, Lake Tefé), also Peru, Bolivia, Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador.
- **Operator/owner:** None (climate/land-use event). Private landholders and loggers drive the deforestation side; no single owner.
- **Deaths:** No human death count found [UNVERIFIED]. More than 140 river dolphins (botos) died in Lake Tefé (Mamirauá Institute, via Poder360).
- **People affected:** No consolidated number found [UNVERIFIED]. 55 of 62 Amazonas municipalities were put in emergency by the governor in September; 58 were in calamity/emergency by the article date.
- **Environmental damage:** Rio Negro at Manaus hit 13.49 m on 17 Oct 2023, lowest since records began in 1902. Surface water in the state fell to 3.56 million ha, down 1.39 million ha from Sept 2022 (MapBiomas).
- **Fines/settlements/cases:** None. No court case.
- **Counter-view / contested link to deforestation:** The World Weather Attribution study says climate change made the drought up to 30 times more likely and was the main driver, with El Niño less important; the IHU summary of it does not mention deforestation. MapBiomas attributes the drought to El Niño plus North Atlantic warming. So the "tied to deforestation" claim is NOT supported by these two sources; local land clearing may worsen it but I found no opened source quantifying that [UNVERIFIED].
- **Sources:** https://www.poder360.com.br/meio-ambiente/nivel-do-rio-negro-atinge-1349-metros-o-menor-da-historia/ ; https://ihu.unisinos.br/categorias/636264-crise-climatica-e-nao-el-nino-esta-por-tras-da-seca-mais-intensa-na-amazonia-diz-estudo-de-atribuicao

## 2. Guarani-Kaiowá: Guapoy massacre (Amambai, MS) and the wider killing pattern, 2022–2024

- **Date:** 23–24 June 2022 (Guapoy); CIMI data for 2023 published July 2024.
- **Place:** Tekoha Guapoy Mirim Tujury, Amambai, Mato Grosso do Sul.
- **Owner at the time:** Private landholders. The farm is reportedly Fazenda Borda da Mata, owned by Waldir Cândido Torelli, who sued Funai and the Guarani Kaiowá in 2018. The state military police acted.
- **Deaths:** 1 killed (Vítor Fernandes, 42, shot by military police, 24 June).
- **People affected:** At least 9 wounded; about 100 riot police and a helicopter were used; at least 2,000 attended the funeral (per Guarani Kaiowá leaders). Statewide: 43 indigenous people murdered in MS in 2023, second in Brazil after Roraima (47) and ahead of Amazonas (36) (CIMI via Agência Pública).
- **Environmental damage:** Not the focus; the issue is loss of traditional land to ranches.
- **Fines/settlements/cases:** Police acted without a reintegration-of-possession order. Federal prosecutors, federal public defender and the company agreed relatives could visit the grave; land not recognised as indigenous. A state court freed three wounded people held at a police station after public defenders argued federal jurisdiction. No conviction found [UNVERIFIED].
- **Counter-view:** The state government called the operation a response to a crime against property and life; the state security secretary said three officers were wounded. Indigenous leaders deny anyone was heavily armed (Cenarium report found via search, not opened). Death toll and dates vary across outlets (one source says 23 June).
- **Other:** CIMI says 1,381 land processes/demands exist, 850 with administrative omissions; demarcation of Panambi–Lagoa Rica (Douradina, MS), requested since 2011, is stalled; a chief was wounded by gunfire in July 2024.
- **Sources:** https://ihu.unisinos.br/?id=619944 ; https://apublica.org/2024/07/roraima-lidera-assassinatos-de-indigenas-com-47-mortes-diz-relatorio-do-cimi/

## 3. Paraná: paraquat drift at a rural school, Espigão Alto do Iguaçu (pesticide poisoning case)

- **Date:** 7 November 2018.
- **Place:** Boa Vista do São Roque community, Espigão Alto do Iguaçu, Paraná.
- **Operator/owner:** Private farmer (paraquat bought and used on the property of Lino Passaia, who said he was not there; a neighbour reportedly drove the sprayer).
- **Deaths:** None reported in the article.
- **People affected:** 96 poisoned, 52 of them children, at a school shared by a municipal and a state school.
- **Environmental damage:** Drift reached school, health post and homes; spraying at about 1 m height with booms set high; state rule requires 500 m from schools and homes.
- **Fines/cases:** Adapar said it would fine the farmer; neighbours filed a police report; the state Public Ministry said it was investigating a criminal case. No final ruling in the article [UNVERIFIED outcome].
- **Context numbers:** Sinan recorded 113 pesticide poisoning cases in Paraná 1975–2017 while the state health secretariat reported 4,761 victims 2007–2017 (the two figures differ in scope; both as given by the article).
- **Counter-view:** Farmer said he was absent; none other found in the opened article.
- **Sources:** https://apublica.org/2018/12/contaminacao-recorde-por-agrotoxicos-no-parana-atinge-mais-de-50-criancas/

## 5. Munduruku vs Teles Pires and São Manoel dams (Karobixexe/Sete Quedas), 2013–2017

- **Date:** Blasting reportedly 2013 [UNVERIFIED, single source]; occupation July 2017; standoff 13–16 October 2017.
- **Place:** Teles Pires river, border Mato Grosso/Pará.
- **Operator/owner (private concession):** Teles Pires: consortium of Eletrosul, Furnas, Odebrecht, Neoenergia (state-owned and private). São Manoel: EDP Brasil, Furnas (Eletrobras-controlled) and China Three Gorges.
- **Deaths:** None found.
- **People affected:** Munduruku number more than 13,000 in 112 villages; MPF says four indigenous peoples are affected by the Teles Pires dams.
- **Environmental damage:** The Karobixexe rapids were dynamited; 12 sacred urns were removed to a company-run museum in Alta Floresta; São Manoel destroyed Dekoka'a (Morro dos Macacos).
- **Cases:** The MPF says both companies face at least 24 judicial actions over licensing irregularities. About 80 Munduruku tried to occupy São Manoel on 13 Oct 2017; about 30 armed National Force police blocked them; MPF opened an inquiry into the National Force. Final outcomes not found [UNVERIFIED].
- **Counter-view:** Companies say they hold licences, followed the law, and found no impacts on sacred sites.
- **Sources:** https://news.mongabay.com/2017/10/munduruku-stand-off-against-amazon-dam-builders-potentially-explosive/

## 7. Raposa Serra do Sol (Roraima), violence and eviction of rice farmers, 2008–2009

- **Date:** Operation Upatakon 3 began 26 March 2008; STF suspended it 9 April 2008 (search summary); STF judgment 19 March 2009.
- **Place:** Terra Indígena Raposa Serra do Sol, Roraima (Makuxi, Wapixana, Ingarikó, Taurepang).
- **Owner:** Federal indigenous land; rice farmers occupying it were private landholders.
- **Deaths:** None found in the opened pages.
- **Violence:** A farmer threw a homemade bomb into a leader's home in Barro, injuring him; three bridges burned (Survival International, 7 April 2008).
- **Outcome:** STF voted 10 to 1 to keep continuous demarcation; Marco Aurélio Mello dissented; 19 conditions imposed; non-indigenous occupants, especially rice growers, must leave. Area 1.7 million ha (search summary only [UNVERIFIED]; the Congresso em Foco page gives no size).
- **Counter-view:** Roraima's governor and senators warned of "civil war" and challenged demarcation (search summary). Survival International is an advocacy source.
- **Sources:** https://www.survivalinternational.org/news/3200 ; https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/74311/supremo-mantem-demarcacao-da-raposa-serra-do-sol

## 8. Barra Grande dam (RS/SC): EIA that hid araucaria forest, 1998–2005

- **Date:** EIA 1998; preliminary licence 1999; works 2001; fraud exposed 2003; agreement 2004; operating licence 2005.
- **Place:** Rio Pelotas/Uruguai, border Rio Grande do Sul and Santa Catarina.
- **Owner (private consortium Baesa):** Wikipedia lists Alcoa 42.2%, CPFL 25%, CBA 15%, Camargo Corrêa Cimentos 9%, DME 8.8%; shares differ in other profiles (search summary).
- **Deaths:** None found.
- **Environmental damage:** The EIA by Engevix left out about 4,000 ha of native araucaria forest (O Eco); Wikipedia says about 40 km2 of araucaria cleared; another outlet says about 6,000 ha flooded (Sul21 snippet, not opened) [UNVERIFIED].
- **Cases:** 2004 agreement among federal government, Ministério Público and Baesa (mitigation/compensation). Engevix fined R$ 10 million and removed from Ibama's technical register; it appealed. A TRF4 ruling in April 2021 reported favourable to the plaintiffs, content not confirmed [UNVERIFIED].
- **Counter-view:** The environment ministry said the agreement dealt with administrative, not criminal, liability; a judge said the "possible fraud" should keep being investigated while the compensation met the project (O Eco snippets, not opened).
- **Sources:** https://oeco.org.br/tag/extras/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barra_Grande_Hydroelectric_Power_Plant

## Patterns

- Killings follow land disputes where the state stalls: Felisburgo, Colniza, Guapoy, Stang; mandantes are convicted late, if ever.
- Private consortia built dams with weak studies (Barra Grande EIA), then penalties land on the consultant or small fines.
- Sacred sites and indigenous land bear the cost of hydro (Teles Pires) and eucalyptus (Aracruz).
- Police sometimes act for landowners (Guapoy).
- The drought-deforestation link is not proven in the sources I opened.
