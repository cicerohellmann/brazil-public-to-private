## Hanna / MBR — additions: the 1961-62 cancellations and what the dictatorship did

- **Years:** 21 Aug 1961 (Quadros approval); 15 Jun 1962 (cancellation order); 29 Aug 1962 (court action); 23 Dec 1964 (Castelo Branco decree); 1965 (MBR); 1967 (case closed).
- **Government/ruler:** Quadros; Goulart parliamentary cabinet (Tancredo Neves, Mines and Energy minister Gabriel Passos); Castelo Branco; Costa e Silva.
- **Correction to the existing entry:** the 15 June 1962 cancellation was an order signed by minister Gabriel Passos (four days before he died), not a decree by Goulart himself. Before it, Quadros had approved (four days before his 25 Aug 1961 resignation) the finding that the deposits' registrations were void and that the deposits should become national reserve. Source (full timeline, with Diário Oficial references, 19 Jun and 22 Jul 1962): https://www.niepmarx.blog.br/MM/MM2017/AnaisMM2017/MC68/mc682.pdf [A]. CPDOC says the Goulart government on 15 June 1962 "determined the stop of Novalimense's activities and the expropriation of its deposits": https://www18.fgv.br/cpdoc/acervo/dicionarios/verbete-biografico/bulhoes-otavio-gouveia-de [A]
- **What was at stake:** iron/manganese/bauxite deposits that the English St John d'el Rey company held and passed to Novalimense (Hanna) in 1938, after the 1934 Constitution limited research/mining rights to Brazilians. The 1962 report listed Águas Claras and Capão do Xavier (Novalimense), Mutuca III (Mannesmann), Pico do Itabirito, Cata Branca (Novalimense/Icominas), Pitangui (Sobramil), São Luís and Bananal (Jair Porto) as legally irregular. Tonnage and hectares: not given in the sources I opened. [niepmarx above]
- **The fight:** Hanna and the Antunes group filed a joint writ of mandamus at the Tribunal Federal de Recursos on 29 Aug 1962, arguing that only a presidential decree, not a ministerial order, could void mining authorisations, and that the 1938 registrations had created vested rights for 24 years. The injunction was granted, then revoked about a year later; the firms appealed to the Supreme Court. [niepmarx]
- **What Castelo Branco did (confirmed):** on 23 Dec 1964 he signed a decree allowing private capital into subsoil exploration and extending to foreign capital the right to take part in Brazilian companies, excluding hydraulic reserves, oil, coal and nuclear minerals. CPDOC says the Hanna case then went to the Supreme Court, which sent the decision back to the federal government, and it was resolved only under Costa e Silva (1967). [CPDOC above]. A Capão do Xavier activist dossier says the Castelo government brought Caemi and Hanna together into MBR in 1965 [C/B, partisan]: http://www.capaoxaviervivo.org.br/destaquecpi.htm (found via search; I did not open the page, so [UNVERIFIED]).
- **How the cancellations were reversed:** I found no opened source that says a formal annulment of the 1962 order was signed after 1964. What the sources show is (a) a new legal framework (Dec 1964 decree, then the Mining Code of 1967, see existing entry), (b) a merger solution (MBR), and (c) a 1967 settlement whose terms I could not find. Terms of the 1967 settlement and who got which deposit: [UNVERIFIED].
- **Who got it / who financed:** Caemi (Antunes) and Hanna via MBR (see existing entry for shares). Hanna-linked figures held posts: the Unesp-type argument in the existing entry is repeated by niepmarx, which lists IPES members tied to the seven firms hit by Quadros/Goulart measures (Hanna, DEMA, SAMA, ICOMI, Sobramil, Arbed, Mannesmann) who later took posts under Castelo Branco. This is an interpretive (Gramscian) claim, not a finding. [niepmarx]
- **Price/terms vs value:** no price. The state gave no compensation figure that I could find. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Who bore costs:** the national mineral patrimony; not quantified.
- **Counter-view:** the companies argued the 1938 registrations were lawful and vested, that the 1962 order was done by despacho rather than decree, and the US ambassador Lincoln Gordon protested it as an attack on a US firm; Lucas Lopes (Novalimense president) called it "illegal". [niepmarx]
- **Sources:** URLs above.

## Albras / Alumar — additions: contract prices and subsidy

- **Years:** protocol 1976; tariff approved Aug 1979; contract signed Nov 1980 (one source) or 1984 (another); Albras start-up Jul 1985 (inauguration 24 Oct 1985); first term to 1 Apr 2004; renewal 1 May 2004 for 20 years.
- **Government:** Geisel, Figueiredo (setup); Lula (2004 renewal).
- **Price terms (original):** a 1976 protocol set a price variable with the aluminium price and a minimum of 8 mills/kWh; the tariff approved in Aug 1979 was 10.5 mills/kWh (US$10.5/MWh). Source: https://tecidosdenarrativas.wordpress.com/2012/03/13/a-historia-da-albras-grande-desconhecida/ [C, blog by a journalist; no primary document cited]. Dates of the contract differ between sources (1980 vs 1984): same blog vs https://lucioflaviopinto.wordpress.com/2026/05/29/a-verdadeira-historia-dol-aluminio/ [B, Lúcio Flávio Pinto].
- **Price terms before the 2004 renewal:** Albras paid about R$34 (US$11-12) per MWh; Alumar paid almost double. [B] https://valeqvale.wordpress.com/2012/09/27/o-maior-contrato-de-energia-do-brasil/ and the Pinto 2026 post above. Pinto gives US$12 in one place and US$11 in another (the text itself is inconsistent).
- **Price terms after 2004:** Albras won its own auction at the R$53/MWh ceiling (about 50% above the old tariff); Alumar's new tariff about R$60/MWh; contract value US$3.4 bn over 20 years (US$170 mn/year) [B] valeqvale. Eletronorte had wanted US$20-22/MWh; Albras offered about US$15 [B] https://www.infomet.com.br/site/noticias-ler.php?bsc=ativar&cod=20505 . Profit-sharing kicks in when aluminium exceeds US$1,450/t [same infomet page].
- **Subsidy size (estimates, all journalistic):** Pinto: subsidy 1984-2004 "over US$3 bn" and "equal to the plant investment of US$1.8 bn" [B Pinto 2026]. The aluminium plant's final cost was US$1.37 bn, 6.2% above the 1980 budget [C tecidos], so the "equal to the plant" claim depends on which cost figure is used. A figure of US$5 bn subsidy and Eletronorte losses of about US$200 mn/year circulated in a snippet I could not trace to an opened page: [UNVERIFIED].
- **Who bore the cost:** Eletronorte accumulated debt of R$5.6 bn by 2004, of which R$3.7 bn came from the subsidised tariffs, and lost about R$400 mn/year on them (Pinto, May 2004) [B valeqvale]; the gap would fall on other consumers [same].
- **Counter-view:** the design tied price to aluminium prices with a floor (8 mills) and later a profit-share clause; the contracts anchored demand for a dam that Eletronorte could not otherwise sell. Eletrobras' own filings describe the 2004 price as a floor at Tucuruí's break-even tariff plus an aluminium-price premium [A, filing with search-snippet only: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001439124/000129281409000877/eletrobrasfs2008_6k.htm , [UNVERIFIED] page not opened].
- **Not found:** the original 1978-80 contract text, and an Eletronorte/TCU cost-of-supply for those years.
- **Sources:** URLs above.

## Estatização of external debt (1974-85) — additions: mechanisms and numbers

- **Years:** 1967 (Res. 63) to 1985; main push 1974-78 (state firms), 1982-84 (deposit "projects" with the Central Bank).
- **Government:** Costa e Silva; Geisel; Figueiredo.
- **Two different instruments (important for the study):**
  1. **Resolução 63 (21 Aug 1967)** is not a transfer to the state. It let commercial banks, private investment banks and BNDE borrow abroad and on-lend to firms. Loans under it were about 7% of external debt in 1968, almost 20% in 1974 and 18.4% of the stock in 1980; in 1981 over one third of gross loans taken. https://www18.fgv.br/cpdoc/acervo/dicionarios/verbete-tematico/resolucao-63 [A]
  2. **Resolução 432 (23 Jun 1977)** (text opened: allows borrowers of foreign loans to make deposits in foreign currency at authorised banks, within Central Bank limits): https://www.bcb.gov.br/pre/normativos/res/1977/pdf/res_0432_v1_O.pdf [A]. With Circular 230 (1974) it formed the "depósitos registrados em moeda estrangeira": firms and banks deposited dollars owed before maturity at the Central Bank, which moved exchange and interest risk to the state. Source (citing Cruz 1995 and Herman 2005): https://www.abphe.org.br/arquivos/2015_suiani_meira_a-estatizacao-da-divida-externa-brasileira.pdf [A]
- **Numbers:**
  - Gross external debt US$3.9 bn (1965) to US$21.2 bn (1975). [Meira above]
  - Public loans as share of total loans: 35.3% (1974) to 60.2% (1978) (Cruz 1995, via Meira). [Meira]
  - Private share of "loans in currency" in 1972: 75%. [B] https://auditoriacidada.org.br/conteudo/cpi-da-divida-analise-tecnica-preliminar-no-5-divida-externa-capitulo-1/
  - Total external debt US$5.4 bn (1970) to US$85.5 bn (1983). [Auditoria Cidadã, advocacy body]
  - Public sector responsible for about 80% of external debt at the peak: PUC-Rio working paper (td121): [UNVERIFIED], URL returned 404 when opened; the claim came from a search snippet.
  - 1983-84 "project deposits": the Central Bank became borrower from the banks and guarantor of global debt, private and public; the deposits cost the Bank exchange-rate correction, interest and fees. [Meira, citing Carneiro 2002]. Agreements with the Central Bank as borrower: CGA-1983 US$4.4 bn and DFA-1983 US$4.3 bn. [Auditoria Cidadã]
  - Amortisations of 1985 (US$6.8 bn) and 1986 (US$8.9 bn) were deposited at the Central Bank. In 1983 the substitution of external with internal debt explains at least 52.1% of the federal government's additional borrowing. [A] IPEA, Cavalcanti 1988, https://repositorio.ipea.gov.br/server/api/core/bitstreams/1faac9f7-872f-4cd8-842f-baae55950e70/content (pdf text read locally)
  - 1985-87 further estatização explains 12% of the debt increase (Carneiro 2002 via Meira).
  - Later: Brady operation about US$50 bn, with US$28 bn of Central Bank liabilities transferred to the Treasury. [Auditoria Cidadã]
- **Who got it:** the private borrowers (industrial groups, banks) whose dollar obligations moved to the Central Bank. **Total US$ of private debt absorbed 1979-85: not found in any opened source** [UNVERIFIED]; the sources give channels and shares, not one absorbed-debt figure.
- **Who bore the cost:** the Central Bank balance sheet and then the Treasury, financed by monetary expansion and domestic debt (IPEA above); in the CPI da Dívida reading, taxpayers via the later Brady transfer.
- **Counter-view:** Meira and Cruz present the rise of public debt mainly as a result of the state firms' borrowing for the II PND and of the creditors' cartel and IMF conditions after 1982, not only as a rescue of private debtors; Carneiro (2002) says state firms' borrowing was the "traditional" channel. The Auditoria Cidadã chapter is advocacy and says the term "estatização" does not even appear in its text. [Meira; Auditoria Cidadã]
- **Sources:** URLs above.

## Proálcool: subsidies to sugar mills and distilleries

- **Years:** 1975-1989 (decline after 1989 crisis).
- **Government:** Geisel; Figueiredo.
- **What was transferred:** subsidised credit (negative real interest, 3 years grace, 12 years to repay) to mills and distillery builders; price parity with sugar for ethanol; Petrobras obliged to buy all alcohol; costs covered by levies on gasoline, diesel and lubricants. The credit terms are from a search snippet of a paper at https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/108140/files/189.pdf [UNVERIFIED, 403 when opened]. The levy mechanism: https://www.biodieselbr.com/proalcool/pro-alcool.htm [B, trade site].
- **Size (disputed):**
  - Deputy Fernando Ferro (PT), 1996: the programme consumed R$11 bn and left R$9 bn of debt, R$4 bn to the financial system and R$5 bn to Petrobras (Conta Álcool). https://adusp.org.br/wp-content/uploads/1996/08/r07a09.pdf [B, opinion piece by an interested politician; no method given]
  - A website puts government spending at about US$7 bn up to 1989 [UNVERIFIED snippet: https://www.portalsaofrancisco.com.br/geografia/proalcool ].
  - More than US$5 bn applied to agriculture and industry 1980-2002 [UNVERIFIED snippet, ageconsearch above].
- **Counter-view (benefits):** foreign-exchange savings of about US$11.5 bn 1975-2000 (trade site, biodieselbr above), output peaking at 12.3 bn litres in 1986-87. An FGV economics article reportedly says official claims of US$9 bn savings were reduced by a later study to under US$3 bn: [UNVERIFIED], PDF is a scan I could not read: https://periodicos.fgv.br/rbe/article/download/2637/6674/16894 .
- **Who got it:** mill/distillery owners (Ferro: "usineiros inadimplentes" sustained by the public purse). A list of named beneficiaries and loan-level data: not found.
- **Who bore the cost:** Petrobras (Conta Álcool), Banco do Brasil/financial system, gasoline consumers via levies (as per Ferro and biodieselbr).
- **What happened after:** 1989-90 supply crisis, MEG blend of 60% ethanol, 34% methanol, 6% gasoline; imports of ethanol and methanol above 1 bn litres in 1989-95. [biodieselbr]
- **Verdict:** the sector is clear, the money total is not. Needs a proper fiscal study (candidate: Unicamp repository item https://www.repositorio.unicamp.br/acervo/detalhe/438155 , not opened).

## Coroa-Brastel (1983)

- **Years:** 1981 (Caixa loan) to 1983 (intervention); trials into 2000s.
- **Government:** Figueiredo; ministers Delfim Netto (Planning) and Ernane Galvêas (Finance); Central Bank president Carlos Langoni.
- **What was transferred:** liquidity support and a Caixa Econômica Federal loan (1981) to Assis Paim Cunha's Coroa/Brastel group; the loan amount was **not found**. Central Bank made "several large liquidity injections"; Paim asked the Bank for a Cr$30 bn credit line, which forced an inspection. https://www.diariodocentrodomundo.com.br/delfim-netto-e-o-caso-coroa-brastel-um-dos-maiores-escandalos-financeiros-da-ditadura-que-bolsonaro-ama/ [B, opinion site]
- **Meeting:** 30 May 1983 Delfim told Langoni at Planalto that Paim had liquidity problems; Bank intervened in June 1983 and liquidated the group. [same]
- **Size:** author's estimate of the hole about US$400 mn; about 35,000 investors held the letters; Paim's group "invoiced US$1 bn/year". [DCM above; pt.wikipedia https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esc%C3%A2ndalo_Coroa_Brastel [B]]. Another estimate of R$250 mn (updated) and 34,000 people: [UNVERIFIED snippet].
- **Who got it:** Paim's financial/brokerage group (Coroa, Brastel, Laureano brokerage linked to a Golbery family connection per DCM, not a finding).
- **Legal mechanism:** Central Bank assistance lines and a Caixa loan; regulators suspended inspections. [DCM]
- **Who bore the cost:** letter holders lost most, since Coroa letters had no public guarantee (DCM). Later, the Treasury: in March 2014 the STF's 1st panel (3-0) held the Central Bank's rescission action time-barred, leaving a judgement for brokerage PEBB of about R$400 mn to be paid by the Treasury. https://jornalggn.com.br/justica/tesouro-devera-pagar-indenizacao-em-caso-coroa-brastel/ [B]
- **After:** Paim confessed to the fake issues and got 8 years 3 months, appealed free; Delfim and Galvêas were accused of diverting funds via the Caixa loan and acquitted by the STF (DCM; pt.wikipedia says charges were filed in 1985).
- **Counter-view:** the DCM author says it cannot be proved that Delfim directly favoured Paim and found no wrongdoing by Langoni.

## Capemi (military pension fund) — Agropecuária Capemi, Tucuruí timber

- **Years:** about 1980-83.
- **Government:** Figueiredo.
- **What:** Capemi (Caixa de Pecúlios dos Militares) set up Agropecuária Capemi to clear and sell timber in the future Tucuruí reservoir, Pará. About a quarter of the contract was executed ("only 10% of the work" in another sentence of the same page); a federal forestry agency (IBDF) cancelled the contract in March 1983. https://grandesfraudes.wordpress.com/2016/05/11/capemi-delfimnetto/ [C, blog; the page links Delfim Netto only in tags, not in the text]
- **Financing:** US$100 mn loan from Banco Nacional de Paris, US$25 mn guaranteed by BNCC, about US$27 mn of external resources in total. [same]
- **Alleged diversion:** at least US$10 mn, possibly US$20 mn, through contracts with firms linked to one person. This is the blog author's estimate. [same]
- **Who bore the cost:** the pension fund members and the state-backed guarantor; an intervention and a 1980s CPI followed (per the same page).
- **Status:** weak sourcing. No bailout amount found. Do not conflate with the "Delfin" case below.

## Delfin (BNH) debt swap, 1982-83

- **Years:** Dec 1982 deal; Jan 1983 intervention; 1991 agreement; Mar 2006 STJ.
- **Government:** Figueiredo; Minister Mario Andreazza (Interior, in charge of BNH).
- **What was transferred:** Delfin (Ronald Levinsohn) owed the Banco Nacional da Habitação about Cr$70 bn (about US$250 mn), Cr$77 bn at intervention, and offered two plots (Barra da Tijuca in Rio, Cotia in São Paulo) at Cr$60.8 bn. BNH technicians valued them at about Cr$6.3 bn, roughly one-sixth; a BNH director resigned. https://www.diariodocentrodomundo.com.br/morre-ronald-levinsohn-protagonista-de-um-dos-maiores-casos-de-corrupcao-da-ditadura-amigo-de-jornalistas-e-de-juizes/ [B, opinion site]. Another account has land valued at Cr$9.6 bn: [UNVERIFIED snippet], so the range of the bank's valuation is Cr$6.3-9.6 bn vs the Cr$60.8 bn accepted price.
- **Who got it:** Delfin group, which had about 3.5 million savings depositors. The swap was approved with the backing of Andreazza, with Delfim Netto and Galvêas also involved (DCM; a claim of that source).
- **Legal mechanism:** BNH settled a debt in kind. The swap came out in Folha de S.Paulo on 30 Dec 1982 (José Carlos de Assis); depositors ran on 3 Jan 1983; the Central Bank intervened and moved accounts to Caixa; O Globo said "irregular operations diverted Cr$6 bn" of depositors' funds. [DCM]
- **Who bore the cost:** BNH (a body fed by FGTS) bore the difference between debt and land value; the sources do not say how losses were finally allocated.
- **After:** in 1991 Levinsohn agreed to pay the rest in 13 years and, per DCM, no instalment was paid; in March 2006 the STJ accepted the payment with the two plots (on a "potential value" basis). [DCM]
- **Counter-view:** the defence's potential-value valuation, which the STJ accepted, treats the land as worth more once built on. Some sources say the STF, not STJ, ruled: sources disagree, [UNVERIFIED].

## Sulbrasileiro, Comind, Auxiliar, Maisonnave (1985)

- **Years:** 7 Feb 1985 (Sulbrasileiro); May 1985 (Meridional); 19 Nov 1985 (liquidations).
- **Government:** Figueiredo to Sarney.
- **What happened:** the Central Bank intervened in Sulbrasileiro on liquidity grounds; in May 1985 the federal government created Banco Meridional do Brasil and expropriated Sulbrasileiro's shares by decree-law (number printed as 7315/1985 in the English Wikipedia entry, which looks odd: [UNVERIFIED]); assets passed to Meridional included about 15,000 items: 5,000 beach plots on ten beaches and 80,000-90,000 hectares of rural property. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_Sulbrasileiro [B]
- **Maisonnave/Comind/Auxiliar:** the three owed US$455 mn raised abroad; Maisonnave group got US$20 mn from the Central Bank in a rediscount line; the Bank vetoed an American Express partnership; on 19 Nov 1985 the government decreed liquidation of Maisonnave, Comind and Auxiliar the same day by decree-law (presented as the only time Sarney used a decree-law to revoke bank charters). Estimated combined deficit: 6.84 trillion cruzeiros (cited to Jornal do Brasil 20 Nov 1985; a JB comment said that could have built about 390,000 low-income homes); a Maisonnave debt of R$406 mn was still on the books in a CMN record of 27 Nov 1997. https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_Maisonnave [B]
- **Who bore the cost:** for Sulbrasileiro, taxpayers via a federally created successor (state expropriated shareholders; one headline cited in English Wikipedia says shareholders would not be compensated, unclear). For the three Nov 1985 banks, creditors and clients; the sources do not give a Treasury cost. Delfin, Coroa and these banks are not covered by any single audit I found.
- **Counter-view:** the Meridional solution was pushed by Rio Grande do Sul politicians (pt/en Wikipedia), i.e. a political rescue of a regional bank rather than of owners; Comind/Auxiliar/Maisonnave were liquidated rather than rescued (so this is less a transfer than a failed rescue).
- **Gap:** no verified total public cost for 1983-85 bank rescues. Candidate source: Garcia 1990 and Maldonado Filho 1985 (cited in Wikipedia, not opened). Another academic PDF: https://lume.ufrgs.br/bitstream/handle/10183/33270/000114367.pdf (not opened).

## 1975 Brazil-Germany nuclear deal and Nuclebrás

- **Years:** Nuclebrás created Dec 1974; agreement signed in Bonn 27 Jun 1975 (in force 18 Nov 1975); scaled back from 1978; jet-nozzle company liquidated 1993.
- **Government:** Geisel (project run by Paulo Nogueira Batista at Nuclebrás).
- **What:** two 1,300 MW PWRs by 1985 with an option for six more by 1990; enrichment (jet nozzle), fuel fabrication, pilot reprocessing; up to 20% of Brazilian uranium ore to Germany. Value: about US$10 bn at 1975 prices (Atlas FGV): https://atlas.fgv.br/verbete/5722 [A]. Another figure: up to DM10 bn, about US$4 bn (Gray 2012 via pt.wikipedia, https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programa_nuclear_brasileiro [B]). The sources therefore give a range of US$4-10 bn; the larger figure is German export projection (DW via https://www.forte.jor.br/2025/04/14/o-fracasso-do-acordo-nuclear-entre-o-brasil-e-a-alemanha/ [B]).
- **Who got it:** Kraftwerk Union/Siemens (reactors), German banks (loans), Nuclep (heavy equipment, created 1975 with Germans). Contract-level terms and prices: [UNVERIFIED]. An amount of Brazil paying DM2.25 bn and a German loan of DM4.3 bn appears in a snippet only: [UNVERIFIED].
- **Technology claim:** the jet-nozzle enrichment was unproven; many scientists thought it costlier than centrifuges (Wilson Center): https://wilsoncenter.org/publication/brazils-1975-nuclear-agreement-west-germany [A].
- **Who bore the cost:** Brazilian Treasury and Eletrobras/Nuclebrás (state financing). Costs "ballooned with little demonstrable result" (Wilson Center). Angra 2 (planned 1985) only operated in 2000/2001; Angra 3 under construction since 1986 has consumed more than R$20 bn (DW/Forte, 2025). Nuclebrás' total debt: not found.
- **Counter-view:** the aim was energy security after the 1973 oil shock and the US cancelling enriched uranium for Angra 1; it became a parallel autonomous Navy enrichment programme from 1979 (Atlas FGV). Of eight planned plants, one runs and one is unfinished (Forte).
- **Legal control:** a parliamentary inquiry (CPI) was convened about 1978 on the agreement; its report is cited but I did not open it. [UNVERIFIED]

## Itaipu: contracts and beneficiaries

- **Years:** treaty 26 Apr 1973; company May 1974; plant inaugurated 1984; debt paid off Feb 2023.
- **Government:** Médici (treaty), Geisel, Figueiredo.
- **Cost:** budgeted at US$10.3 bn; about US$16.2 bn estimated in 1991. https://www18.fgv.br/cpdoc/acervo/dicionarios/verbete-tematico/itaipu-binacional [A]. Itaipu later gives about US$12.5 bn (nominal construction including financing rollovers): https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/republica/usina-itaipu-divida/ [B]. A total of US$63.5 bn paid over the debt's life appears in a Gazeta do Povo snippet: [UNVERIFIED].
- **Who financed:** Eletrobras (the main creditor: US$16 bn of US$19.8 bn debts in the 1997 renegotiation at 7.5% a year); BNDES, Banco do Brasil; originally about 70 financiers with Treasury guarantees. [CPDOC; Gazeta]
- **Who got the contracts:** civil works to the Brazilian consortium Unicon (five firms) and the Paraguayan Conempa (six), 1975 contract. [CPDOC]. Unicon's members as per Itaipu's transparency page (Cetenco, CBPO, Camargo Corrêa, Andrade Gutierrez, Mendes Júnior): [UNVERIFIED], I did not open the page. Contract prices: not found; a 2020s court ruling ordering Itaipu to pay Unicon R$28 mn in labour-deposit refunds is a separate matter: https://www.lex.com.br/mantida-condenacao-a-itaipu-binacional-para-ressarcir-o-consorcio-de-construtoras-unicon-em-r-28-milhoes-referentes-a-indenizacoes-trabalhistas/ [UNVERIFIED snippet].
- **Legal mechanism/price rule:** tariff "pelo passivo", covering operating costs and debt service, so debt costs go directly to consumers. In 2018 "linked energy" sold at US$43.80/MWh against US$6.06 for unlinked energy. [Gazeta]
- **Who bore the cost:** Brazilian and Paraguayan consumers through the tariff; Brazil bought Paraguay's surplus until 2023, with the 2009 deal raising Paraguay's compensation by 200% (Brazil's payments from about US$120 mn to US$360 mn a year by 2011, per CPDOC). Utilities' arrears of about US$4 bn in the early 1990s and a Paraguayan dispute over roughly US$4 bn of the debt. [CPDOC]
- **Counter-view:** Itaipu is a working, amortised asset (record 98,630 GWh in 2013, CPDOC); the controversy is the debt's dollar charge and the contractors' margins, which I could not document.
- **Sources:** URLs above.

## Calha Norte (1985)

- **Years:** 1985-1990 first phase.
- **Government:** Sarney (the first civilian government; the plan was prepared by the dictatorship's Conselho de Segurança Nacional, a link made in the sources below: "último projeto da ditadura").
- **What:** a military and settlement programme on a 160 km strip along the northern borders, 6,500 km long, 1.2 million km2 (about one quarter of the Legal Amazon), 1.6 million people. Planned budget 10,787,165 OTNs through 1990; the total spent was unknown at the time. https://fpabramo.org.br/1989/04/01/calha-norte-alem-das-fronteiras/ [B, Teoria e Debate 1989, authors critical of the project]
- **Drafted:** secretly in military offices, coordinated by Gen. Rubens Bayama Denys, head of the Casa Militar. [same]
- **Who benefits (claim):** the authors say the strip holds large gold, diamond and manganese deposits and that a government survey says 40% of the region is exploited by foreign companies; this is their interpretation. The Cimi said the plan would affect 50,000 Indigenous people from 33 nations. [same]
- **Money later:** a Brasil de Fato piece says the budget jumped from R$1.2 mn to R$24 mn the following year [UNVERIFIED snippet: https://www.brasildefato.com.br/2023/12/26/ultimo-projeto-da-ditadura-mantem-sob-gestao-da-defesa-r-3-bi-para-obras-em-10-estados/ ].
- **Counter-view:** the programme's stated aims were border protection, settlement, a bilateral policy and an Indigenous policy [UNVERIFIED snippet, Wikipedia pt]. No source I opened shows a concession of land or mining rights to a named company under it. So this is a "militarised frontier plus mining-access" argument, not a documented transfer. Treat as a thin case.
- **Gap:** budget in reais, who built what, and any mining titles.

## Public land in Mato Grosso and Pará: sales to colonisers and companies

- **Years:** 1950s-1990.
- **Government:** state of Mato Grosso; federal INCRA and Sudam.
- **Hectares (all from Moreno 1999, via a 2017 academic summary):** https://governancadeterras.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Lilian-da-Rosa-Aspectos-Historicos-da-ocupacao-em-MT.pdf [A, PDF text read locally]
  - 1950-64: colonisers got on average 200,000 ha each at Cr$7-10/ha and resold at Cr$100-300/ha, above the 100,000 ha limit that needed Senate approval.
  - The state land department (DTC) was closed in 1966 after repeated fraud.
  - Decree-Law 1.164/1971 put about 60% of Mato Grosso's land under federal control until 1978.
  - 1978-90: the state company CODEMAT collected more than 6 million ha of public land and sold more than 5 million ha in the north (Aripuanã, Juruena, Alta Floresta, Juína...). INTERMAT collected about 6.4 million ha; INCRA more than 7 million ha, and titled about 3.6 million ha along federal highways.
  - Private colonisers SINOP, INDECO and CONTRIGUAÇU got large areas and resold lots of 100-300 ha to settlers from the South.
  - Titles above the constitutional limit of 3,000 ha were arranged through powers of attorney to nominee buyers ("laranjas").
- **Who financed the buyers:** Sudam tax incentives (see Codeara below) or the buyers' own resale; prices per hectare for 1970s sales: not found.
- **Programmes named "Terra Pública"/INCRA direct sales to companies:** not found in any opened source. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Pará:** I found only a Pará example in a snippet (Companhia de Terras da Mata Geral financing a company by selling land and timber): [UNVERIFIED] https://periodicos.ufpa.br/index.php/pnaea/article/download/11412/7867 . No hectare series for Pará.
- **Maranhão side note (Sarney land law):** the state's Companhia Maranhense de Colonização (COMARCO, December 1971) immediately took in 2.1 million ha of public land. https://entropia.slg.br/index.php/entropia/article/download/332/355 [A, PDF text read locally]
- **Who bore the cost:** the public domain, and the Indigenous and posseiro occupants; settlers paid high resale prices.
- **Counter-view:** the sales were also how the state populated the north; the authors' main complaint is the lack of control, fraud and speculation, not the settlement itself.

## Fazenda Suiá-Missu — additions: the sale chain to Liquigás/Agip

- **Hectares in the sources:** Sudam table 695,843 ha with Cr$7,878,000 in incentives (above, Campo-Território). Other figures: 500,000 ha per Sudam and 670,000 ha per Ianni (1979), and about 800,000 ha after Ariosto da Riva joined the Ometto group, per a NAEA working paper: https://periodicos.ufpa.br/index.php/pnaea/article/download/11370/7839 [A, PDF read locally]. De Olho gives 696,000 ha.
- **Sale chain:** the Ometto family bought the area in 1962; in the 1970s they sold most to Liquigás, owned by the Italian Ursini group, which renamed it Liquifarm Agropecuária Suiá-Missú S.A.; Ursini failed in 1978, and in 1979-81 Agip do Brasil absorbed Liquigás and Liquifarm under the state ENI/Agip Petroli. [Campo-Território above]. Price paid by Liquigás: not found.
- **Later:** part was sold to the Brazilian Garavelo group in 1989 (250,000 ha) [Campo-Território]. In 1992 FUNAI delimited a 200,000 ha Marãiwatsédé Indigenous Land, of which 168,000 ha inside the farm; at Rio-92 ENI promised to return about 165,000 ha, which did not happen because the Brazilian subsidiary's shareholders disagreed; the area was then invaded by posseiros. [NAEA; Campo-Território]
- **Who got it / financed:** Sudam tax incentives paid by the Treasury to the Ometto-owned company; a state-owned Italian oil group became owner by absorbing a bankrupt private one (the state-ownership link: ENI is Italian state-controlled; see Campo-Território).
- **Counter-view:** the sources say the sale chain was private-to-private; the public transfer was the incentive and the title, not the 1970s sale. Whether the land was federal or state public land at origin is disputed: Campo-Território describes fraud in the 1960 registration and later usurpation, but no court finding is cited in the text I read.

## Patterns

1. **Rules first, deals second.** In Hanna's case the sequence is: cancellation (1961-62), new framework (Dec 1964 decree, 1967 code), merger (MBR) and a 1967 settlement. The reversal is legal engineering, not a single restoring act, and I could not find the 1967 terms.
2. **Public infrastructure sold forward in price.** Tucuruí power for aluminium (8 to 10.5 mills in 1976-79, about US$11-12/MWh by 2004) and Itaipu's passivo tariff both move the cost to other consumers; the sources give debt and subsidy sizes only as journalistic estimates.
3. **Risk is nationalised, upside is not.** Resolution 432 deposits and the 1983-84 project deposits shifted exchange and interest risk to the Central Bank, and the Brady transfer (US$28 bn of Bank liabilities to the Treasury) finished it. No source gives the absorbed private-debt total.
4. **Rescues of connected financiers recur.** Coroa-Brastel (Central Bank injections, Caixa loan, US$400 mn hole), Delfin (BNH debt of Cr$70 bn settled with land valued at a sixth to a quarter... of Cr$60.8 bn: Cr$6.3-9.6 bn), Sulbrasileiro (state successor Meridional). The courts later acquitted ministers (Coroa-Brastel) or accepted the swap (Delfin), and the Treasury paid a R$400 mn judgement.
5. **Land: the paper was the product.** In Mato Grosso, millions of hectares of public land passed through state companies and nominee buyers (CODEMAT sold more than 5 million ha), and Sudam incentives paid for farm projects of 196,000-696,000 ha.
6. **Data gaps run in the same direction.** For the transfers that benefit private firms (Itaipu contract prices, Nuclebrás contracts, Proálcool beneficiaries, Caixa loan to Coroa, Calha Norte spending) the totals are either missing or only in advocacy sources. Next step: the CPI do Acordo Nuclear report, TCU and Eletrobras records for Itaipu and Tucuruí, and the Central Bank's 1980s annual reports.
