## Encilhamento (1890-91)

- **Years:** 1889-1892 (peak 1890-91).
- **Government/ruler:** Provisional Government under Deodoro da Fonseca; Finance Minister Rui Barbosa (left the ministry in Jan 1891).
- **What was transferred and how big:** Not a single asset but a license to create money and companies. The law of 17 Jan 1890 created three regional emission banks (Norte, Centro, Nordeste), authorized to issue up to 450,000 contos, "more than double the paper money in circulation"; notes were inconvertible and backed by public-debt bonds. Decrees 164 and 165 of 17 Jan 1890 cut the paid-in capital needed to trade shares from 20% to 10%. Source: https://seer.ufrgs.br/AnaliseEconomica/article/download/74976/54428. Money supply rose 3.5 times from 1889 to 1894 (Franco & Lago 2012, cited in the same source). 14 Rio banks were founded in 1889 (same source). Listed companies: about 90 in 1888, about 450 in 1891 (Franco & Lago 2011, via https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encilhamento). Note issue of 335,000 contos in 1890-91 appears in a thesis quote [UNVERIFIED, source not traced].
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** Per Nassif/Franco/Levy as summarized at https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encilhamento: ex-slaveholders and coffee rentiers (liquidity after abolition), bankers such as Mayrink, and British banks (Rothschilds). Banco Nacional do Brasil shares traded at up to a 45% premium (Levy 1980, same page). Financed by the new notes themselves.
- **Price/terms vs value:** Exchange rate in pence per 1,000 reis: 27 (Nov 1889), 8 (1892), 12 (1902), per https://www.cobdencentre.org/?p=26973 (partisan Austrian-school blog; its own "337%" figure is a miscalculation of 27/8, so only the raw rates are used). Money supply +40% by Sept 1890 (same blog). Furtado, quoted in the Analise Economica article: paper money in circulation fell from 216,000 to 197,000 contos between 1880 and 1889 while foreign trade grew from 411,000 to 477,000 contos, i.e. the starting shortage was real.
- **Legal mechanism:** Decree-laws of the Provisional Government (banking law 17 Jan 1890; company-formation decrees 164/165); Decreto 1.154 of 7 Dec 1890 merged the banks into the Banco da Republica, allowed to issue three times its reserves (Cobden, above; decree via https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/391295/publicacao/15813363, search result only [UNVERIFIED]).
- **Who bore the costs:** Small investors who lost in the crash (pt.wikipedia, above); consumers through inflation and devaluation; the Treasury, which funded rescue via foreign borrowing: Rothschild loans totalled £7.5m from 1894 (Cobden, above). The Baring crisis of Nov 1890 raised Brazil's risk premium in London (Triner & Wandschneider, cited in the Analise Economica article).
- **What happened after:** Bubble burst 1891-92; Rui's banks were merged and liquidated; the 1898 funding loan followed (already in 02).
- **Counter-view:** Stein, Fishlow, Suzigan, Versiani and Lobo argue the credit helped textile and industrial formation and that devaluation protected industry; Fonseca calls Rui a precursor of developmentalism (Analise Economica article, above). Critics: Taunay 1923, Furtado, Almeida & Croce 2016 (same).
- **Sources:** https://seer.ufrgs.br/AnaliseEconomica/article/download/74976/54428 ; https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encilhamento ; https://www.cobdencentre.org/?p=26973

## Coffee valorization (1906-1922) - additions

(Taubate is already in 02; this adds the loans, stock and later rounds.)

- **Years:** 1906-1922.
- **Government/ruler:** State of Sao Paulo (Tibirica), then the Union from 1908 (guarantee), Epitacio Pessoa 1919-22.
- **What was transferred and how big:**
  - Taubate Convention signed 26 Feb 1906 by Tibirica, Francisco Sales and Quintino Bocaiuva: minimum price 55-65 gold francs per 60 kg bag, £15m loan sought, 3-franc gold surtax per bag, Caixa de Conversao. 1906 crop about 16m bags; world supply 29.6m vs consumption 16m. Source: https://www.rbgdr.net/revista/index.php/rbgdr/article/download/154/126.
  - Loans per the US report: £1m Treasury notes 7 Aug 1906 (net £919,000); £3m from J. Henry Schroeder & Co. and National City Bank (contract 14 Dec 1906); a further £3m under N.M. Rothschild & Sons dated 28 Jan 1908; final £15m loan guaranteed by the Federal Government, 10 years to 1 Jan 1919, authorized 25 Aug 1908 (Law 1.127, https://www.al.sp.gov.br/repositorio/legislacao/lei/1908/lei-1127-25.08.1908.html, search result only). Peak stock 8,474,623 bags at cost £17,227,243. Surtax raised from 3 to 5 francs. Last sales Apr 1911 at 73-75 francs per 60 kg. Source: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1913/d37 (OCR is garbled; some figures uncertain).
  - 1917: Decreto 3.316 of 16 Aug 1917 authorized up to 300,000 contos of paper emission, 150,000 earmarked for coffee defense; only 110,000 contos reached Sao Paulo, which bought about 3.1m bags at 4$900 per 10 kg. Paper-money stock rose from 896.8 thousand contos (end 1913) to 1,700.1 thousand (end 1918). Source: https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/119179/1/MPRA_paper_119179.pdf.
  - 1920-22: Decreto 4.182 of 13 Nov 1920 authorized 50,000 contos of emission and a Banco do Brasil Carteira de Redescontos capped at 100,000 contos; federal intervention March 1921; Nov 1921 short-term credits of £5.5m (Brazilian Warrant Co. and Schroeder); May 1922 consolidation loan of £9m from N.M. Rothschild, Baring Brothers and J. Henry Schroder (same MPRA source).
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** Financiers "large profits", planters "some net profit", Sao Paulo State a net loss (FRUS 1913 d37, above). Theodor Wille & Co. bought at 7$000 per arroba and took 200 reis per bag commission; in 1906/7 Wille bought 7m bags at Santos and 1.2m at Rio (Mendonca, rbgdr source above). A seven-member committee included Sielcken of Crossman & Sielcken (FRUS, above).
- **Price/terms vs value:** Sao Paulo held over 8m bags abroad by 1908 and could not pay storage (Mendonca). 1922 terms were harsher than 1908; a creditor-dominated committee controlled stock sales; Brazil agreed to no new valorization while the loan ran, and Brazilian Warrant Co. got exclusive rights over valorization operations (MPRA, above). No independent valuation of the stock vs the loan terms was found.
- **Legal mechanism:** State convention then federal guarantee (Law 1.127/1908); emission decrees 1917 and 1920; Banco do Brasil rediscount window.
- **Who bore the costs:** Foreign consumers: US consumers paid about $57m a year for a 6-cent rise, outstanding debt about $64m on 1 Jan 1911 (FRUS, above); domestic taxpayers through the surtax, the federal guarantee and inflation from the emissions.
- **What happened after:** Debt of Sao Paulo assumed federally in 1930 and Coffee Institute 1924 (both cited to Furtado at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taubat%C3%A9_Agreement, no numbers there).
- **Counter-view:** Mendonca notes other states refused the surtax and Rio state's coffee was hurt, yet Furtado held the operation postponed a structural imbalance (rbgdr source). The US report also records the scheme ended with the loan repaid and price higher than before 1906 [interpretation of FRUS; no net-profit total found].
- **Sources:** as linked above.

## Coffee burning and destruction, 1931-1944

- **Years:** 1931-1944.
- **Government/ruler:** Vargas; Conselho Nacional do Cafe (1931), Departamento Nacional do Cafe by Decreto 22.452 of 10 Feb 1933 (https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/1930-1939/decreto-22452-10-fevereiro-1933-514936-publicacaooriginal-1-pe.html, snippet only [UNVERIFIED]); mandate extended to 30 Jun 1944.
- **What was transferred and how big:** Government bought surplus coffee from planters and destroyed it. Totals disagree: 78.2m bags (https://dailycoffeenews.com/2021/09/22/90-years-ago-seeking-salvation-brazil-burned-billions-of-pounds-of-coffee), about 72m (Santos municipal education PDF, https://www.santos.sp.gov.br/static/files_www/conteudo/SEDUC/EducaSatos/9deg_ano_estudantes_2a_edicao_1.pdf, snippet only [UNVERIFIED]), 80m (https://iepecdg.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/20251002CafeCindes.docx-1.pdf, snippet only [UNVERIFIED]). Range 72-80m bags. 1937 peak burned 70% of the annual stock (Daily Coffee News).
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** Planters and the coffee trade received purchase payments; financed by raised customs duties plus foreign loans (Daily Coffee News). Coffee was over 50% of export value around 1929 and a bag fell from 200,000 reis to about 20,000 in 1929 (same).
- **Price/terms vs value:** Not found (no purchase price per bag or total outlay on an opened page).
- **Legal mechanism:** Decrees creating CNC/DNC; the funding tax on exported coffee [details UNVERIFIED].
- **Who bore the costs:** Taxpayers/consumers via duties and loans; non-coffee sectors.
- **What happened after:** Programme ended 1944; Furtado's well-known argument that it sustained demand (an income-maintenance effect) is cited generally, not checked here [UNVERIFIED].
- **Counter-view:** Defenders see it as Keynesian-style demand support before Keynes; no opened source quantified it.
- **Sources:** as linked.

## Banco de Credito da Borracha and the Washington Accords (1942-1946)

- **Years:** 1942-1946 (bank life: 20-year term).
- **Government/ruler:** Vargas; US side Rubber Reserve Company / Rubber Development Corporation.
- **What was transferred and how big:** Decreto-lei 4.451 of 9 Jul 1942 created the bank with capital of 50,000 contos; at least 55% Treasury, 40% Rubber Reserve Company, remainder Brazilians only; exclusive right of final purchase and sale of all rubber (Art. 8). Source: https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/declei/1940-1949/decreto-lei-4451-9-julho-1942-414643-publicacaooriginal-1-pe.html. Capital raised by Decreto-lei 5.651 of 5 Jul 1943 to Cr$150m: 87,500 shares Treasury, 60,000 RDC, 2,500 private Brazilians; the value fund equalled capital within 3 years, helped by US premiums (Martinello, https://www.ufac.br/editora/publicacoes/a-batalha-da-borracha-na-segunda-guerra-mundial/AbatalhadaborrachanaSegundaGuerraMundial.pdf). About 55,000 recruited (Martinello) or about 60,000 (https://portalamazonia.com/amazonia/soldados-da-borracha-60-mil/).
- **Who got it (and who financed them):** The US purchased rubber for the war; the RRC contract ran 3 Mar 1942 to 31 Dec 1946: fixed 39 cents per pound for Upriver Acre Fine f.o.b. Belem, +2.5 cents premium on 5,000-10,000 tons a year, $5,000,000 RRC fund, financing via a Reconstruction Finance Corporation letter of credit (https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1942v05/d654). Rubber barons/aviadores in the Amazon lost the retail monopoly to the bank (Martinello).
- **Price/terms vs value:** Fixed price set by the US; no independent estimate of whether 39 cents was below market was found.
- **Legal mechanism:** Washington Accords 3 Mar 1942 + Decreto-lei 4.451/1942; SEMTA (recruitment).
- **Who bore the costs:** The migrants. Projections of 100,000 tons a year were unrealistic (Martinello). "Half never returned" is an estimate of about 30,000 deaths (Portal Amazonia); real count not documented. Of those returning in first half of 1946, 248 were judged unfit, mostly malaria and avitaminosis (Martinello). No contract guarantees of return transport were honoured in full [claim not verified].
- **What happened after:** Law 13.831/2019 put them in the Livro dos Herois; ADCT art. 54 (1988) grants a pension of 2 minimum wages; EC 78/2014 added R$25,000; about 6,500 pensioners remained in Nov 2025 (Radio Senado, search result [UNVERIFIED]).
- **Counter-view:** The bank is described as financially successful (value fund equalled capital in 3 years, Martinello); the death figure is an estimate.
- **Sources:** as linked.

## Light (1978-79 buy-back) - additions

- **Years:** Dec 1978 decision, closed 12 Jan 1979.
- **Government/ruler:** Geisel; Eletrobras (president Antonio Carlos Magalhaes).
- **What was transferred and how big:** Brascan's 83% stake in Light. Passed to the Rio de Janeiro state government and privatized 21 May 1996 (Carazza, Valor 10 Feb 2020, https://www.gesel.ie.ufrj.br/app/webroot/files/IFES/BV/carazza1.pdf).
- **Price/terms vs value:** Disputed. US$380m, US$460m below book value (so book about US$840m, derived): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brascan citing Globe and Mail 29 Dec 1978 and 12 Jan 1979; also US$380m in https://repositorio.upf.br/server/api/core/bitstreams/e4538f1a-5e44-4135-af7f-b7e7aa6cc55c/content; US$382m (https://braziljournal.com/breaking-ronaldo-cezar-coelho-dobra-aposta-e-chega-a-15-da-light/). Carazza gives US$1.14bn for 83% of capital (same Valor piece); basis unexplained, may include debt [UNVERIFIED]. Range: US$380-382m vs US$1.14bn.
- **Legal mechanism:** Share purchase by Eletrobras. A private-market alternative backed by ministers Simonsen and Ueki was blocked by Magalhaes (Carazza).
- **Who bore the costs:** Treasury/Eletrobras. Criticism (Leite 1997 p. 244, in the UPF thesis) is that the concession expired in 1990 with assets reverting free to the Union, so the state paid for what it would have received.
- **Counter-view:** Wikipedia's Brascan page says Brascan was "forced by the dictatorship"; a purchase below book value argues against overpayment.
- **Sources:** as linked; BNDES timeline https://web.bndes.gov.br/bib/jspui/bitstream/1408/920/1/O%20setor%20el%c3%a9trico%20brasileiro%20e%20o%20BNDES_reflex%c3%b5es_P-final.pdf.

## Amforp (1964 buy-out) - additions

- **Years:** Offer 1961; Apr 1963 agreement; contract Nov 1964.
- **Government/ruler:** Goulart (negotiation interrupted), then Castello Branco.
- **What was transferred and how big:** Amforp/Bond & Share assets and credits in Brazil (including ten subsidiaries per Eletrobras) to Eletrobras under Law 4.428 of 14 Oct 1964 (https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/546278/publicacao/15715156, search result [UNVERIFIED]).
- **Price/terms vs value:** Per Lauren dos Reis Bastos thesis (quoting Saes & Sasse 2012 p. 141): US$135m for the assets, plus US$10m compensation for delay and US$7.7m for interest and dividends, paid as a loan over 45 years at 6.5% a year; the April 1963 agreement already stood at US$135m, calculated on historical cost under the water code and considered fair for all Amforp assets (signed by Roberto Campos, then ambassador, approved by San Tiago Dantas). Source: https://repositorio.upf.br/server/api/core/bitstreams/... (thesis on CEERG/Amforp; local copy bastos.txt, URL path not re-verified [UNVERIFIED]). The pt.wikipedia figure of US$470m was not corroborated.
- **Legal mechanism:** Law 4.428/1964; Treasury guarantee; Eximbank loans (search result [UNVERIFIED]).
- **Who bore the costs:** Treasury/Eletrobras borrowers; the thesis says the purchase "limpar a barra" with the US after the 1963 delays.
- **Counter-view and Brizola:** Brizola, as federal deputy, on 30 May 1963 called the purchase a "crime de lesa-patria" and blamed San Tiago Dantas (Bastos thesis). His 1959 encampacao of CEERG used a symbolic Cr$1 offer; the company claimed no less than Cr$2.1bn; investment figures cited: gross Cr$1,630,457,000 and net Cr$1,362,623,000 in 1959, historical investment Cr$3,098,143,000 corrected to 1963 (same thesis). The CEERG case never got a first-instance judgment; the thesis infers the indemnity was settled within the 1964 deal. Lacerda's position: not found.
- **Sources:** thesis above; Eletrobras note on Nov 1964 negotiations (search result [UNVERIFIED]).

## Brazil Railway, Sao Paulo-Rio Grande and Madeira-Mamore - additions

- **Years:** 1890-1917, Madeira-Mamore handed to the Union 1931.
- **What was transferred:** Sao Paulo-Rio Grande: original Teixeira Soares concession carried 6% gold interest on 37,000:000$000 of capital for 30 years and 30 km of land each side for 50 years; Decreto 305 of 7 Apr 1890 set capital at 30 contos per km and cut the land zone to 15 km each side. Port of Para: 6% guarantee on construction capital plus a 2% gold tax on port imports; company created 7 Sept 1906, authorized US$32m of shares, US$23.5m issued, a £3.6m 50-year 5% bond and 62.5m francs of 60-year 5% bonds. Source for all: Correa thesis, https://memoriaferroviaria.assis.unesp.br/colecoes/wp-content/uploads/tainacan-items/9388/38405/correa_lm_dr_assis.pdf (citing Gremaud 1992).
- **Madeira-Mamore:** Port of Para Co. held 50% of the shares in 1907 (same thesis). The Treaty of Petropolis (1903) traded Acre for building the line within four years plus £2m, in two installments (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Petr%C3%B3polis). Catramby contract by Decreto 6.106 of 7 Aug 1906; Decreto 6.755 of 28 Nov 1907; Decreto 6.838 of 30 Jan 1908 (https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrada_de_Ferro_Madeira-Mamor%C3%A9; this page has internal date inconsistencies). About 6,000 deaths among about 30,000 workers in 1907-12 (same page). The Brazil Railway handed the line to the federal government in 1931; price not found. Deactivation Decreto 58.501 of 25 May 1966, last train 1972.
- **Financing/profit:** An income statement in the thesis lists a £130,000 profit on issuing £1.6m of Madeira-Mamore 5% bonds and £3,000 guarantee commission; dating unclear [UNVERIFIED]. Founders projected about US$70m profit in 10 years (same). Brazil Railway went bankrupt 1913/14 with a forced agreement in 1917 [UNVERIFIED, search summary].
- **Madeira-Mamore guarantee terms:** not found on an opened page.
- **Counter-view:** the railway was the price of Acre, a state obligation, not purely a gift to a private group.

## Docas de Santos (1888-1980) - additions

- **Years:** 1888-1980.
- **What was transferred:** Port concession to Gaffree and Guinle after rival bidders withdrew; initial partnership capital 4,000 contos for 4 years; tax exemption; the 1869 ports law (Decreto 1.746) allowed rate cuts only if net profit exceeded 12%, charges up to 2% on import value and 1% on exports. Source: https://www.pucsp.br/sites/default/files/download/cehal/download/INf-Publico-Pesqusa-Docas-Codesp.pdf.
- **Term:** Decreto 9.979 of 12 Jul 1888 gave 39 years, later extended up to the 90-year maximum; Codesp created 1 Oct 1980; concession ended 7 Nov 1980 (https://mlawreview.emnuvens.com.br/mlaw/article/download/54/139). Some sources say 90 or 92 years [UNVERIFIED].
- **Not found:** profit and dividend totals, tariff history, buy-out price. A claim of a 50% tariff rise when dividends reached 8% is undated [UNVERIFIED].
- **Note:** the state invested heavily in the port itself (PUC-SP above).

## Companhia de Terras Norte do Parana (CTNP) - additions

- **Years:** 1925 onward.
- **What:** Organized 24 Sept 1925 by Arthur Thomas and Antonio Moraes Barros; Parana Plantations Ltd of London; 1925 purchases of tracts of 350,000 and 100,000 alqueires; plan to colonize 515,000 alqueires between the Paranapanema, Ivai and Tibagi rivers. All from search summaries only [UNVERIFIED].
- **Not found:** purchase price per alqueire, title disputes, costs to the Kaingang.

## Patterns

1. Private gain came by instrument, not by sale: money-creation (Encilhamento), guarantees (coffee, railways), exclusive purchase rights (rubber), and land zones.
2. Foreign finance usually set terms: Rothschild, Schroder, Baring in coffee; RRC in rubber; Brazil Railway syndicates.
3. Costs moved to the public: surtaxes, inflation, and in 1964 and 1979 the state buying back what it might have received free at reversion.
4. Valuation is the weakest point: Light 380m vs 1.14bn, CEERG Cr$1 vs Cr$2.1bn, and coffee burning 72-80m bags show how little is settled.
5. The poorest paid in lives: Madeira-Mamore workers and rubber soldiers; counts are estimates.
