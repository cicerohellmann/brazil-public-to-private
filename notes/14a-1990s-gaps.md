# 14a. 1985-2002 federal gaps (additions to note 04)

Scope: cases not covered, or only partly covered, in `04-privatization-1985-2002.md`. Entries titled "additions" only add facts to a case already in note 04.
Rule used: every number has a URL next to it. Anything not confirmed from an opened page is marked [UNVERIFIED]. Where sources disagree, both are given.

## Petrochemical privatizations — additions

- **Years:** 1992-96 for the pole-by-pole sales; Copene control moved in 2001-02.
- **Government/ruler:** Collor, Itamar Franco, Cardoso (BNDES as manager).
- **What was transferred, how big:** 27 companies, US$3.7 bn including about US$1 bn of debt transferred, 1992-96 (BNDES 50 anos, https://web.bndes.gov.br/bib/jspui/bitstream/1408/14477/1/BNDES%2050%20anos%20-%20Hist%C3%B3rias%20Setoriais_O%20setor%20petroqu%C3%ADmico_P_BD.pdf). Sales were individual, pole by pole (same source).
- **Who got it (and who financed):** Copesul sold 15 May 1992 to a group including Ipiranga 17.81%, OPP 9.91%, OPP Polietilenos 9.91%; Petroquisa kept 14.99% (IBRAC/CADE, https://revista.ibrac.org.br/revista/article/download/1083/960/2082). Petroquisa kept 17.48% of PQU and 15% of Copesul (BNDES 50 anos, link above). Copene: the private group already held control; Petroquisa stayed with a 15.4% voting minority (BNDES 50 anos). A 1995 auction of Petroquisa shares left Norquisa with full control of Copene (GEM, https://www.gem.wiki/Braskem_Camacari_Petrochemical_Complex). Odebrecht and Mariani bought control in July 2001 at an auction of government-held shares; Braskem was approved 16 Aug 2002 and traded from 2 Sep 2002 (BNDES 50 anos). Buyer financing by BNDES for these deals: not found [UNVERIFIED].
- **Price/terms vs value:** Aggregate US$3.7 bn (above) is the only figure from an opened page. Before the Dec 2000 auction, press expected US$1 bn to 1.5 bn; BNDES had coordinated the process for over three years; Odebrecht was asked to step out and an Ultra-BNDES SPE was left unsigned (Istoé Dinheiro, https://istoedinheiro.com.br/o-tabuleiro-na-bahia). Encyclopedia.com says Odebrecht bought Conepar for BRL 785 mn (https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/odebrecht-sa); minimum price and number of bids [UNVERIFIED]. No independent valuation (who estimated) was found [UNVERIFIED].
- **Legal mechanism:** PND auctions under Lei 8.031/1990 and, from 1997, Lei 9.491/1997; Petroquisa (Petrobras subsidiary) kept minority stakes and later sold them.
- **Who bore the costs:** Not quantified in any opened source [UNVERIFIED]. Petroflex buyers using BNDES credit lost about 27% in real terms, which is a buyer loss, not a public one (IPEA, https://repositorio.ipea.gov.br/bitstream/11058/14177/1/avaliacao_e_agenda_do_programa_nacional_de_desestatizacao.pdf).
- **What happened after:** Braskem became the Odebrecht-led consolidator (BNDES 50 anos; GEM link above).
- **Counter-view:** BNDES presents the sector sales as restructuring that kept Petroquisa as a minority partner (BNDES 50 anos).
- **Premise check:** Searches did not support a "Copene sold in 1996" event; only the 1995 Petroquisa share auction and the 2001 control sale appear in opened pages.

## Ports (Lei 8.630/1993) and Tecon 1 — additions

- **Years:** Law 1993; Tecon 1 Santos concession Nov 1997; Salvador 1999.
- **Government/ruler:** Itamar Franco (law); Cardoso (concessions).
- **What was transferred, how big:** Terminal operation moved to private lessees under arrendamento, with OGMO managing labour, Companhias Docas remaining as port authorities (Lei 8.630, https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/550506; ANPET, https://transportes.anpet.org.br/anpet/article/download/480/366/1723). Tecon Santos: R$274.5 mn, 25+25 years (Santos Brasil, https://www.mediagroup.com.br/HOST/Santos-Brasil/2011/eng/ra/11.htm). Salvador port minimum price US$21 mn, 1999 (BNDES history, https://www.bndes.gov.br/wps/portal/site/home/transparencia/desestatizacao/processos-encerrados/Historico).
- **Who got it (and who financed):** Santos Brasil (Tecon 1 winner). Bidder composition and financing [UNVERIFIED]. Claims of a US$250 mn investment commitment and US$92 mn starting bid come from search snippets only [UNVERIFIED].
- **Price/terms vs value:** No independent valuation found [UNVERIFIED].
- **Legal mechanism:** Lei 8.630/1993 (revoked by Lei 12.815/2013).
- **Who bore the costs:** Not documented in opened sources [UNVERIFIED].
- **What happened after:** The 1993 regime was replaced in 2013 (Lei 12.815).
- **Counter-view:** Not found in opened pages.

## Sarney-era privatizations — additions

- **Years:** 1985-1990.
- **Government/ruler:** Sarney.
- **What was transferred, how big:** Under a Nov 1985 decree (Decreto 91.991), BNDESPar reprivatized firms it had taken over: 13 companies from June 1987 to Dec 1989, about US$556 mn raised and almost US$500 mn of liabilities (mostly BNDES credits) transferred (BNDES, https://web.bndes.gov.br/bib/jspui/bitstream/1408/10058/1/Privatization%20BNDESpar%20experience_1987-1989%20.PDF). BNDES counts 38 companies and about US$780 mn for the 1980s (BNDES history, link above); the 13 vs 38 gap is scope (BNDESPar 1987-89 vs the decade), list not obtained [UNVERIFIED]. Cofavi and Usiba (Siderbras) were among the sales (BNDESPar paper).
- **Who got it:** Mafersa: Oct 1989 auction suspended by Sarney; sold 11 Nov 1991 to Refer at Cr$143.50 per share vs minimum Cr$54.95 (161% above); Refer sold 65% for R$1,000 in 1995; face value US$48.4 mn (Capital Aberto, https://capitalaberto.com.br/empresas/2010/11/privatizada-e-falida). Financing [UNVERIFIED].
- **Broadcasting:** April 1988 Radiobrás decree; 23 of 37 stations transferred or closed; 1,028 radio and TV licences granted in the period (Agencia Brasil, https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/radioagencia-nacional/radio-nacional-90-anos-em-90-historias/apos-expansao-maior-parte-da-nacional-e-privatizada-no-governo-sarney). Decreto 95.886 (March 1988) [UNVERIFIED].
- **Price/terms vs value:** Mafersa above. No overall valuation [UNVERIFIED].
- **Who bore the costs:** Liabilities transferred were mostly BNDES credits (BNDESPar paper), so the bank held the exposure.
- **What happened after:** Mafersa ended in bankruptcy (Capital Aberto title "Privatizada e falida").
- **Counter-view:** BNDES framing is of orderly divestiture of firms acquired through rescue (BNDESPar paper).

## BNDES financing of privatization buyers

- **Years:** 1991-2003.
- **Government/ruler:** Collor to Cardoso; audit in Lula's first year.
- **What was transferred, how big:** A single total for all sectors was not found in any opened page [UNVERIFIED]. Sector figures: in electricity, distributors yielded R$27 bn but consumed R$22 bn of BNDES money; 14 of 22 firms got R$7 bn (TCU audit as reported, https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/3763/auditoria-revelou-prejuizos; https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/4343/gestao-tucana-do-bndes-sob-suspeita). 20 distributors and 4 generators were sold for US$30 bn in 1995-2003 (same Congresso em Foco pages).
- **Early phase:** BNDES sold securitized credits on 12 years at full monetary correction plus 6% a year (IPEA, link in the petrochemicals entry). Siderbras sales: cash about 3% (Passanezzi Filho 1992 as cited by Garcia, https://www.eco.unicamp.br/images/arquivos/artigos/LEP/L11/LEP11_11_carlos.pdf); Usiminas US$1.49 bn with US$39 mn cash (Garcia).
- **Rules and breaches:** 50%-of-minimum-price financing rule and 30%-of-total-assets exposure limit; the audit reports both were exceeded (Congresso em Foco, links above).
- **Who got it:** AES owed US$1.2 bn; the 2003 deal gave BNDES 50% minus one share of Brasiliana and cut the debt to US$510 mn (Congresso em Foco). 8 of 12 financed distributors had not settled debts (same).
- **Price/terms vs value:** Who estimated: TCU and BNDES itself, as reported by Congresso em Foco.
- **Who bore the costs:** BNDES net worth fell from R$13 bn to R$10 bn between 2002 and mid-2003 (Congresso em Foco; cause not isolated to privatization loans) [partly UNVERIFIED]. Taxpayers via the Treasury funding the bank.
- **What happened after:** A R$3 bn capitalization line found no taker (Congresso em Foco). TCU journal discussion: https://revista.tcu.gov.br/ojs/index.php/RTCU/article/download/569/631/1163.
- **Counter-view:** Defenders argue the credit was the only way to make buyers appear in a thin domestic market; no opened source states this explicitly [UNVERIFIED].

## PND proceeds vs debts transferred

- **Years:** 1990-2002.
- **Totals by period (FIPE, https://downloads.fipe.org.br/publicacoes/bif/bif476-42-44.pdf):**
  - Collor 1990-92: 68 companies included, 18 sold; US$4.0 bn plus US$1.3 bn debt transferred.
  - Itamar: 15 companies; US$4.6 bn plus US$1.9 bn debt.
  - Cardoso 1995-2002: more than 100 companies; US$78.9 bn plus US$14.9 bn debt. Federal US$51.0 bn (debt US$8.1 bn); state US$27.9 bn (debt US$6.8 bn).
- **Cross-checks:** BNDES history: 2000 total R$19.9 bn (federal R$14.4 bn or US$7.7 bn; state R$5.54 bn or US$3.04 bn); debt transferred R$517 mn or US$289 mn in 2000; 1990-92 US$4 bn; 1993-94 US$4.5 bn; 1995-96 US$5.1 bn (https://www.bndes.gov.br/wps/portal/site/home/transparencia/desestatizacao/processos-encerrados/Historico). Wikipedia lists R$78.6 bn for the Cardoso years (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatizations_under_the_Fernando_Henrique_Cardoso_presidency); currency mismatch with FIPE US$78.9 bn is unresolved, treat as range R$78.6 bn / US$78.9 bn, not equivalent.
- **Currency of payment (to mid-1993, IPEA):** expected about US$17 bn; raised US$5.36 bn at face value. Average ágio 13.7% (23.1% in 1992, 14.1% in 1991, 0 in 1993). Paid in: Certificados de Privatização 24.2%; securitized Union debt 29.6% (83.4% of that overdue Siderbras debts); Siderbras debentures 17.5%; FND 11.5%; TDA 8.3%; CEF letters 5.3%. About US$46 bn of securitized debts outstanding mid-1993 (IPEA, https://repositorio.ipea.gov.br/bitstream/11058/14177/1/avaliacao_e_agenda_do_programa_nacional_de_desestatizacao.pdf). IPEA economic tally: 5,357.5 + 3,613.6 + 172.1 + 680.5 = 9,823.7 US$ mn (labels of the middle terms not confirmed) [UNVERIFIED].
- **Steel sub-ledger:** Siderbras sales US$6.33 bn against US$7 bn expected (Garcia, link above); BNDES (2000) puts steel at US$5.56 bn (cited by Garcia).
- **Who bore the costs:** Net fiscal effect depends on the value of securities accepted at face and on state debts; not netted by any opened source. Castelar estimates state distributors lost up to US$26 bn for the Treasury (Congresso em Foco, https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/4343/gestao-tucana-do-bndes-sob-suspeita).
- **Premise check:** A "US$85 bn PND total" was not found; FIPE gives the US$78.9 bn + US$14.9 bn debt above.
- **Counter-view:** Proceeds plus debt relief are presented officially as a gain; critics point to the paper used as currency and the BNDES credit (sections above).

## CC5 scheme and CPI do Banestado

- **Years:** 1996-2002 flows; investigation from 2003; CPMI installed 18 June 2003, ended 27 Dec 2004.
- **Government/ruler:** Cardoso (flows); Lula (CPMI).
- **What was transferred, how big:** Estimates differ widely.
  - Senate item: US$30 bn, 1996-2002, Operation Macuco (https://www2.senado.leg.br/jspui/handle/id/609990).
  - Federal Police delegate: US$124 bn through CC5 accounts in ten years (Tribuna PR, https://www.tribunapr.com.br/noticias/politica/cc5-movimentaram-us-124-bilhoes-em-dez-anos/) [single source, UNVERIFIED as to method].
  - CPMI report: evasion up to R$150 bn; 91 indictments proposed (Conjur, https://www.conjur.com.br/2004-dez-14/relatorio_cpi_banestado_aponta_evasao_150_bi/).
  - Tax assessments R$8.376 bn; figures of US$24 bn and over US$28 bn; US$8.2 mn seized (Gazeta do Povo, https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/vida-publica/autuacoes-fiscais-do-caso-banestado-chegam-a-r-83-bilhoes-eb0no3kch70qb6y36s9lydf0u).
  - Range for sums moved: US$24-30 bn (Senate, Gazeta) up to US$124 bn (PF delegate); the R$150 bn figure is "evasion" and not directly comparable. O Povo's R$ figures conflict with Gazeta's US$ figures (https://www.opovo.com.br/noticias/politica/2018/03/entenda-o-que-foi-o-caso-do-banestado-narrado-em-o-mecanismo.html).
- **Who got it:** 90+ "laranja" accounts in 1996-97 (O Povo); about 100 convicted (O Povo, [UNVERIFIED] count).
- **Legal mechanism:** CC5 non-resident accounts used for remittances abroad via Foz do Iguacu banks; the mechanism text (Carta-Circular 5) was not opened [UNVERIFIED]. A 1996 central bank decision for Foz do Iguacu banks led to an indictment proposal against the then president of the central bank, Gustavo Franco (Tribuna PR, https://www.tribunapr.com.br/noticias/brasil/final-melancolico-para-a-cpi-do-banestado/amp/).
- **Who bore the costs:** Treasury (evasion), plus the state bank Banestado, privatized to Itau in 2000 [Itau sale terms UNVERIFIED].
- **What happened after:** Two competing CPMI reports; it ended unvoted on 27 Dec 2004 (Tribuna PR above; Conjur, https://www.conjur.com.br/2004-dez-28/cpi_banestado_terminou_pizza_presidente_oab/).
- **Counter-view:** Franco's indictment was a political outcome of one report; the other report did not follow it (Tribuna PR). Search summaries did not support a Janot link to Banestado.

## Embraer — additions

- **Years:** 1994 sale; follow-up 2000-01 and 2010-16.
- **Government/ruler:** Itamar Franco (sale), Cardoso (restructuring).
- **What was transferred, how big:** 55.4% of capital sold for US$171 mn (Flightglobal, https://flightglobal.com/embraer-forced-to-impose-job-cuts/12529.article). Other reports: US$182.4 mn (Aviation Week, https://aviationweek.com/embraer-privatized) and R$154 mn (Capital Aberto, https://capitalaberto.com.br/empresas/2008/05/sem-amarras). Range US$171-182.4 mn; the R$ figure is in another currency and not reconciled.
- **Who got it (and who financed):** US$130 mn of the price was paid in moedas (privatization securities) (DIEESE, https://www.dieese.org.br/notatecnica/2018/notaTec198Embraer.pdf).
- **After:** 1,700 of 5,550 jobs cut in 1995 (Flightglobal). NYSE listing raised US$544 mn in 2000-01; free float rose from 18.6% to 59.0% (DIEESE). BNDES financing R$8.2 bn in 2010-16 (DIEESE).
- **Who bore the costs:** Workers (job cuts); the buyers' use of discounted public paper (DIEESE).
- **Counter-view:** Embraer later became a global exporter; this is the usual defence of the sale and is not sourced from an opened page here [UNVERIFIED].

## Seguro-apagao (Encargo de Capacidade Emergencial, CBEE)

- **Years:** charge from Feb 2002; injunction Nov 2002; ended Dec 2005 or Feb 2006 (sources differ below).
- **Government/ruler:** Cardoso, then Lula.
- **What was transferred, how big:** A surcharge on consumers to pay for emergency thermal capacity contracted by the state company CBEE. Rate: R$0.0057 per kWh in 2002 with an exemption under 80 kWh (Tribuna PR, https://www.tribunapr.com.br/noticias/justica-federal-suspende-cobranca-do-seguro-apagao/); R$0.0067 per kWh in Feb 2005 (CanalEnergia, https://acendebrasil.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/2005_02_15_CanalEnergia.pdf). A search summary cited R$0.0035 and annual revenue about R$1 bn, and a suspension in Dec 2005 [UNVERIFIED; not opened]. Cumulative total collected: not found.
- **Who got it:** CBEE and the contracted plants. CBEE collected about R$300 mn in fines (CanalEnergia).
- **Price/terms vs value:** Cost of contracted capacity vs use not found [UNVERIFIED].
- **Legal mechanism:** Emergency program via MP and Lei 10.438/2002; collected through distributors; 17a Vara Civel Federal injunction, 20 Nov 2002 (Tribuna PR). PIS/Cofins rose from 3.65% to 9.25% and the charge was cut 22% (CanalEnergia).
- **Who bore the costs:** All grid consumers except low income [UNVERIFIED, search summary only].
- **What happened after:** Charge ended early after CBEE was capitalised [UNVERIFIED].
- **Counter-view:** Defenders held the plants were insurance against another shortfall; no opened source states this [UNVERIFIED].

## Recomposição tarifária extraordinária — additions

- **Years:** 2001-2003.
- **Government/ruler:** Cardoso (MP 14/2001), Lula (settlement).
- **What was transferred, how big:** Law 10.438/2002 provided a one-time extraordinary tariff recomposition for 2001 rationing losses, applied for 12 months and then deducted from the next annual adjustment (Planalto, https://planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/2002/l10438.htm). Caps of 2.9% (residential, rural, public lighting) and 7.9% (others) came from the Camara text of MP 14 [UNVERIFIED; cited by search summary, https://www.camara.leg.br/internet/plenario/result/redfin/MPV%200014-01%20%20Disp%C3%B5e%20sobre%20a%20expans%C3%A3o%20da%20oferta%20de%20energia%20el%C3%A9trica%20emergencial....htm]. The law authorized public credit institutions to finance the shortfall; BNDES put about R$7 bn into RTE financing and R$8 bn into plants (TCU/BNDES reporting, https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/3763/auditoria-revelou-prejuizos).
- **Who got it:** Distributors and generators. Concessionaires' apagao loss is estimated at R$8 bn by Pinguelli Rosa (Congresso em Foco).
- **Price/terms vs value:** Loss estimate above is one author's; no official total in an opened page [UNVERIFIED].
- **Who bore the costs:** Consumers through the tariff and BNDES via loans (law and audit above).
- **What happened after:** 8 of 12 financed distributors had not settled debts (Congresso em Foco).
- **Counter-view:** Castelar and Lessa argue rationing, not privatization, was the problem (Congresso em Foco, https://www.congressoemfoco.com.br/noticia/4343/gestao-tucana-do-bndes-sob-suspeita).

## Patterns

1. Public paper as currency: buyers paid with securities at face value, so cash was a small share (Siderbras cash about 3%; Usiminas US$39 mn of US$1.49 bn).
2. Public bank as lender: BNDES financed buyers and later absorbed the defaults (AES, distributors), and exposure limits were exceeded per the audit.
3. Debts moved with the assets: transferred debt appears next to every PND total.
4. Fiscal numbers differ by source and currency (FIPE US$ vs Wikipedia R$; US$24-124 bn for CC5); always quote ranges.
5. Costs landed on consumers (tariff surcharges), workers (Embraer cuts) and the bank, while gains are rarely tallied in the opened sources.
6. Several key totals are still missing: total buyer financing, Acominas price, Escelsa financing, cumulative seguro-apagao.
